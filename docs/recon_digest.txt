DIMENSION: wrong-turns
NARRATIVE:
The intellectual history of MLC is a history of correct instincts repeatedly ambushed by the satellite/neutral regime. The conjecture was born in the Orsay notes (1984/85) already yoked to its payoff — Douady and Hubbard proved MLC implies density of hyperbolicity for quadratics while admitting "we do not know how to prove either" — and the founding generation expected it to fall quickly. That expectation is documented twice over: contemporaries recall Douady believing the solution was around the corner, and McMullen wrote in the Bulletin of the AMS in 1994, after Yoccoz's breakthrough and Swiatek's and Lyubich's announcements, that MLC "is not too far out of reach." Even the set's connectivity had a false start: Mandelbrot's 1980 pictures convinced him the set was disconnected dust until Douady-Hubbard proved otherwise — an early lesson that numerics mislead on exactly this kind of question, and part of why no serious numerical case against MLC has ever existed.

The deepest wrong guess was structural: that local connectivity in the dynamical plane would be the engine ("plough in the dynamical plane, harvest in the parameter plane"). In fact the dynamical analogue of MLC is false. Cremer-point Julia sets are never locally connected (Sullivan/Douady), Herman built non-LC Siegel examples, and Douady and Hubbard themselves constructed infinitely renormalizable quadratics with non-LC Julia sets (written up by Milnor in 1992) — precisely by stacking satellite tunings toward the cardioid's cusp. Sorensen (2000) made such examples explicit, and Levin (CMP 2011) delivered the sharpest twist: for explicit rotation-number classes the Julia set is provably non-LC while M is provably locally connected at those very parameters. Each wrong guess here taught the same lesson: MLC is a parameter-space rigidity statement that survives the total failure of its dynamical counterpart.

The second great obstruction story concerns a priori bounds, the compactness engine of the Sullivan-McMullen-Lyubich renormalization program. Yoccoz's puzzle method (MLC at all finitely renormalizable parameters, ~1990) fails at renormalizable maps for a precise reason — the modulus sum degenerates to a convergent geometric series. Kahn and Lyubich then proved bounds for primitive combinatorics bounded away from the main molecule (2006-09), but the satellite regime resisted, and this was no accident of technique: Sorensen's examples and the Cheraghi-Shishikura analysis show a priori bounds genuinely fail for satellite combinatorics (degenerating geometry at small scales). The response — Inou-Shishikura near-parabolic renormalization, Levin's multiplier methods, pacman renormalization, and the Dudko-Lyubich neutral bounds — replaced the single-roadmap vision with a patchwork of regime-specific theories. Adjacent expectations fell in parallel: quadratic Julia sets can have positive area (Buff-Cheritat 2012, completing Douady's own late-career reversal of the zero-measure expectation, with examples in the same satellite regime); satellite baby copies of M are homeomorphic but not quasiconformally equivalent (Lomonaco-Petersen 2017), and magnified Feigenbaum copies are "hairy" (Milnor 1989, Lyubich 1999), killing naive self-similarity; Thurston's no-wandering-triangles theorem does not extend to cubics (Blokh-Oversteegen 2004), showing the combinatorial model of M is a degree-2 miracle. Even the Fatou conjecture's pedigree contains a wrong expectation: McMullen documents that Fatou's probable intent (non-hyperbolic maps confined to countably many subvarieties) is false by Rees's positive-measure theorem.

Two negative findings are notable. First, no withdrawn or debunked arXiv preprint claiming a full proof of MLC or of complex density of hyperbolicity could be located; the field's false starts live in expectations and stalled programs, not in flawed published proofs (the closest episode is the 1992-1997 gap between Swiatek's announcement and the refereed Graczyk-Swiatek/Lyubich proofs of the real case). Second, despite the non-LC Julia sets and the failure of bounds, no leading expert appears on record conjecturing MLC false; documented skepticism targets provability and cost (Avila warning students off the problem; careers damaged, per Quanta 2024), not truth. As of mid-2026 the wrong turns have been metabolized into a precise frontier: MLC holds for bounded-type combinatorics including Feigenbaum points (Dudko-Lyubich), along the real line and veins, and for parabolically bounded primitive renormalization (Kahn-Kapiamba-Lyubich, June 2026); Dudko's ICM 2026 address reduces what remains to unbounded satellite combinatorics and interpolation between positive- and zero-entropy regimes — exactly the territory where every earlier guess went wrong.

DIMENSION: yoccoz-limits
NARRATIVE:
The Yoccoz puzzle program, circa 1990, is the foundation of everything known about MLC. Its canonical written form is Hubbard's 1993 exposition "Three theorems of J.-C. Yoccoz" (Yoccoz never published his own proof; Hubbard worked from Yoccoz's lectures and manuscript notes, with input from Branner, Douady, Faught and Shishikura, while Milnor's 1992 Stony Brook lectures, arXiv:math/9207220, wrote up the "easy" dynamical half). The mechanism is fully explicit there: puzzle pieces cut by external rays landing at the alpha-fixed point and an equipotential; nested annuli between consecutive pieces organized by Branner-Hubbard tableaux; the Branner-Hubbard criterion (Prop. 6.1) that divergence of the series of annulus moduli forces pieces to shrink to points; and, in parameter space, a parapuzzle whose annuli M_A correspond to dynamical annuli A, with mod(M_A) controlled in terms of mod(A) by an extremal-length argument. The output: J_c is locally connected for every non-infinitely-renormalizable quadratic without indifferent cycles (Theorem II), and M is locally connected at every non-infinitely-renormalizable parameter (Theorem III), with indifferent cycles handled separately (Theorems I.C, 14.6). The companion Yoccoz inequality (Theorem I.B) confines log of the multiplier of a repelling fixed point with rotation number p/q to the disc of radius (log d)/(mq) tangent to the imaginary axis at 2 pi i p/q — equivalently Re(tau)/|tau − 2 pi i p/q|^2 ≥ mq/(2 log d) — with independent weaker versions by Pommerenke and Levin (hence "PLY inequality"). This yields limb diameter O(1/q) (sharp classical form: diam L_{p/q} ≤ (log 2)/(pi q)); Milnor's conjectured optimal rate is O(1/q^2), toward which Levin has the main refereed partial results and Kapiamba's preprint arXiv:2103.03211 gives the first provably optimal 1/q^2 two-sided bounds for infinite families of bounded continued-fraction length, via Lavaurs maps and near-parabolic renormalization.

Where the program stops is equally precise. Hubbard's Prop. 8.1/Cor. 8.2 show renormalizability is equivalent to periodicity of the critical tableau, in which case the critical nest converges and the critical end of the puzzle is homeomorphic to a whole small filled Julia set, not a point: the divergence criterion structurally requires a recurrent non-periodic tableau. Finitely renormalizable maps are rescued by re-running the puzzle on the last renormalization; infinitely renormalizable maps fail at every level, and local connectivity at the critical point becomes equivalent to a priori bounds — uniform lower bounds on moduli of the renormalization annuli — which genuinely fail for some satellite combinatorics (Sorensen/Milnor examples of non-locally-connected Julia sets at infinitely renormalizable parameters, following Douady-Hubbard).

The later strengthenings track this boundary exactly. Kahn-Lyubich's Quasi-Additivity Law and Covering Lemma (Annals 169 (2009)) provide the near-degenerate analytic machinery that puzzles alone lack, giving Yoccoz's theorem in every degree (Annals 170 (2009), with the parameter side by Avila-Kahn-Lyubich-Shen) and a priori bounds — hence MLC — for infinitely renormalizable combinatorics avoiding the molecule: bounded primitive ratios (Kahn 2006), decorations (Ann. Sci. ENS 2008), and the molecule condition (2009). The satellite/zero-entropy regime required different technology: Cheraghi-Shishikura's still-unpublished 2015 preprint (near-parabolic renormalization, high-type satellite combinatorics), Levin's bounds-free multiplier methods, and Dudko-Lyubich's pacman renormalization line, culminating in the first bounded-type satellite MLC examples (GAFA 2023) and "MLC at Feigenbaum points" (arXiv:2309.02107, to appear Publ. Math. IHES), which finally settled MLC at the classical period-doubling Feigenbaum parameter.

As of mid-2026 the front is moving fast: Dudko's December 2025 survey (arXiv:2512.24171) frames the remaining obstruction as the zero-entropy/neutral and unbounded-satellite regimes and conjectures hyperbolicity of a unifying "Molecule Renormalization"; Kahn-Lyubich's "IV. Elephant Eyes" (arXiv:2601.21905, Jan 2026) and Kahn-Kapiamba-Lyubich's "MLC for parabolically bounded primitive renormalization" (arXiv:2606.27272, June 2026) extend a priori bounds to primitive combinatorics degenerating toward the cusp. The consensus sharp boundary: puzzles + near-degenerate moduli analysis own positive core-entropy; satellite, neutral, and near-molecule scales are the live frontier, and MLC in full remains open.

NARRATIVE:
The renormalization frontier of MLC in mid-2026 is best organized by combinatorial type of infinite renormalization. The classical base camp is Kahn-Lyubich (2006-2009): a priori bounds, hence rigidity and MLC, for bounded primitive type and its 'decorations'/molecule-condition extensions — refereed except for Kahn's Part I, which famously remains a Stony Brook IMS preprint. The satellite regime was first breached from two independent directions: Cheraghi-Shishikura's 2015 manuscript (arXiv:1509.07843), which used the Inou-Shishikura near-parabolic invariant class to prove MLC at some infinitely satellite-renormalizable parameters of sufficiently high type and which — like the foundational 2008 Inou-Shishikura preprint itself — still appears to be unpublished; and Dudko-Lyubich's pacman-renormalization paper (arXiv:1808.10425), which IS the GAFA 2023 paper 'Local connectivity of the Mandelbrot set at some satellite parameters of bounded type' (the research-front prompt misattributes that GAFA paper to Cheraghi-Shishikura). Dudko-Lyubich then completed the entire bounded-type case in 'MLC at Feigenbaum points' (arXiv:2309.02107, v3 Dec 2025), settling MLC at the classical period-doubling Feigenbaum point and Goldberg-Khanin-Sinai universality for tripling; Dudko's Stony Brook page reports it accepted at Publications Mathematiques de l'IHES, though no journal-ref appears on arXiv yet.

The new June 2026 event is Kahn-Kapiamba-Lyubich, 'MLC for parabolically bounded primitive renormalization' (arXiv:2606.27272, submitted June 25, 2026, 73 pp). 'Parabolically bounded' families consist of finitely many combinatorial types plus finitely many 'parabolic tails' generated by iterated limb-insertion at parabolic cusps of the main cardioid: topology of the renormalization combinatorics is allowed to grow without bound, but only in the controlled way that occurs when primitive copies sink toward a cusp. Theorem A gives beau a priori bounds for every parabolically bounded family of prime primitive types; Theorem C gives rigidity, i.e., MLC at all such parameters — reportedly the first MLC class violating the secondary-limbs condition that bounded all previous results. The proof machinery — Thin-Thick Decomposition of degenerating Riemann surfaces, a Value Calculus on weighted arc diagrams contracted uniformly by dynamical pullback, a Wanderers Theorem refining the Kahn-Lyubich Covering Lemma, and a Wave Lemma for propagating laminations of definite width — is engineered precisely to control degeneration of renormalizations with unbounded periods. This is an unrefereed preprint less than a month old; its own introduction flags the remaining cases as general primitive near-parabolic combinatorics, general neutral parameters, and 'virtually-satellite' types awaiting virtual-renormalization machinery.

On the neutral side, Dudko-Lyubich's 'Uniform a priori bounds for neutral renormalization' (arXiv:2210.09280, 2022, still a preprint) established non-perturbative pseudo-Siegel bounds for all neutral quadratics and the Mother Hedgehog — the first non-perturbative a priori bounds in the non-JLC category, going beyond the perturbative high-type regime of Inou-Shishikura and Cheraghi's program (whose refereed outputs, such as the arithmetic-geometric model in Nonlinearity 2023, remain confined to high type). The 2025 sequel Dudko-Luo-Lyubich 'Variation II: psi-bullet-ql Siegel maps' (arXiv:2509.23031) repackages these bounds compatibly with psi-quadratic-like renormalization theory for transfer to rational families — visible infrastructure-building toward the unbounded satellite case.

Dudko's ICM 2026 survey (arXiv:2512.24171, Dec 30, 2025) supplies the authoritative roadmap: what remains for a priori bounds for ALL quadratic polynomials is exactly two problems — completing the remaining unbounded satellite cases, and developing interpolation techniques between definite-positive-entropy and zero-entropy settings (via 'virtual Julia sets') — after which 'a clear path towards the MLC (rigidity)' opens. The structural conjectures behind full MLC-plus-universality remain open: hyperbolicity of neutral/cylinder renormalization for all rotation numbers (Conjecture 3.5) and the Molecule Conjecture (Conjecture 3.6, tracing to Dudko-Lyubich-Selinger JAMS 2020), with the survey's Question 3.1 asking for a single uniformly hyperbolic operator unifying the quadratic-like, neutral, and puzzle regimes over all of the boundary of M. On timing, expert consensus is deliberate optimism without dates: the Quanta feature (Jan 2024) records Lyubich's 'tremendously devious' assessment and his joke that MLC will arrive with volume 4 of his textbook; the announced SCGP workshop 'Half a Century of Dynamical Renormalization' (Feb 2027, organized by Dudko, de Faria, Khanin, Lyubich, Martens, with particular focus on MLC) signals a field consolidating for the final push rather than announcing one. A caveat for the orchestrator: fine-grained internal details quoted from arXiv HTML (theorem numbering in the survey, exact tool formulations in KKL) came through automated summarization and should be spot-checked against the PDFs before quotation in the dossier.

DIMENSION: alternative-avenues
NARRATIVE:
Outside the Kahn-Lyubich-Dudko-Kapiamba analytic program, the avenues toward MLC divide into three tiers: reformulations that are mathematically complete but inert (laminations, computability, fibers), machineries that are powerful in adjacent regimes but structurally blocked at the MLC frontier (iterated monodromy groups, rigorous numerics, transcendental methods), and avenues that are essentially empty (o-minimality, statistical physics). The computability tier is the most crisply developed: Hertling (MLQ 2005) proved that M's distance function is computable if density of hyperbolicity holds — so MLC, which implies it, would settle Penrose's question affirmatively in the bit model — while Blum-Shub-Smale undecidability handles only the algebraic model. The warning signs are real: Braverman-Yampolsky produced non-computable quadratic Julia sets, and Coronel-Rojas-Yampolsky (arXiv:1703.04668) produced a one-parameter cubic family whose Mandelbrot-like bifurcation locus is non-computable, showing that parameter-space computability is not automatic. No poly-time consequence of MLC is known; unconditional computability of M is still open, making M perhaps the only celebrated planar fractal whose computability status is genuinely conditional on a dynamical conjecture.

On the combinatorial side, Thurston's QML/pinched-disk model is a finished theory — the abstract Mandelbrot set is compact, connected, locally connected, effectively computable via Lavaurs' algorithm, and MLC is precisely the statement that M is homeomorphic to it (equivalently, Schleicher's fibers are all trivial; equivalently, combinatorial rigidity). Thurston's own late program (core entropy) was completed by Tiozzo (Inventiones 2016) and the posthumous 'Degree-d-invariant laminations' paper, but experts uniformly treat this as a description of the model, not an attack on rigidity: Dudko's ICM 2026 survey does not mention laminations at all. The same pattern holds for self-similar groups: Bartholdi-Nekrashevych's twisted-rabbit solution and Bartholdi-Dudko's decidability of Thurston equivalence (Inventiones 2021) show IMG/biset machinery is decisive for postcritically finite combinatorics, but the limit-space dictionary requires expansion, and the MLC-critical parameters (infinitely renormalizable, Cremer/Siegel) are exactly non-expanding — no published work extends IMG methods to non-PCF rigidity.

Computer assistance is closer to the action than commonly appreciated: beyond the Lanford (1982) and Eckmann-Wittwer (1987) Feigenbaum proofs, Gaidashev-Yampolsky proved golden-mean Siegel renormalization hyperbolicity computer-assistedly, Lanford-Yampolsky established computer-assisted a priori bounds for parabolic renormalization (acknowledged in Dudko's ICM survey), and Dudko-Sutherland proved dim_H(Feigenbaum Julia set) < 2 rigorously by machine. But all of these certify one renormalization operator at a time, whereas MLC needs bounds uniform over all combinatorics including cusp-degenerating limits; no one has proposed a computer-assisted route to full a priori bounds in print. Formal verification has one real foothold: Geoffrey Irving's Lean 4 'ray' project machine-verified the connectedness of M and all Multibrot sets via Bottcher coordinates (with Koebe quarter and verified interval-arithmetic renders) — the deepest holomorphic dynamics formalized anywhere — though local connectivity, puzzles, and renormalization remain unformalized by anyone.

The remaining avenues are thin. O-minimality is a non-starter for M itself (infinitely many hyperbolic components violate the finite-component property of definable sets — folklore, apparently never even written down), with the nearest serious model theory being Pfaffian/Pila-Wilkie work on Bottcher coordinates aimed at unlikely intersections. McMullen's Teichmuller-theoretic towers program was absorbed into the mainstream (his 3-manifold analogies explicitly motivate Kahn's near-degenerate regime), his universality theorem explains why MLC matters for every holomorphic family, and the Bleher-Lyubich-Roeder Lee-Yang-Fisher program exports renormalization dynamics into statistical physics rather than importing anything toward MLC. Transcendental dynamics contributed the 'docile' framework (Alhamed-Rempe-Sixsmith) as an analogue of local connectivity and — via the Marti-Pete-Rempe-Waterman counterexample to Eremenko's conjecture — a sobering precedent that plausible topological conjectures in this field can be false. Meanwhile the mainstream program itself moved sharply in 2026 (Elephant Eyes a priori bounds, January; MLC for parabolically bounded primitive renormalization, June), with Dudko's ICM survey identifying unbounded satellite combinatorics as the last wall before 'a clear path towards the MLC'.

DIMENSION: topology-models
NARRATIVE:
The topological-model side of MLC is completely settled as a system of equivalences; what is open is only whether the equivalent statements are true. Douady and Hubbard (CRAS 1982) proved M is a full compact continuum by exhibiting the conformal isomorphism Phi from the complement of M to the complement of the disk; by Caratheodory-Torhorst, MLC is then equivalent to continuous boundary extension of Phi^{-1}, i.e. to every parameter ray landing with landing point continuous in the angle. The Douady-Thurston pinched disk — the quotient of the disk by Thurston's quadratic minor lamination, equivalently Douady's 'abstract Mandelbrot set' — is a concrete candidate homeomorphism type: there is always a monotone projection relating M's ray combinatorics to the model, and MLC says exactly that this projection is a homeomorphism. Schleicher's fibers (arXiv:math/9902154, math/9902155, math/9902156) localize this: MLC iff every fiber (the equivalence class of parameters not separated by rational ray pairs) is a point, with pointwise triviality strictly stronger than pointwise LC; fibers are the cleanest bridge to rigidity, since 'fiber of c is trivial' literally says 'the combinatorics of c determines c'.

That is why experts insist the real content of MLC is combinatorial rigidity rather than point-set topology. The chain is: MLC iff triviality of fibers iff combinatorial rigidity of (non-hyperbolic) quadratics; rigidity implies density of hyperbolicity (Douady-Hubbard, mid-1980s), and density of hyperbolicity is equivalent (McMullen, via Mane-Sad-Sullivan) to the No Invariant Line Fields conjecture — a queer interior component would be exactly a fat fiber. Yoccoz (c. 1990) proved trivial fibers at all at-most-finitely-renormalizable non-hyperbolic parameters; AKLS extended this to unicritical families and Kozlovski-van Strien to non-renormalizable polynomials of all degrees; Schleicher had shown fibers are trivial at Misiurewicz points and hyperbolic-component boundaries. Hence MLC is precisely rigidity of infinitely renormalizable quadratics. Refereed coverage there includes Lyubich's secondary-limb class (Acta 1997), Kahn and Kahn-Lyubich's primitive a priori bounds classes (2006-2009), all real parameters (Levin-van Strien, Lyubich, Graczyk-Swiatek), and — the first satellite breakthrough — Dudko-Lyubich's bounded-type satellite parameters (GAFA 2023, arXiv:1808.10425). The current frontier is preprint-level: MLC at Feigenbaum points via renormalization hyperbolicity (arXiv:2309.02107), Kahn-Kapiamba-Lyubich's parabolically bounded primitive renormalization (arXiv:2606.27272, June 2026), and Cheraghi-Shishikura's high-type satellite control (arXiv:1509.07843), with Dudko's ICM 2026 survey (arXiv:2512.24171) as the authoritative roadmap.

The unconditional counterpart lives on the real line: density of hyperbolicity is a theorem for real quadratics (Graczyk-Swiatek and Lyubich, both 1997) and for all real polynomials with real critical points (Kozlovski-Shen-van Strien, Annals 2007). These proofs exploit the order structure of R, real Koebe/cross-ratio distortion, quasisymmetric rigidity, and — decisively — real a priori bounds valid for ALL real combinatorics. The complex obstruction is now a theorem, not just folklore: Blokh-Levin-Oversteegen-Timorin (arXiv:2205.03157, CMP 2025) proved classical polynomial-like a priori bounds fail for satellite renormalization with unbounded relative periods, so the compactness route that closed the real case cannot close the complex one; Dudko-Lyubich's pacman/pseudo-Siegel technology is the designed replacement, and the unbounded near-neutral satellite regime is universally identified as the last hard case.

On working criteria and quantitative geometry: the field's LC toolkit consists of Caratheodory-Torhorst, Whyburn-type finite-cover criteria (Milnor's expository lectures, arXiv:math/9207220), fiber triviality, and above all Yoccoz's (para)puzzle criterion — nests of puzzle pieces shrink to points when separating annuli have divergent modulus sum. MLC by itself is purely qualitative: compactness would automatically upgrade it to uniform local connectivity and arcwise connectivity (Peano continuum), but with no modulus; since Shishikura's theorem (Annals 1998) gives Hausdorff dimension 2 to the boundary locally everywhere, any Holder Caratheodory loop is capped at exponent 1/2, and experts expect no global Holder modulus at all. The modern quantitative formulations are instead Dudko's 'geometric counterparts': the Bounded Geometry Conjecture for hyperbolic components (uniform qc disks/cardioids with comparable copy diameters, which would imply MLC), uniform precompactness of renormalizations over all boundary scales, and Tan-Lei-type parameter-dynamical similarity. Remaining positive topological facts round out the picture: M connected and full (theorem), boundary dimension 2 (theorem), arcwise connectivity open but known along trivial fibers and tuned real arcs (Kahn, Riedl, Schleicher), and the Lebesgue area of the boundary genuinely open — with Buff-Cheritat's positive-area Julia sets keeping positive boundary area a live, if unresolved, possibility.

NARRATIVE:
The Julia-set side of the MLC landscape is now sharply mapped. Proven locally connected (JLC): all at-most-finitely-renormalizable quadratics with repelling cycles (Yoccoz, c. 1990); all real quadratics z^2+c, c in [-2,1/4] (Levin-van Strien, Ann. of Math. 1998; Lyubich-Yampolsky, Ann. Inst. Fourier 1997, via real a priori bounds); geometrically finite maps — parabolic, Misiurewicz, semi-hyperbolic (Douady-Hubbard; Carleson-Jones-Yoccoz 1994; Tan-Yin 1996); Siegel quadratics of bounded type (Petersen, Acta 1996) and of a.e. rotation number (Petersen-Zakeri, Ann. of Math. 2004), recently extended to higher degree and rational maps (J. Yang arXiv:2010.14003; Wang-Yang-Zhang-Zhang, JLMS 2025); and, decisively, all bounded-type (Feigenbaum) infinitely renormalizable quadratics (Dudko-Lyubich, arXiv:2309.02107, to appear Publ. Math. IHES). Proven NOT locally connected: every Cremer polynomial (Douady-Sullivan, 1980s), Siegel quadratics whose disk boundary omits the critical point (Douady/Herman), and infinitely renormalizable satellite-type examples with rapidly growing combinatorics (Sorensen, J. Geom. Anal. 2000, after Douady-Hubbard; quantified by Levin, CMP 2011).

The logical relationship between JLC and MLC is subtle and asymmetric, and a priori bounds are the hinge. MLC at c does not imply JLC at c: Yoccoz gives MLC at every Cremer parameter while Douady-Sullivan forbid JLC there, and Levin proved MLC (rigidity) at explicit satellite parameters whose Julia sets are non-locally-connected. Conversely, JLC at c yields no route to MLC at c: MLC at an infinitely renormalizable c is equivalent to combinatorial rigidity — the shrinking of the nest of small Mandelbrot copies around c — a parameter-plane statement requiring uniform control over nearby parameters; Dudko's ICM survey stresses that even a locally connected Julia set can pass through many 'near non-JLC' renormalization levels where quadratic-like bounds fail. The proven machine delivering both simultaneously is complex a priori bounds: bounds imply JLC (McMullen's unbranched condition; Hu-Jiang, Jiang TAMS 2000) and, combined with rigidity arguments (Lyubich's secondary limbs condition, Acta 1997; Kahn-Lyubich; AKLS Annals 2009 in the finitely renormalizable degree-d case), imply MLC at c. Notably, Cheraghi-Shishikura (arXiv:1509.07843, still an unpublished manuscript) achieved rigidity — hence MLC — at certain high-type satellite parameters entirely without a priori bounds, using Inou-Shishikura near-parabolic renormalization.

On the real line the story is complete: real a priori bounds (Levin-van Strien; Lyubich-Yampolsky; Graczyk-Swiatek) plus combinatorial rigidity of real quadratics — proven independently by Lyubich (Acta 1997) and Graczyk-Swiatek (Annals 1997), which also settled real density of hyperbolicity — give local connectivity of M at every real parameter. The real case is thus the model of the bounds-plus-rigidity paradigm working end-to-end.

The current frontier is neutral (Siegel/Cremer-adjacent) satellite dynamics, where the obstruction is now a theorem: Blokh-Levin-Oversteegen-Timorin (CMP 2025, arXiv:2205.03157) proved satellite polynomial-like restrictions have moduli bounded above in terms of the relative period, so a priori bounds provably fail for unbounded satellite combinatorics — near-neutral rotation forces degeneration. The response has been to build weaker-but-uniform structures: Dudko-Lyubich's pseudo-Siegel bounds and Mother Hedgehog for arbitrary neutral quadratics (arXiv:2210.09280), extended in 2025 with Luo (arXiv:2509.23031); Cheraghi's trichotomy for high-type irrationally indifferent attractors (arXiv:1706.02678, v5 2025) refining Perez-Marco's hedgehog theory (Acta 1997); and Shishikura-Yang's high-type Siegel Jordan curves (to appear JEMS).

The 2023-2026 feedback loop from JLC into MLC is the strongest in decades: Dudko-Lyubich's GAFA 2023 paper gave the first satellite MLC examples with locally connected, positive-area Julia sets via pacman renormalization; their 2023 Feigenbaum-points theorem closed the entire bounded-type case (JLC + MLC + Goldberg-Khanin-Sinai universality); and Kahn-Kapiamba-Lyubich's June 2026 preprint (arXiv:2606.27272) pushes MLC into 'parabolically bounded' primitive combinatorics degenerating toward the cusp. Dudko's ICM 2026 survey frames what remains — unbounded satellite/neutral combinatorics, conjecturally handled by a hyperbolic Molecule renormalization — as the last structural obstacle. Full MLC remains open as of July 2026, with no credible claim of a complete proof.

GAP-FILL NARRATIVE:
The sharpest calibration for MLC is that its exact analogues are refereed-theorem FALSE in every neighboring parameter space. In the cubic family, Pierre Lavaurs proved in his 1989 Orsay thesis ('Systemes dynamiques holomorphes: explosion de points periodiques paraboliques') that the cubic connectedness locus is not locally connected, using parabolic implosion; the geography behind the proof — products of Mandelbrot sets sitting inside cubic parameter space — was observed numerically by Milnor ('Remarks on iterated cubic maps', Experiment. Math. 1 (1992)) and rigorously realized by Epstein-Yampolsky's intertwining surgery (Ann. Sci. ENS 32 (1999), 151-185; arXiv:math/9608213), whose abstract explicitly credits Milnor's experiments as inspiring Lavaurs' non-LC proof.

The antiholomorphic story is even more instructive because it fails in exactly the places where the quadratic proof machinery lives. Nakane (ETDS 13 (1993)) proved the tricorn is connected, but only via a real-analytic — not conformal — uniformization of its exterior, already signaling that parameter rays behave differently. Hubbard-Schleicher (arXiv:1209.1753; Frontiers in Complex Dynamics, Princeton 2014, 73-102) proved no multicorn of degree d >= 2 is path connected: umbilical cords of odd-period hyperbolic components wiggle in sin(1/x)-like structures. Since a compact connected locally connected metric space is arcwise connected, the multicorns are not locally connected — the tricorn analogue of MLC is false. Inou-Mukherjee then destroyed the ray-landing paradigm itself (Invent. Math. 204 (2016), 869-893; arXiv:1406.3428): rational parameter rays accumulating on odd-period hyperbolic components do not land but accumulate on arcs of positive length of parabolic parameters, and the boundary contains undecorated real-analytic arcs, so centers of hyperbolic components and Misiurewicz parameters are not dense on the boundary — three foundational density/landing facts about M, all false one door over.

The second pillar of the quadratic theory, continuity of Douady-Hubbard straightening, also collapses outside the quadratic unicritical world. Inou's preprint 'Combinatorics and topology of straightening maps II: discontinuity' (arXiv:0903.4289, 2009, apparently still unpublished; Part I with Kiwi is refereed) shows straightening is discontinuous in degree >= 3 except in disjoint type; Inou-Mukherjee (Trans. AMS 374 (2021), 6445-6481; arXiv:1605.08061) proved the baby-tricorn-to-tricorn straightening is discontinuous at infinitely many explicit parameters by showing all non-real umbilical cords wiggle, settling a conjecture of Hubbard, Milnor, and Schleicher — the first discontinuity of straightening on a real two-dimensional slice of an analytic family. Meanwhile Buff-Henriksen (Comm. Math. Phys. 220 (2001), 333-375) showed even a one-complex-dimensional cubic slice can have connected but non-locally-connected bifurcation locus, by embedding quasiconformal copies of Cremer quadratic Julia sets into parameter space.

The expert synthesis: MLC cannot be a consequence of soft topology or general renormalization principles, because every ingredient — landing of rational rays, density of Misiurewicz points, continuity of straightening, local connectivity itself — provably fails for cubics, multicorns, or antiholomorphic slices. Whatever proves MLC must exploit what is genuinely special to unicritical holomorphic families: conformal exterior uniformization, continuous quadratic straightening, and quadratic-specific rigidity (a priori bounds). This is precisely why experts treat MLC as plausible (no such obstruction mechanism has ever been found inside M, and rays do land at all known classes of parameters) yet delicate (the obstruction mechanisms exist and are theorems immediately next door).

GAP-FILL NARRATIVE:
The measure-theoretic literature supplies the quantitative dimension the combinatorial MLC dossier lacks: it says how big the remaining bad set is, and the answer is 'invisible to every natural boundary measure, yet topologically and dimensionally fat'. The two pillar theorems are Graczyk-Swiatek (Inventiones 142 (2000) 605-629) and Smirnov (IMRN 2000 no. 7, 333-351): for Lebesgue-a.e. external angle — equivalently harmonic-measure-a.e. boundary point of the degree-d Mandelbrot set — the parameter ray lands at a Collet-Eckmann parameter, and in Smirnov's version the exceptional angle set even has Hausdorff dimension zero. Collet-Eckmann parameters have all cycles repelling and are at most finitely renormalizable, so Yoccoz's theorem already gives local connectivity of M there. The standard corollary (stated in surveys rather than as a numbered refereed theorem) is that MLC holds at harmonic-measure-almost-every boundary point, and the entire open problem lives on a harmonic-measure-zero (indeed angle-dimension-zero) set of infinitely renormalizable parameters.

Graczyk-Swiatek then spent two decades sharpening what 'typical boundary point' looks like: the critical-value Lyapunov exponent is exactly log d (IMRN 2015, doi 10.1093/imrn/rnu161); typical points are not John-accessible, the boundary spirals infinitely around typical rays, they are reachable only through flat angles with iterated-log-slow aperture decay, and the bifurcation locus is non-porous on a positive density of scales along a.e. ray (JLMS 106 (2022) 2730-2751, doi 10.1112/jlms.12646 — beware secondary sources that misquote this as porosity); and typical points are McMullen-style measurably deep, i.e. the complement of M_d has vanishing area density there, with C^1-conformal parameter-phase similarity maps whose derivative inverts a convergent transversality series (Advances in Mathematics 421 (2023) 108998, arXiv:1904.09434). So at typical points MLC holds but the geometry is maximally hairy — a point the dossier should make when explaining why numerics near the boundary are hard.

The size ledger for the bad set is a striking mismatch of gauges. Makarov's 1985 theorem makes harmonic measure exactly one-dimensional, while Shishikura (Annals 147 (1998) 225-267, arXiv:math/9201282) makes the boundary Hausdorff-dimension two, with HD(J_c) = 2 for Baire-generic boundary c — the exact opposite of the measure-typical picture where HD(J_c) < 2. On the MLC-hard side, Avila-Lyubich (Annals 195 (2022) 1-88, arXiv:1504.02986) produced Feigenbaum quadratic polynomials with positive-area Julia sets, with a positive-Hausdorff-dimension set of such parameters, while Dudko-Sutherland (Inventiones 221 (2020) 167-202, arXiv:1712.08638) proved the classical Feigenbaum Julia set has dimension < 2 and zero area. Whether the boundary of M itself (equivalently its infinitely renormalizable part) has positive area remains open in both directions. Two flanking results complete the picture: on the real slice, Lyubich (Annals 156 (2002)) and Avila-Moreira (Annals 161 (2005) 831-881) prove Lebesgue-a.e. real parameter is regular or Collet-Eckmann, so the real MLC-hard set is Lebesgue-null; and De Thelin-Gauthier-Vigny (Bull. LMS 53 (2021) 660-672, arXiv:2003.14355) extend bifurcation-measure-typical Collet-Eckmann to general algebraic families, showing the phenomenon is not an artifact of z^d + c.

Bottom line for the dossier: between 'proven classes' and 'open cases' sits a proven quantitative statement — the open cases form a set of zero harmonic measure and zero angle-dimension, so MLC is settled at typical boundary points in every external-observer sense; yet that same exceptional set carries full Hausdorff dimension 2 of the boundary, contains positive-area Julia-set parameters, and its Lebesgue area is an open problem, which is why the conjecture is simultaneously 'almost everywhere true' and genuinely hard.

Under the Sullivan dictionary (Sullivan 1985), the MLC conjecture has an exact Kleinian-group counterpart, and that counterpart is a solved problem. The correspondence was articulated remarkably early: McMullen's ICM 1990 Kyoto address places the Douady-Hubbard MLC conjecture side by side with Thurston's conjecture on Bers' boundary, observing that both assert a geometrically infinite conformal dynamical system is uniquely encoded by a lamination on the circle (invariant under z^2 on one side, under a surface group on the other). His 1995 Current Developments survey makes the program symmetric and quantitative: the Ending Lamination Conjecture (ELC, his Conjecture 4.3) implies density of convex cocompact Kleinian groups (Cor. 4.13), exactly as MLC implies density of hyperbolicity in the quadratic family (Douady-Hubbard, Cor. 6.2), with MLC at a parameter equivalent to combinatorial rigidity (Thm. 6.1). The Kleinian side was then actually carried out: Minsky's punctured-torus classification (Ann. of Math. 149 (1999), arXiv:math/9807001) - whose Section 8 is literally titled "Connectivity and a priori bounds" and proves uniform length bounds on pivot geodesics - followed by the full Ending Lamination Theorem via Minsky's bilipschitz model manifold (Ann. of Math. 171 (2010), arXiv:math/0302208) and Brock-Canary-Minsky (Ann. of Math. 176 (2012), arXiv:math/0412006). The density corollary was completed too: the Bers-Sullivan-Thurston density conjecture, the analogue of the still-open Fatou conjecture, was proved by Ohshika (Geom. Topol. 2011) and Namazi-Souto (Acta Math. 2012) using Tameness plus the ELT, after Brock-Bromberg's drilling results (Acta 2004). Expert consensus on the pairing is documented verbatim: a 2005 BIRS report (Binder-Jones-Rohde-Yampolsky) says the ELC "had the same place in the field as the MLC" conjecture, and - presciently - predicted the then-new Kahn-Lyubich Quasi-Additivity Law would lead to MLC for satellite-type infinitely renormalizable parameters.

The missing conceptual layer in the dossier is Douady-Lavaurs parabolic implosion, the theory that explains why the near-parabolic/satellite regime is the hard core of MLC. Lavaurs' 1989 Orsay thesis (under Douady) proved that when a parabolic parameter is perturbed, suitably timed high iterates converge not to the limiting map but to Lavaurs maps g_sigma — compositions of attracting Fatou coordinate, phase translation, and inverse repelling Fatou coordinate — and Julia sets jump discontinuously to enriched geometric limits J(f_0, g_sigma). Douady's 1994 survey 'Does a Julia set depend continuously on the polynomial?' (AMS Proc. Sympos. Appl. Math. 49, 91-138) made the discontinuity of c -> J_c at parabolic parameters canonical. The local classification data are the Ecalle-Voronin invariants, dynamically packaged as horn maps between Ecalle cylinders; Inou-Shishikura's near-parabolic renormalization — the engine of the dossier's Cheraghi/Buff-Cheritat threads — is literally a normalized horn map, and Shishikura's Annals 147 (1998) theorem that the boundary of M has Hausdorff dimension 2 is the flagship quantitative application of implosion. The MLC-relevant moral: near parabolics, renormalization loses compactness because limits are enriched by geometric limits (a direct analogue of geometric limits of Kleinian groups), and every modern attack on satellite parameters is an attempt to control this enrichment.

The same machinery has recently produced two new homeomorphic incarnations of M, both absent from the sweep. Petersen and Roesch (arXiv:2107.09407, v2 April 2024, 80 pp — still a preprint as of mid-2026 but already load-bearing in refereed literature) settled Milnor's 1993 conjecture from 'Geometry and dynamics of quadratic rational maps' (Experiment. Math. 2 (1993)): the parabolic Mandelbrot set M_1, the connectedness locus of the slice Per_1(1) of quadratic rational maps with a persistent multiplier-1 parabolic fixed point, is homeomorphic to M, uniquely so under hybrid identification. Strikingly, the homeomorphism is nowhere Holder on the boundary, so it admits no local quasiconformal extension: M_1 is a topological twin of M but a metric stranger, the singularity being of Minkowski-question-mark type (parabolic vs hyperbolic external circle dynamics). Building on this, Bullett and Lomonaco (Advances in Mathematics 458 (2024), art. 109956; arXiv:2010.04273) proved the modular Mandelbrot set M_Gamma — the connectedness locus of the Bullett-Penrose (2:2) holomorphic correspondences, each a mating of PSL(2,Z) with a parabolic quadratic map by their Inventiones 220 (2020) theorem resolving the 1994 Bullett-Penrose conjecture — is homeomorphic to M_1, hence to M. The enabling technology is Lomonaco's parabolic-like mapping theory (arXiv:1111.7150), the exact parabolic analogue of Douady-Hubbard straightening.

For the MLC dossier the payoff is threefold. First, since local connectivity is a topological invariant, MLC is now equivalent to local connectivity of M_1 and of M_Gamma: MLC has acquired genuinely new models — one living entirely inside the parabolic world, one on the Kleinian-group side of Sullivan's dictionary — through which puzzle technology and mating theory can be transported. Second, the nowhere-Holder phenomenon is a sharp warning that topological equivalence of parameter spaces coexists with radical metric inequivalence, the same tension that makes a priori bounds (metric statements) so much harder than combinatorial rigidity. Third, Pascale Roesch — whose Yoccoz-puzzle expertise (local connectivity of cubic Newton Julia sets, Ann. Sci. ENS 2008; Fatou component boundaries with Yin) powers the M_1 proof — belongs in any census of active MLC-adjacent puzzle practitioners. McMullen's 2026 Inventiones paper on the question mark function and conformal welding of cusped Julia sets shows this parabolic-hyperbolic circle of ideas is producing top-tier refereed results right now.

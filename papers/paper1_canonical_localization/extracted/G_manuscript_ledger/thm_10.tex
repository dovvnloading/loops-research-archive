\begin{proposition}[Base-change propagation of sheet resolution; manuscript Prop.~12.52]\label{prop:base-change}
Let \(X\xrightarrow{i}Z\xleftarrow{f}Y\), suppose \(i=e\circ q\) with \(X\xrightarrow{q}P\xrightarrow{e}Z\), where \(q\) is a covering and \(e\) is an embedding, and form
\[
E=X\times_Z Y,\qquad R=P\times_Z Y .
\]
Then \(q':E\to R\), \(q'(x,y)=(q(x),y)\) is a covering, \(e':R\hookrightarrow Y\), \(e'(p,y)=y\) is an embedding, and the pullback projection \(i':E\to Y\) satisfies \(i'=e'\circ q'\). The \(i'\)- and \(q'\)-kernel pairs agree, and
\[
(q')^{-1}(p,y)\cong q^{-1}(p).
\]
Thus the factorization and its infinite full fibers propagate through every full, unselected exact Kahn equalizer on the complementary-cover locus. This statement neither selects the proof-used connected component nor extends the factorization across a filled or prime-end completion.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The square defining \(q'\) is the pullback of the cover \(q\), so \(q'\) is a cover. If \(e'(p_1,y_1)=e'(p_2,y_2)\), then \(y_1=y_2=y\) and \(e(p_1)=f(y)=e(p_2)\). Injectivity of \(e\) gives \(p_1=p_2\); the inverse on the image is \(y\mapsto(e^{-1}f(y),y)\), so \(e'\) is embedded. The remaining identities follow by substitution and injectivity of \(e'\).
\end{proof}

# Research assessment: Paper 3

Date: 2026-07-27

## Bottom line

Keep this as a separate paper, but keep it in the category it can honestly
occupy: a specialist planar-topology and proof-technology paper about finite
protected carrier recognition. It is not a new case of Mandelbrot local
connectivity, and its attachment formula is not a new foundation for planar
separation theory.

The original 79-page Draft I was not independently publishable in its existing
form. Its final direct-decoration application substantially duplicated the
stronger companion compactification paper, while several prime-end conventions
lagged corrections already made there. Draft II repairs that problem. It gives
Paper 3 a clean role:

- Paper 1 contains canonical localization and collision/factorization machinery.
- Paper 2 contains the direct-decoration rooted-end compactification.
- Paper 3 contains finite protected-access certification, exact recognition and
  failure tests, packet audits, and the finite-to-infinite compactness gate.

On that scope, the work has real standalone value. The strongest value is the
integrated certificate architecture, not any one classical ingredient.

## What is plausibly publishable

The paper now has four mutually supporting theorem packages.

1. A protected ribbon-routing certificate is realized by a full continuum
   while specified nulled prime-end chains survive, distinct relative collars
   remain distinct, and cyclic order in each common source complement is
   preserved without local connectivity.
2. Certificate existence is equivalent to one component of a frozen free
   domain carrying simultaneous singleton ports to every atom. Failure comes
   with a separator that cannot be repaired by monotone enlargement while the
   inventory remains frozen.
3. A fixed typed packet assignment has order-independent fullification and a
   finite audit with persistent merger, localization-escape, and port-loss
   witnesses.
4. The new attachment section gives a homomorphism-level separation ledger,
   an exact connected-trace criterion for arbitrary full continuum connectors,
   arbitrary finite defect birth under Hausdorff convergence, and a safe
   decreasing-envelope limit theorem.

Together these form a coherent methods paper: realization, recognition,
auditing, sharp failure, and a controlled limiting regime.

## New proof work in Draft II

For a connected connector \(C\), pairwise disjoint full atoms \(A_i\), traces
\(T_i=C\cap A_i\), and
\(B_i=C\cup A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_i\), compact-pair Mayer--Vietoris and compacta
Alexander duality give

```text
0 -> reduced Čech H^0(T_i)
  -> Čech H^1(B_i)
  -> ker[Čech H^1(B_{i-1}) -> Čech H^1(T_i)]
  -> 0.
```

The restriction to \(T_i\) is surjective. Under finite-rank hypotheses this
gives the corrected balance law

```text
sigma(B_i)
  = sigma(B_{i-1})
    + components(T_i) - 1
    - sigma(T_i).
```

The negative term is essential: attachment along a separating trace can fill
a pre-existing hole. Omitting it is false, as the closed-annulus/inner-disk
example shows. The paper now states the short exact sequence naturally, the
splitting only noncanonically, and the rank identity only under explicit
finiteness hypotheses.

When the connector and atoms are full, every trace is nonseparating. The final
carrier is therefore full if and only if every trace is connected, with no
finite-component or local-connectivity hypothesis. This extends the
one-point-tree realization to arbitrary full continuum connectors with
connected traces.

The Hausdorff example is sharp: full polygonal-arc connectors meeting one atom
once can converge to an arc whose limiting trace has \(m+1\) components, making
exactly \(m\) bounded complementary components. Thus singleton traces and
fullness do not pass through arbitrary Hausdorff limits. The positive
counterpart is the decreasing-envelope theorem: decreasing full continua with
connected nonempty atom traces have a full intersection with connected traces,
and common protected-collar data pass to the limiting carrier.

## Corrections made beyond the new section

- Replaced weak nested-side language with nulled prime-end chains.
- Replaced avoidance of whole closed collars with relative tails
  \(\overline D\setminus A\), so impressions may overlap inside an atom.
- Made port/tree incidence and embedded-tree conditions explicit.
- Repaired the slit lemma's impossible boundary-valued map into an open domain
  and its deleted-tip mismatch; the proof now removes half-open slits and
  reattaches accessible tips.
- Repaired the one-atom sufficiency case in the free-component theorem.
- Corrected the Carathéodory statement: one landing access is not equivalent to
  local connectivity; continuous extension of the whole boundary map is.
- Replaced CW/singular-only duality language with compacta Čech cohomology and
  a source that covers the needed generality.
- Restricted cyclic-order comparison to chains in one source complement.
- Replaced an overstrong “prime end for every tree edge” claim by the precise
  regular-neighborhood ribbon boundary word.
- Strengthened packet naturality to require transport of the entire typed
  assignment.
- Bundled every non-collar forbidden constituent into the certificate's compact
  set \(Z\), eliminating a formal mismatch in the necessity proof.

## Literature and novelty

The historical baseline is substantial. Janiszewski--Mullikin separation,
Borsuk's unicoherence theory, Eilenberg's multicoherence theory, compacta
Alexander duality, and compact-pair Mayer--Vietoris already contain the
underlying contact-count mechanism. The paper now says this explicitly. The
general ledger is best described as a classical homological mechanism tailored
to the protected-carrier problem; identical modern notation is not evidence of
priority.

The defensible contribution is the synthesis:

- named prime-end accesses represented without landing assumptions;
- relative collar tails and a frozen future inventory;
- exact equivalence with a common free component;
- typed packet completion and monotone-persistent failure witnesses;
- the homomorphism-level attachment diagnostic;
- a sharp demonstration of how finite carriers fail at a Hausdorff limit; and
- a positive inverse-envelope architecture.

Kahn--Lyubich decoration theory already supplies a priori bounds in its
decoration category. Kahn--Kapiamba--Lyubich now prove bounds and MLC in a
parabolically bounded primitive category using substantially quantitative
machinery. Dudko's ICM survey describes the broader MLC program. Paper 3 is a
finite planar interface to such work, not a replacement for modulus estimates,
coherent all-depth marking, or rigidity.

The open obligations include all-depth direct-decoration closure, virtual

scope boundary in Draft II. A finite carrier theorem can be complete and useful
while the global program remains open.

## Publication recommendation

Treat Draft II as a serious research preprint and seek two targeted human
reviews before journal submission:

1. a continuum/prime-end topologist should audit the compact-pair Čech
   Mayer--Vietoris invocation, the crosscut-survival lemma, and the cyclic-order
   argument; and
2. a complex dynamicist should audit the comparison and priority language
   against the decoration, puzzle, and Hubbard-tree literature.

The mathematical spine is now credible, but roughly 70 pages is long for the level of
the main claim. A journal version should probably compress the extended
comparisons, one of the two proofs of the one-point attachment lemma, and some
provenance detail, aiming nearer 45--55 pages. Keep the explicit failure
examples and the new attachment/limit section; those materially improve the
paper.

Do not merge the direct-decoration application back in. If a downstream
application is desired, use one short corollary that cites Paper 2 and makes
the dependency one-way.

## Best next research horizon

The new results isolate three concrete, non-rhetorical gates:

1. Construct decreasing full connector envelopes, compatible with pullback,
   whose traces remain connected and whose relative collars are frozen across
   levels.
2. Add quantitative routing inside the certified free component: modulus,
   width, or uniform incidence control that the finite topological theorem
   deliberately does not provide.
3. Prove coherent all-depth prime-end marking in the complement. Finite
   distinctness alone does not control overlapping impressions or collapsing
   collar widths.

That is where new discovery should now be concentrated. Another abstract
compactness extraction will not cross the gate; the missing work is a
dynamically natural inverse-envelope construction plus quantitative control.

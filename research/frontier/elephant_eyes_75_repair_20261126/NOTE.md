# Verdict: BOUNDED NEGATIVE / OPEN — Elephant Eyes (7.5), p. 18

**No**: this follow-up did not establish the missing native multiplicity, nor construct a configuration satisfying the hypotheses of §7 that violates it. The precise missing lemma is below. The main new result is a sharp *conditional scalar threshold* for weakening the factor in the printed two-case loss argument: with the same summation set and the same newborn bounds, a retained normalized factor strictly above 4/5 gives positive order-N loss. In particular, a sufficiently near-full factor less than 2N+1 suffices for that step; a single packet does not suffice uniformly. This is not an erratum-level repair or a refutation of (7.5).

## 1. Native hypotheses and the precise open interface

EE §4.0.1 (p. 9): start with an even quadratic-like map with connected Julia set and repelling fixed points, and a renormalization of period p with filled little Julia sets K_i, K_0 containing 0. E1 fixes rotation 1/q at the dividing fixed point and the specified sector mappings (central degree two, other sector restrictions univalent); E2 says K_0 is the unique little set in the central domain; E3 says p=q+b with b bounded by a fixed bound and b<q. §7.1 (p. 15) works with the corresponding renormalizable pseudo-QL maps, N>1, translation disk Ω_l formed by M=2N+1 consecutive sectors in the translation region, index set I_l=[l-N,l+N] modulo p, with appropriate representatives of the chain of short arcs. P1 is (1−ε)-comparability of local weights within the slice; P2 puts at least (1−ε) of each local weight on the two short arcs. The Translation Property gives L disjoint such slices for large p. Lemma 5.1 (p. 11) gives W_1≤…≤W_{p−1}≤W_0≤2W_1, W_loc=W_0. The failure branch is W_ver<λ W_loc (λ>1). No abstract incidence model is claimed to meet any of these dynamical/analytic hypotheses.

The text defines Ω′ as the p-return pullback based at K_l (§4.0.3, p. 10); set D_s=(f^s)*Ω′, using the paper's centered pseudo-domain pullbacks and **actual integer times**, rather than only residues. On p. 17 the argument introduces V=D_l, a central packet and fast pull-off; ν_n is the total horizontal weight lost under the next pullback in D_{n−1} (count each actual canonical terminal arc once in this step). On p. 18 the last-legitimate-pullback map applied to the single family of arcs based at K_0 in V is at most two-to-one, and the terminal weights total at least W_0/2 as printed. This bound and containment in at least one of M candidate domains give no lower bound on the number of distinct counted occurrences beyond one per terminal arc. The page switches between I, I_l and I_0. Identifying these as actual time intervals, not just relabeling residues, is part of the missing transport.

**Missing native lemma (target, not proved).** Fix a bound B on b and λ>1. For every sufficiently small ε>0 there is N_0=N_0(B,λ,ε) such that for every N≥N_0, one can choose sufficiently large L and thresholds p_* and T_* depending only on B,λ,ε,N,L (not f or l), with the following property. For every renormalizable pseudo-QL map f with the above E1–E3, p≥p_*, W_loc≥T_*, W_ver<λ W_loc, and every admissible translation window Ω_l satisfying P1/P2 and falling in the no-definite-vertical-mass branch of §7.4–7.5, let J_l be the actual integer representative of the M-step event interval I_l used to sum (7.3) and (7.4), with D_s as above. Then the native event measure satisfies

    Σ_{n∈J_l} ν_n ≥ (1−ε) M W_0/2.                     (ML)

Here "admissible" includes the hypotheses of the §7.4 low-vertical-width choice; if a different actual interval is required by the construction, the loss and newborn estimates must be proved on that same interval. All constants uniform in f, p, l subject to thresholds. This quantifies the missing estimate without assuming the conclusion of the Amplification Theorem.

A sufficient constructive form is:

* For every such f and Ω_l, produce M source packets P_s indexed by distinct start times s modulo p in the relevant one-cycle interval, consisting of actual canonical horizontal arcs based at K_0 in D_s, with total tagged mass at least (1−ε)M W_0. The stronger per-packet lower bound (1−ε)W_0 would suffice but is not logically necessary.
* For every tagged arc (s,β), construct its legitimate last pullback in D_{n−1} for some actual n∈J_l, with both lifts at the next step illegitimate. Write the destination e=(n,γ). Supply a weight comparison w(e)≥w_s(β), or quantify a total error and absorb it in ε. Prove all maps at the proper markings/endpoints, including when extra return or restriction is involved.
* For every e, at most two tagged occurrences may map to it in the entire disjoint union of packets; more generally the total preimage weight must be ≤2w(e). Require that for every n the sum of the weights of the distinct destinations at time n is ≤ν_n. Merely being in a geometric representative of some domain does not prove this.

Then Σ_s Σ_β w_s(β)≤2Σ_e w(e)≤2Σ_n ν_n, proving (ML). Distinct actual times count as different occurrences only because the definition of ν_n counts losses at those steps. This is a proof of a conditional implication, not of the hypotheses. A per-index ν_n lower bound is stronger than (ML); the weighted joint version suffices.

## 2. Attempted repair: off-center starts and actual-time accounting

Reopened EE PDF pages 7–18 (selected relevant pages rendered). Lemma 4.1 (p. 10) bounds return images of tree edges, and 4.2 retains the inner strip of the return window and the tree-aligned connecting arcs. Lemma 4.3 excludes pull-off in a pullback omitting K_1 when its endpoint-distance condition holds. None gives a canonical rectangle of specified width in every shifted restriction. Corollary 5.14(ii), p. 13, is a concentration assertion in U minus the little sets for 1≤n≤κq, not a simultaneous assertion in all D_s. Lemma 6.1 (p. 13) constructs the separating pair at K_0, and 6.2 (pp. 13–14) gives fast pull-off in A^p; the application to V is invoked in §7.5. Lemma 6.3 (p. 14) counts the last legitimate arcs at most twice in its global setting. It is the model of the one-packet step, not a window-replication assertion. Corollary 6.4 is not a uniform-in-p substitute: 6.3's initial threshold is not established uniform by that statement.

**Off-center candidate.** In the low-vertical branch on p. 17, (7.1) (p. 15) gives small series conductance not just at the center but for every K_s with |s−l|≤κN. The path decomposition in the proof of Lemma 7.2 (p. 16) suggests extending the bound W(Ω_l minus K_l)≤C W_loc/N to such sites: the peripheral paths are bounded by C W_loc/((1−κ)N), and the vertical paths by the low-vertical mass of the non-peripheral slice. For a fixed κ close to 1 this still tends to zero relative to W_loc. This is a promising *conditional* route: it needs the same family/marking decomposition uniformly for off-center K_s, a proper pullback containing K_0 at the relevant time with the requisite degree and collar bounds (including the extra p-return), and a comparison of the canonical arc weights in the actual D_s with that small annular width. Lemma 7.3 (p. 17) proves its displayed centered estimate with 0≤j<p, n+j≡l. Substituting an off-center target or j+p is not an instance of its stated conclusion. Even if the annular estimate extends, fast pull-off and endpoint retention must be verified for the shifted domains. No verified bridge completing these steps was found. A naive use of P1 replaces W_0 by W_s which may be as low as W_0/2; P1 only compares sites within one slice.

**Joint fiber count, conditional.** If a legitimate chain starts at K_0 at time s and ends at e=(n,γ), an endpoint of γ has label −(n−1−s) modulo p. Hence each of its at most two endpoint labels determines at most one residue class of s. Since the starts are distinct modulo p, there are at most two possible starts. At a fixed start and terminal time a unique forward image in the properly marked covering would determine the source arc. This proves joint multiplicity ≤2 *if* that well-defined image and all terminal memberships hold. The image of a representative under a branched map or immersion after forgetting extra preimages need not by itself define that canonical source arc: the proper homotopy/covering identification is essential.

**New actual-time check.** If both endpoints of a shifted source arc survive as genuine little sets, their labels shift by −1 at each legitimate step. In a genuine covering a pull-off (both lifts illegitimate) at the next step requires crossing the exceptional branch/label situation; the lemma 4.3 restriction also needs endpoint retention. Even granting a residue-level calculation that all candidate pull-off times belong to I_l, actual times n and n+p correspond to different D_{n−1} and D_{n+p−1}. An arc based at a start s at the right end of a chosen integer representative of J_l cannot have a later terminal time inside that same interval. This is a simple time-order obstruction to the *naive* choice of M starts inside J_l, not an obstruction to any appropriately shifted start construction. Starts earlier by a period might fix placement but require renewed confinement and a new weight comparison. Likewise the eroded-window count from #147 gives only geometric endpoint placements, not events. Lemma 3.12 (pp. 7–8) yields equality for genuine coverings and a one-direction comparison for properly mapped arcs; neither it nor K Lemma 2.6 permits reversing the comparison to assert equal weights in a smaller window. Lemmas 3.13–3.15 (pp. 8–9) treat parallel concatenation and broken pieces, not multiple identical-width terminal occurrences.

Thus the missing input is weighted transport to *actual loss events*, not merely combinatorial containment or a bound on fiber size. Neither a full repair nor a native overlap configuration is supplied. The time-order observation is only about proposed labels, not a counterexample satisfying the hypotheses. No claims of failure of EE.

## 3. How much of (7.5) does the printed downstream proof need?

Reopened p. 18, both uses of (7.5), and p. 16, Lemma 7.2 and Theorem 7.1. Write M=2N+1, W=W_loc=W_0, r=W_1/W∈[1/2,1]. Suppose, **conditionally**, for the same actual set J_l, a weakened estimate

    Σν_n ≥ (t M/2) W,   t=α(1−ε),  0<α≤1.

The original uses α=1. From (7.3) and (7.4) (p. 18), assuming the printed O(N) per-step error with uniform constant for fixed B and other parameters, summing over M steps gives the first lower bound on loss H:

    H ≥ Σν_n/2 − Ση_n/4 − O(MN)
      ≥ (MW/4)(t−r) − O(N²).

For the near-equal case p. 18 replaces the newborn estimate (7.2) by (W_0−W_1)+ν_n/2. On the range where that replacement is justified, its summed lower bound (allowing the same harmless additive error) is

    H ≥ Σν_n/2 − M(W_0−W_1) − O(N²)
      ≥ (MW/4)(t−4(1−r)) − O(N²).

**Conditional scalar proposition:** if the two newborn bounds may be used as stated on the respective sides of a split at r=1−a, then a>0 with 1−t<a<t/4 gives a uniform positive coefficient in both cases. Such an a exists iff t>4/5. Indeed r≤1−a gives t−r≥t−1+a>0; r≥1−a gives t−4(1−r)≥t−4a>0. The best scalar lower envelope when both bounds are available is max(t−r,t−4(1−r)); its minimum on [1/2,1] is t−4/5 at r=4/5, by the intersection of the two affine functions. Thus 4/5 is sharp for these *two inequalities alone*, not a necessary native geometric threshold. If t=4/5, the two leading lower bounds vanish at r=4/5; if t<4/5 they are both negative there. This does not mean H is actually negative. If the second bound is only available when a lies in a smaller near-equality regime, also require a in that regime; do not import the sharp threshold as a theorem about the printed geometric argument. In particular, for any fixed allowed small a>0, a sufficiently near-one t satisfies both strict inequalities.

For instance α=19/20 and ε=1/100 yield t=1881/2000; choosing a=1/10 gives t−1+a=81/2000 and t−4a=1081/2000. Then the coefficient is uniformly positive for fixed parameters, provided the replacement is available on r≥9/10. After taking W≫N with constants depending on the margin, the O(N²) term is absorbed and H≥c M W with c>0. An M−O_B(1) retained-factor estimate works as N grows and ε is small. Even a fixed fraction sufficiently close to 1 can work. An O(1) factor (one packet in particular) yields t=O(1/M); it cannot force the required loss uniformly via these estimates. Even at r=1 it supplies only O(W) in the second bound, not order-N W. A positive lower bound with small t in a narrower subcase is not a proof for all r. The code checks the scalar minimization on a rational grid including the exact intersection and tests the strict-margin split; the analytic argument above, not the grid, proves the proposition.

**Does a weaker inequality suffice for Theorem 7.1?** Yes for the *loss step*, under the other printed geometric hypotheses and a near-full factor as above. The theorem itself asks for arbitrary λ>1, not just an amplification slightly above 1 (p. 16). Lemma 7.2 assumes for **each** of the L disjoint translation windows an actual pullback with n≤3p and a marked site projecting within κN of the center, whose annular width is at least δ W with δ>0; it then uses the Covering Lemma and series estimate (7.1) to get local vertical weight ≥c W/N, and sums the distinct sites to obtain λ amplification for L≥2λ N/c. The c depends on the local-width and covering constants; the argument requires N large enough that peripheral conductance is negligible. §7.5 explicitly seeks vertical weight of order N W and uses the two loss cases on p. 18 for that purpose. If a replacement loss of order N W is converted into the same definite-local-width condition uniformly, the smaller factor is compatible with arbitrary λ by choosing L. An O(1) total weight is not shown to give that condition: averaging over O(N) sites would at best give a lower bound of order W/N from an order-W total, not the fixed-fraction δW estimate with the needed interior localization. Nor does one low-vertical window certify all L windows. We do not prove the loss-to-vertical conversion or a new route for smaller factors; a hypothetical fixed δ obtained otherwise could suffice via Lemma 7.2. No verdict that the theorem fails.

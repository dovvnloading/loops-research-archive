## 3. Selected estimate: why (7.5) is load-bearing

Fix N, M=2N+1 and a window Ω_l as in §7.1; I_l=[l−N,l+N] modulo p. The weights W_n^K of §5.0.1 are widths at K_n in the punctured ambient domain U minus the union of the little filled Julia sets; local weights in 6.3 instead refer to U^p minus that union. A width in a restricted window is not automatically the same as the global local weight. In §7.5 the starred pullbacks are of the centered window from §7.1, with Ω′ from §4.0.3; Lemma 4.2 guarantees the inner sites and does not assert exact boundary losses or retention of every site. Use the notation of §7.5: Ω' is the returned/pulled-back window from §4.0.3; V=(f^{l+p})*Ω contains K_0. The sums refer to pull-off events in the successive window pullbacks; ν_n measures the total horizontal weight removed from the (n−1)-st window at its next pullback, and η_n is the part of W_0 used for newborn horizontal arcs.

The paper's preceding displays are

    newborn horizontal weight ≤ (2ν_n+η_n)/4 + O(N),             (7.2)
    loss ≥ ν_n/2 − η_n/4 − O(N),                               (7.3)
    Σ η_n ≤ M W_1.                                              (7.4)

Equation (7.5) is the only lower bound in these three lines of accounting with a factor M. Conditional on the stated error bounds and a consistent identification of the index sets, summation gives

    total loss ≥ M[(1−ε)W_0−W_1]/4 − O(N²),                    (7.6)

which has positive order N·W_loc when W_0 exceeds W_1 by a definite fraction of W_loc and W_loc is sufficiently large for fixed N. In the near-equal case the printed proof replaces (7.2) by ε' W_loc+ν_n/2, so the loss bound is Σν_n/2−M ε' W_loc; again the M factor in (7.5) is needed. A mere W_0/2 lower bound for the whole sum does not give the claimed order-N loss by either displayed case argument for large N. This algebra is conditional on the other inequalities, and does not independently validate (7.2), (7.4), or the replacement estimate.

### Precise sufficient interface; not a repair claim

Let P_m be tagged initial essential packets, one for each of M sites. Suppose (A) each has weight ≥(1−ε)W_0 (or the sum of initial weights has this M-scaled lower bound); (B) each source occurrence has an eligible terminal occurrence (tagged by the actual pull-off step and a canonical horizontal arc of that step's window); (C) its weight does not decrease; (D) every terminal occurrence is hit at most twice in the *whole disjoint union* of initial packets; and (E) the terminal occurrence weights, counted once per actual event, are included in Σ_{n∈I_l}ν_n. Then summing (C), using (D), gives (7.5). An averaged version of (A) with the same total lower bound is enough for (7.5). A factor M−O(b+1) with small additional relative error may suffice for the two-case loss with N large and adjusted error margins; an arbitrary fixed positive fraction of M is not enough for the first case with (7.4) unchanged.

This is an explicit target for a geometric repair, not an asserted consequence of the published hypotheses. In particular, local comparability P1 or repeating the one-packet proof M times without (D)–(E) is insufficient. Nor does an arc belonging to several geometric windows automatically mean its width appears in several distinct ν_n.

### Candidate window-overlap route, checked at the index level only

A possible alternative to M independent source packets is to count repeated placements of a terminal packet at distinct pull-off times. In a purely linear sliding-window model with M sites, endpoints r≤s are both in the eroded interval [t−N+b,t+N−b] exactly when s−N+b≤t≤r+N−b. There are max(0,M−2b−(s−r)) integer centers t. For a short arc (bounded s−r) this is M−O(b+1); in the no-wrap translation range it suggests why an order-M count might be achievable even with boundary losses. The verifier checks the index formula exhaustively on a bounded integer range. In actual EE, 4.2 guarantees the central retained slice and the aligned tree connections, not all canonical arc representatives with those endpoints. P2 concerns short arcs in Ω_l, not arbitrary canonical arcs in V or all their terminal placements. Even a terminal arc with a small cyclic endpoint separation needs a legitimate lift in each window, a canonical rectangle width comparison in that window, and membership in the actual event ν_n (including agreement of the time and center indexing). The eroded-interval count alone proves none of these. If these placements and widths were proved with joint multiplicity ≤2 and total starting mass as above, the weaker factor M−O(b+1) with a small additional relative error would still suffice for the positive-order-N loss. This is a possible route for a subsequent reviewer, not a geometric repair or a separate claimed obstruction.

### Incidence diagnostic (not a dynamical counterexample)

In an abstract incidence model, two tagged source arcs each of weight W_0/2 map to one terminal occurrence of weight W_0/2. The occurrence lies in an eligible domain, and has exactly two preimages. Assign it to one of M eligible event rows, all other rows zero. Total pulled-off weight W_0/2 satisfies the single-packet count and the membership condition, yet is less than M(1−ε)W_0/2 for M≥5 and ε=1/10. This verifies the logical distinction between these two *explicit counting premises* and the asserted conclusion. It does not model the conformal surface, covering, canonical lamination, actual f, or E1–E3. In particular zero rows are not claimed realizable. No actual counterexample; hence BOUNDED NEGATIVE, not GAP.

## 7. Independent review, 2026-11-26 (AI-assisted; not proof certification)

**Final label: BOUNDED NEGATIVE / OPEN.** The version and page locators were checked on EE v1. The map is a statement/dependency map, not verification of every proof; corrections above distinguish theorem numbers, exact confinement bounds, degree and iteration scopes. Section 7.5 uses (7.5) in both cases. The one-packet inference, even granted, gives at most the claimed one-packet lower bound, not the displayed factor M without further geometric information.

**Quantified missing estimate.** Fix the bound on b and λ>1, and a sufficiently small ε>0; take N sufficiently large relative to the errors in confinement, choose L sufficiently large in terms of N and λ, and take the period and local-width thresholds in §7.1–7.2 sufficiently large for these fixed parameters. For every renormalizable ψ-QL f with the written E1–E3 satisfying these thresholds and W^ver<λW_loc, and each admissible Ω_l to which the no-definite-vertical-mass branch of §7.4–7.5 is applied, the required estimate on the actual pull-off event weights ν_n of §7.5 is

    Σ_{n∈I_l} ν_n ≥ (2N+1)(1−ε)W_0/2,    I_l=[l−N,l+N] mod p.

The thresholds and error constants must be uniform in f and the chosen l (with compatible representatives of the actual time indices I, I_l, I_0 in the text). This is the target in the contradiction argument, not a theorem proved here. To derive (7.6), one must also use (7.4) on the same event set. For the first case, if W_0−W_1≥a W_loc with a>0, take ε<a and W_loc sufficiently large for fixed N.

# Verdict: OPEN — no marked local uniform dilatation theorem established

**Status: OPEN / source-audit + conditional radial implication (METADATA only).** The reviewed near-parabolic theorems do not give the requested comparison of the actual satellite polynomial-like returns with quadratics. The ordinary-modulus route is obstructed as in #140, but degeneration of the optimal *marked local dynamical* comparison dilatation is not proved. This is a scoped negative answer about the implications actually justified by the sources below, not a claim of exhaustive nonexistence of a theorem.

Write q_n=n+2 and k_n=product_{j<n} q_j. ## 1. Primary-source audit: what IS controls

* Inou–Shishikura, [The renormalization for parabolic fixed points and their perturbation](https://www.math.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~mitsu/pararenorm/ParabolicRenormalization.pdf), manuscript dated Dec 12, 2008, printed pp. 13–15, Definition of F_1 and Theorems 1–3 (opened and page images inspected). In their coordinates P(z)=z(1+z)^2, and F_1 consists of maps P composed with phi^{-1}, with phi a normalized univalent map on the fixed domain V, admitting a quasiconformal extension to the plane. The critical point -1/3 is in V; the other critical point -1 is outside. This is a single-critical-point **domain-coordinate factorization**, not a conjugacy to a degree-two polynomial. Theorem 1 (p. 13) gives invariance under parabolic renormalization and extension of the new univalent coordinate to a larger domain V' containing the closure of V. Theorem 2 (p. 14) gives uniform contraction for the corresponding renormalization in its Teichmüller structure. Theorem 3 (p. 14) gives perturbative renormalization with a uniformly small nonzero rotation in the specified cones (0<|alpha|<=alpha_*, |arg(±alpha)|<pi/4), landing in a rotated F_1 with extension and contraction. This does not assert that a return of an arbitrary quadratic on its satellite little Julia set is in F_1. The bottom-renormalization discussion (p. 15) warns that “the domain of definition does not have a simple characterization by the multiplier of a single fixed point”; the second fixed point and its multiplier enter.
* The high-type iteration following Theorem 3 concerns continued fractions of irrational rotations, with all partial quotients large. A rational 1/q with q large passes the numerical smallness and positive-cone test if it is the analytic rotation, and its single continued-fraction digit is large. But the next Gauss rotation is zero: -1/(1/q)=-q=0 mod Z. The address (1/3,1/4,...) is a sequence of rational *combinatorial* rotations of different dividing cycles, not the sequence of complete quotients of one irrational. For a repelling dividing point the analytic log is complex and is not 1/q. Thus q>=N alone establishes neither the analytic input domain nor infinite high-type iteration.
* Cheraghi–Shishikura (CS), [Satellite renormalization of quadratic polynomials](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.07843), arXiv:1509.07843v1, pp. 8–9, defines the analogous IS class using P and normalized univalent coordinates with quasiconformal extensions. The family Q_alpha is a separate normalized quadratic family. Theorem 2.9, p. 15, takes f in the rotated IS class with alpha in the specified A^+(r_3), or the admitted Q_alpha family, and gives restricted top and bottom near-parabolic outputs in the corresponding rotated IS classes (new rotations -1/alpha and -1/beta, respectively; beta is the second fixed-point logarithmic multiplier); the coordinates have analytic extension beyond V for inputs in A^+(r_3) × IS (the last sentence of Theorem 2.9 does not assert that extension for the separate Q_alpha case). The next step is allowed only when its rotation is in the appropriate analytic domain again (cf. §5, p. 52). Negative rotations are treated by the conjugate construction. The Teichmüller metric in §2, p. 9, compares the univalent coordinates via inf log Dil(phi_hat_g composed with phi_hat_f^{-1}); Theorems A and B (p. 3) concern the Lipschitz/hyperbolic behavior of these near-parabolic renormalization coordinates. Even granting a uniform quasiconformal extension bound for all the renormalized charts, this is not a bound for dynamical conjugacies of the original polynomial-like maps.

Indeed, for two maps f=P composed with phi_f^{-1}, g=P composed with phi_g^{-1}, the chart comparison h=phi_g composed with phi_f^{-1} satisfies g composed with h=f on the matching domains (with rotations dealt with separately). A conjugacy would require g composed with h=h composed with f. These are different identities; there is no intertwining on the images or control of the ordered access germs in the first identity. The uniform extension and contraction theorems therefore cannot simply be read as the asserted K bound. The stronger-looking QC constructions in CS §3.4 also need checking: the H^1 and H^2 maps (pp. 30, 34–35, Lemmas 3.17 and 3.20) compare translation to a lift on a strip and are used to control the Fatou-coordinate dependence. Their boundary intertwining identities do not identify the actual parent filled-set access sectors with those of a connected quadratic; no uniform marked torus comparison is stated there. IS §3, remark (c), p. 10, also explicitly distinguishes pre/post-composition by generally distinct maps from conjugacy.

## 2. Three towers, and the circularity to avoid

1. Actual dynamical returns F_n=f_c^{k_n}|U_n -> V_n; CS §4.3 and (31)–(32), pp. 49, 52, give the original dividing-cycle multiplier and the classical disk with radius k_n log 2/(2 pi q_n). This is exactly the observable of #139.
2. Recursive PL straightenings: CS Theorem 4.2, p. 48, supplies hybrid straightening of a quadratic-like map, and §4.3, p. 49, uses the polynomial-like renormalization of quadratics. Those straightened maps are quadratics, not automatically the normalized IS coordinate maps. Hybrid conjugacy is not in general conformal on a neighborhood of a repelling boundary point. Its local dilatation is not automatically uniform over n; even uniform bounds on individual successive hybrid identifications would permit products of the bounds to grow. A fixed initial finite-prefix hybrid conjugacy can transfer an already proved marked bound for a fixed realization if it intertwines the relevant returns and markings; it does not prove the missing tail bound, nor uniformity across parameters.
3. Near-parabolic tower g_1=Q_alpha, g_{j+1} the appropriate top or bottom renormalization of g_j, with restrictions and normalizations. On a tower *already admitted* in the analytic domains, CS Proposition 6.2 and Corollary 6.3 (pp. 59–60) identify the appropriate near-parabolic multiplier with the actual dividing multiplier. The rational combinatorics 1/q_n has one digit (cf. (38), p. 56 and (42)–(43), p. 59), hence the all-bottom pattern. This says which operations are to be used; it is not a theorem that every actual return in tower 1 has the global IS form or a uniformly quasiconformal marked dynamical comparison to tower 2. A multiplier identity conditional on admissibility cannot prove the admissibility it assumes. The complex analytic rotations and the integer lift must also be checked; reducing modulo Z loses the lift.

CS Theorem C (p. 5) gives existence, for sufficiently high-type satellite combinatorics, of a quadratic parameter admitting both the indicated PL and near-parabolic renormalizations. Its quantifier is existence, not inclusion of all parameters of an arbitrary nested copy in an IS domain. The prefix starting at 1/3 need not meet its threshold; the tail can be restarted with larger digits, but an independent finite-prefix transfer is needed for conclusions about the original family. Nor does Theorem C assert a uniform marked QC conjugacy even for its constructed representative. For an already admitted bottom step with the correct integer lift -1/beta=-q+alpha_next, |alpha_next|<=r implies |beta|<=1/(q-r); this elementary observation is *conditional* on that lift, admission and identification with the actual rho, not evidence for the requested uniform K or the whole-copy claim.

CS Proposition 6.10, p. 67, uses the QG condition (44), p. 62, including q_{n+1}>=q_n^2 for these single-digit blocks. It does not apply to q_n=n+2 on any tail. Proposition 6.7(b), p. 63, bounds the actual disk under its assumptions; for these blocks G^{m_i-1} is the identity. Substituting the conclusion of the admissible tower into the entrance proof of that tower is circular. The #138 angular question is not settled by this audit. Nothing here disproves CS Theorem D.

## 3. Precise missing statement M and the conditional 1/q calculation

**M (uniform marked local straightening/torus comparison for the satellite tail).** There exist K_*<infinity and N such that for every n>=N and each parameter of the specified class for which F_n and its repelling dividing fixed point a_n exist, there is a quadratic polynomial Q_n with connected filled Julia set, a repelling fixed point b_n, and an orientation-preserving K_*-QC conjugacy between neighborhoods of a_n and b_n (intertwining the dynamical germs whenever defined), taking the cyclically ordered q_n external-access sector germs of F_n to the corresponding external-access sector germs of Q_n. In particular the induced map on the repelling multiplier tori takes the primitive marked access class (-1,q_n) to the same class, with compatible logarithm lift. Neighborhood sizes may shrink with n. A single fixed parameter version of M is strictly weaker than the whole-copy version. For precision, the marked sectors here are the q_n periodically permuted complementary-access germs of the parent filled set, continued by the repelling inverse branch (not arbitrary arcs with the same cyclic order); the induced marking must agree with the attachment-continued logarithm of (32). In the universal log cover, label their lifts by j in Z so translation by 2 pi i advances j by q_n; choose tau so translation by tau advances j by 1. Then q_n tau-2 pi i preserves a lifted sector, and changing tau by 2 pi i k changes the integer lift by k q_n. The analogous choice is made for Q_n. A uniformly QC conjugacy between neighborhoods of the *filled sets* carrying this marking (call this stronger statement M_PL) is sufficient for M. M itself is a weaker local statement, not Levin's literal global-neighborhood hypothesis.

The access requirement is essential. A bare germ at a repelling fixed point can be linearized by Koenigs coordinates. The quadratic w -> rho w+w^2 has the same multiplier at 0, so the two germs are conformally conjugate near their fixed points. This tautological local K=1 need not preserve any external-access sectors or produce a connected filled set with the prescribed dividing point; it gives no Yoccoz bound.

**Real inequality (Levin).** G. Levin, [Boundaries for multipliers of periodic points of holomorphic mappings](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~levin/Levin1990_Article_BoundariesForMultipliersOfPeri), Siberian Mathematical Journal 31 (1990), 273–278, definition and eqs. (1)–(2), p. 273, Theorem 3, p. 277. The original theorem's conjugacy hypothesis is a QC map of neighborhoods of the connected filled sets of the polynomial-like map and its degree-m polynomial, intertwining the maps. For a repelling periodic point of period l on the boundary, with r external accesses, the displayed inequality is log|lambda| <= (2 K l/r) log m. Here l=1 (fixed point of the parent return, not period k_n for the original polynomial), m=2, and r=q_n for the degree-two parent filled set: its straightened dividing point has the prescribed reduced rotation 1/q_n and exactly q_n accesses (CS §4.1, p. 46, §4.3, pp. 48–49, and (31)–(32), p. 52). An orientation-preserving filled-set neighborhood conjugacy transports the external prime-end accesses bijectively. This is a count for the parent, not the child PL cycle. In particular for such a marked neighborhood straightening the bound is log|rho_n|<=2K log 2/q_n. The dilatation in this theorem is the dynamical conjugacy dilatation, not a chart dilatation and not a stated function of the annulus modulus. Primary PDF text opened; the page-image request timed out and direct byte download was unavailable, so this is a text-verified citation rather than a newly byte-locked copy. Do not read the theorem as saying an arbitrary conjugacy of just unmarked fixed-point germs suffices.

**Complex inequality (why M suffices for the radial norm).** J. H. Hubbard, [Local connectivity of Julia sets and bifurcation loci: three theorems of J.-C. Yoccoz](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hubbard/Yoccoz.pdf), pp. 475–476, Proposition 3.2 and Proposition 3.5 (opened and page images inspected). Prop. 3.5 gives the annulus in a polynomial's repelling torus corresponding to a period-q component of the complement of the lifted filled set (one access cycle), with modulus pi/(q log d). Prop. 3.2 bounds the modulus of an annulus in a flat torus by area divided by the squared length of the corresponding shortest lattice translation. Use these two statements on the quadratic comparison, not directly on F_n.

Write tau=log rho_n, Re tau>0. The torus for the repelling germ of F_n is C/(2 pi i Z+tau Z), from punctured linearizing coordinates. A local dynamical QC conjugacy descends to a QC torus map of the same dilatation: represent each class sufficiently close to the fixed point using the local contracting inverse, and extend the conjugacy equivariantly by the multiplier. Compatibility makes this independent of the representative; in the linearizing charts and their conformal translates the dilatation does not increase. By the marking, the pullback annulus has the required core translation q_n tau-2 pi i. QC modulus distortion and the two propositions imply

    pi/(K_* q_n log 2) <= mod A_n <= 2 pi Re tau / |q_n tau - 2 pi i|^2.

In particular |tau-2 pi i/q_n|^2 <= (2K_* log 2/q_n) Re tau. For z_n=tau/(2 pi i), put b=K_* log 2/(2 pi). Completing the square,

    |z_n-(1/q_n-i b/q_n)| <= b/q_n,
    q_n |z_n| <= sqrt(1+b^2)+b = C(K_*).

This proves the conditional radial 1/q bound with no factor k_n. Levin's real inequality alone bounds Re tau (equivalently -Im z), not the full norm of the marked log. The full disk follows by Hubbard's two propositions and the marking, without invoking Levin on a mere germ. No angular conclusion is asserted. A weaker sufficient missing input for this proof (not asserted equivalent to M or M_PL) is an embedded annulus of modulus >= pi/(K_* q_n log 2) in the *marked repelling multiplier torus* of F_n with that core. This is not the fundamental annulus V_n minus closure(U_n). For every prescribed small r>0, M gives |z_n|<=r once q_n>=C(K_*)/r. With the whole-copy quantifiers this would supply the **actual-multiplier** radial input needed at the one-digit blocks of CS Proposition 6.7(b) for this address, not the claimed containment of their original large PLY disks and not the hypotheses of the entire CS proof.

Neither Levin nor Hubbard supplies M: they give the conditional estimate once the comparison/annulus is supplied. The IS coordinate-change result has neither the dynamical nor the marked-torus conclusion of M.

## 4. Other comparison routes and why they do not close M

* M. Lyubich, [Dynamics of quadratic polynomials I, II](https://commack.math.stonybrook.edu/~mlyubich/papers/acta.pdf), Acta Math. 178 (1997), Proposition 2.5, pp. 195–196, reviewed in #140: a uniform positive lower bound mu on the quadratic-like modulus yields a bound on the Teichmüller distance to the quadratic family depending on mu. This is a sufficient condition for ordinary straightening control; there is no converse here about marked local conjugacies.
* Blokh–Levin–Oversteegen–Timorin (BLOT), [version of record, CMP 406:141 (2025)](https://publications.hse.ru/pubs/share/direct/1052158150.pdf), Main Theorem p. 2, Definitions 2.3–2.4 p. 6 (opened and page 2 inspected; use the edition identified in #140). For the actual satellite PL restrictions satisfying its separation/satellite hypotheses, the upper bound for relative satellite period s and degree d*=2 is 2 pi/log(4(s+1)). For the parent F_n in question, s=q_{n-1}, so this tends to zero (for n>=2); see #140's index check. Thus demanding a uniform ordinary fundamental annulus lower bound is not an available route. This upper bound does not give a lower bound on the distortion of any marked local comparison of shrinking neighborhoods.
* Dudko–Lyubich, [MLC at Feigenbaum points](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.02107v3), Theorem A p. 3 and the definition of bounded combinatorics on p. 11 (opened, theorem image inspected), has bounded-type hypotheses in its renormalization bounds. Our q_n are unbounded. Neither that theorem nor compactness for bounded-type towers gives M for this tail.
* Other recent neutral-renormalization approaches were screened, not exhaustively audited: Dudko–Lyubich [2210.09280v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.09280v2), §1.1 and §1.5(IV) (HTML), discusses the extension to satellite combinatorics as a direction; [2609.37383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37383), Dudko–Lim–Lyubich, neutral towers (author reports abstract access; independent reviewer read the institutional [IMS abstract](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/ims-preprints), but the arXiv full text and theorem page are UNVERIFIED in the independent review; #135's historical review is not a substitute). The latter compares bi-infinite attractor towers near Mother Hedgehogs. Even if applicable to those towers, an entry/identification theorem for the actual forward unbounded satellite quadratic-like tower, transporting its marked repelling sectors to a quadratic, would still be required. No page-verified use of that theorem is made here. No claim is made that all recent preprints have been examined.

The #140 obstruction is therefore inherited for the ordinary-modulus sufficient route, **not** for M itself. There is no demonstrated sequence on this address on which every marked local conjugacy must have K_n -> infinity. An independent proof of M or a direct proof of the marked annulus input is a precise next research problem.

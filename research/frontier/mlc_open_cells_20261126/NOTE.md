## Empty-cell proofs (B)

Let P_j be the absolute periods of successive maximal renormalizations and r_j=P_j/P_(j-1) their integer relative periods, as in the atlas and DL26 §2.2.5 p.11. A finite prefix changes neither boundedness nor an eventual digit-growth condition. Real means original parameter real; canonical maximal straightening commutes with conjugation (uniqueness of the quadratic straightening). The primary nontrivial limbs are exchanged by conjugation; disjoint conjugate wakes admit no real infinitely renormalizable point unless the rotation is 1/2.

- B1 (14): Cofinal absolute primitive periods with bounded successive ratios bound each intervening maximal relative period: each is an integer factor of such a ratio. A finite prefix cannot create unboundedness. Kahn arXiv:math/0609045v3 p.4 defines the bounded ratio condition.
- B2 (12): An eventually primitive address has finitely many satellite levels. Each rational signed continued fraction is finite; the union of their digits is finite.
- B3 (6): Real maximal straightening preserves conjugation; every nonzero satellite dividing rotation equals its negative modulo one, hence 1/2 and period 2. An eventually satellite real address has only finitely many preceding primitive levels, so all relative periods are bounded.
- B4 (2): Real maximal straightening preserves conjugation. Each satellite rotation is 1/2, with one signed digit 2; thus every satellite digit is bounded.
- B5 (4): The signed first digit b_(i,1) is the nearest integer to q_i/|a_i|, hence at most q_i; within-level squares make it minimal. QG_N forces b_(i+1,1)>=q_i^2>=b_(i,1)^2, with b_(i,1)>=2. Minimum digits tend to infinity; finite prefixes do not alter this. CS2015 p.6 and (44) p.62; author revision (6.8) p.62.
- B6 (2): Conjugation preserves the real straightened map and reverses its nontrivial dividing rotation, so E1 forces q=2. E3 gives relative period p=q+b with a uniform bound on b. Finite prefixes preserve boundedness. EE §4.0.1 p.9.
- B7 (2): KKL p. 3 and §3.1 p. 19 define tails Phi_n ... Phi_q(M), n>=q>=2, in the 1/(n+1) limb; these nonreal primary limbs exclude real infinitely renormalizable points by disjoint conjugate wakes. Only the finitely many isolated types of the family can be real. Their periods are bounded; finite prefixes preserve boundedness.

Rules are disjoint by priority B1 through B7 as assigned in the per-cell file; other contradictions can also hold. The verifier checks each assigned rule's required atlas axes (rather than merely accepting any B label).

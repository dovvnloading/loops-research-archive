## Exact estimate and dependency direction

Write B_i for the first-level little bushes, K_0 for the distinguished **full** little filled Julia set, Y = K_0 union the other B_i (the paper's Upsilon), and S_k = U^k minus Y. In this section K is the extremal width of curves from K_0 to the remaining boundary of S_0, not W_bullet(G_m). By the bush construction B_0 is contained in K_0, and all these sets lie inside the parent bush B_F. The 7.1 assumption (7.1) is W_bullet(G_m) <= K_p for m sufficiently large, with G_m = (R^{n bullet})^m(F). Eq. (7.2) asks for K <= K_2(K_p,pbar) uniformly over these maps. It is a bound on the *inhomogeneous* peripheral annulus, not the ordinary homogeneous bush-to-bush annulus.

The chain of this proof is Theorem 7.1 -> (7.2) -> unbalanced renormalization (§3.4.5, p. 26) -> Lemma 3.7 + Remark 3.8, p. 27 -> ordinary beau ql bounds (7.2 theorem) -> A and the cited rigidity/MLC consequences in B (p. 3). Without a uniform replacement for (7.2) the printed route from pseudo-ql bush bounds to ordinary bounds is not complete. Conversely, demonstrating this bridge would not by itself certify its upstream theorem or the downstream rigidity theory.

### What survives in (7.3)

For fixed k use the proper identifications from §3.4.2(III), §3.4.6/Lemma 3.6 (pp. 24,26). A subset of an iota-proper bush is iota-proper if its unique lift is the identified subset; this follows by taking preimages and restricting the homeomorphism on B_F. Consequently the marked complement map S_k -> S_0 is holomorphic, carrying the peripheral loop at K_0 once around its counterpart. Assuming the source's proper identifications at each iterate, Schwarz–Pick on this loop gives its source peripheral length >= target peripheral length. Width of the cyclic covering annulus (3.6), p. 20, is proportional to that length. The path-family width K is defined by (3.1), p. 18, not by (3.6). Each path from the distinguished boundary to another boundary lifts from the distinguished boundary of its cyclic cover to the opposite ideal boundary. The extremal-width mapping inequality (Kahn v3, Lemma 9.1, p. 53) therefore gives K <= the cyclic-annulus width at level zero. Hence the local boundary width at k is >= K, conditional on the proper degree-one peripheral identification. This is a one-way comparison; it does not prove the reverse bound.

Theorem 3.1 (p. 22) gives local WAD weight >= K-C_top. Every canonical vertical arc crosses from B_F to the outer boundary, so monotonicity under overflow and disjoint supports imply W(A_ver^k) <= W(U^k minus B_F) <= 2^k K_p. (This is only an upper bound; an outer-to-itself arc is not counted in the vertical or horizontal subdiagram.) Therefore, counting self-arcs with multiplicity two in the local WAD,

    W(A_{K_0,hor}^k) >= K - C_top - 2^k K_p,
    W(A_hor^k) >= (K - C_top - 2^k K_p)/2.

The local weight convention matters even if later alignment excludes essential self-arcs. The same bounded topology gives a bounded number N of horizontal arcs (cf. (4.3)); some K_0-incident edge has width at least (K-C_top-2^k K_p)/(2N). Removing bounded per-arc threshold buffers does not destroy this lower bound asymptotically for bounded k. This only supplies a **wide incident edge** (possibly a self-arc before alignment), not the entire (7.3) equality or the special periodic rectangle with the same endpoint. We do not use an equality for the total horizontal weight.

### Exact missing hypothesis, and hostile self-check

Required for the next printed line: for arbitrary small fixed delta, uniform c>0 (depending at most on pbar and delta) and sufficiently large K, find R in S_0 from K_0 to B_a, a != 0, of width >= cK, representing a genuine periodic arc relative to Y, and a single sublamination G of width >= (1-delta)W(R) for **every** s=1,...,10 pbar (integer s) with:
1. its restriction under iota^{sp_1} remains horizontal with the two same marked endpoints;
2. each restricted curve contains a crossing of the periodic lift R^{(s)} under f^{sp_1}, with end pieces trivial relative to f^{-sp_1}(Y) as in Definition 3.12, p. 30.

A choice for each s separately without a quantitative intersection/retention bound is not enough. Stable support, and most curves remaining horizontal, do not directly establish (2). The homogeneous proof of §4.3 uses an injective correspondence on the periodic flower in (2.13), and the duplicate-lift inequality from Lemma 2.4, to control lost weight; it also pushes its rectangle from U^t back to V in §4.3.1 using Lemma 3.11 and buffer estimates. The analogues need to be checked with K_0 fixed, not cyclically replaced by the other B_i. In particular:
- the preimage of the distinguished full K_0 under f^{p_1} need not be just its central component; B_i are permuted by f but Y is not; restricting to f^{p_1} is sensible but not by itself a proof of the (2.13) bijection on the needed subset;
- the global maximal-weight choice in §4.3 need not have the distinguished endpoint. One needs either a maximality argument closed under duplicate-lift pushforward within that incident set, or another way of propagating width to a periodic incident edge;
- lifting the wide *local* weight from the bound above does not give the simultaneous univalent overflow and triviality of end pieces, as required by (3.28)–(3.30) and Def. 3.12;
- Remark 6.3 is relevant to **after** the rectangle is found. It does not state the required weighted extraction before Theorem 6.1.

Hostile check against the audit: it is possible that the source's §4.3 argument has a straightforward extension by applying a return map to a superattracting model and following the marked preimages. No impossibility of such an extension is proved. In particular, using a local rather than global maximum may work if its pushforward arc remains incident or there is a bounded-degree weight-comparison chain; this needs a proof, not an abstract counterexample to the actual map. Because the actual native topological and conformal constraints could enforce precisely the missing statements, the verdict is OPEN, not GAP. The finite incidence example in verify.py (a chain with K_0--B_1 weight K and B_1--B_2 weight 2K) checks only that the scalar local/global implication fails for nonnegative weighted diagrams, not that such a WAD occurs dynamically.

### Check of the pull-off and final bound (conditional)

Theorem 6.1 p. 36 assumes a wide delta-almost periodic rectangle between neighboring bushes, uses a spiral count around the unchanged B_a, and in the nonspiraling case invokes Lemma 2.1 (pp. 12–13) to locate two preperiodic barriers. The spiral-degree inequality (p. 37) uses the degree 2^s around B_a; this endpoint is unchanged in the proposed replacement. Remark 6.3 specifically permits replacing the *other* endpoint by a disjoint forward invariant set (here for the return). The two level-two bushes and separating barriers in Lemma 2.1 must still give the needed separation in the complement of enlarged K_0. Their placement is stated for the original satellite flower, and the quantitative retained lamination must already satisfy the modified (I)/(II). I did not independently verify this separation extension. Thus the remark is not ignored, but treated as a stated extension whose quantitative use is conditional.

The numerical part of (6.3) is correct: w=W(R)>0 and u=W(G_a)>0, with disjoint successive overflow, W(G^q)>=(1-delta)w and W(R^{(q)})=w. The series law gives (1-delta)w <= (1/w+1/u)^(-1), equivalently u >= ((1-delta)/delta)w. As delta -> 0 the factor diverges. This proves only that algebra, conditional on the actual geometric hypotheses (the disjoint successive subfamilies and preserved width). If curves cross both level-two bushes, p. 37 uses Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 3.10 for the two alternatives; if not, the Wave Lemma 5.1 handles the wave; spiral case p. 38 uses the same series law. I do not import a blanket univalence or degree hypothesis for a changed geometry, nor independently re-audit Lemma 5.2 here.

Indexing hostile check: n in Theorem 7.1 can be >2; bounds at multiples of n alone do not bound levels nm+2. The sup-chain inclusion (3.17) p. 26 gives a *larger* direct domain than the iterated one, so width of the direct map is <= the composed map's width (3.21), p. 27; the reverse inequality would be unjustified. But apply Theorem 7.1 also to the finitely many direct starting maps R^{r bullet}F, r=0,...,n-1, with the same pbar-dependent n and K_p. For each r the corresponding block-iterated map embeds by (3.17) into the direct map R^{(nm+r) bullet}F, with the same central bush; annulus monotonicity gives the upper bound on the latter's W_bullet. Taking the maximum of finitely many starting thresholds, all sufficiently deep *direct* maps have the bound. Here we use the geometric inclusion with matched bushes, not the ambiguous un-subscripted W in (3.21). Repeat the proposed (7.2) argument with a direct G=R^{k bullet}F, using the modified (6.2) (rather than (6.1)) to obtain a large width for R^{(k+2) bullet}F or G, contradicting these direct bounds. This repair depends on the missing marked rectangle and pull-off in the form (6.2). It also explains how to obtain all depths, not merely the sparse sequence. It is not a proof of (7.2).

### Downstream citation check

Unbalanced renormalization §3.4.5 p. 26 uses properness of the full little Julia set; the paper uses (7.2) to bound the cyclic covering annulus (3.6), p. 20. The bound on the path family (3.1) is not literally the definition of the cyclic width; the required upper comparison in the marked setting is treated as part of the conditional downstream interface, not verified by the one-way inequality above.

# DIRECT_DECORATION_END_PRIME_END_COFINALITY — singleton ends, but not singleton prime ends

**Date:** 2026-11-26. **Base:** main at 6ba50a4b452f7703c17200dd3b3bab4b117ee413.
**Ledger ID:** DIRECT_DECORATION_END_PRIME_END_COFINALITY (open). **Result:** (c), an actual quadratic counterexample to the single-prime-end/cofinal-chain clause, with a general corrected theorem on the source-defined direct-decoration packages. No claim about all infinitely renormalizable quadratics or MLC.

## 1. Claim, scope, and dependency target

The ledger asks: “For every direct-decoration infinite address, construct the actual multisector Bottcher angular footprint with compatible inverse lifts and prove that the exterior traces of the nested puzzle cylinders form a fundamental chain cofinal at one external prime end, including endpoint addresses, so that its impression is exactly the nested end fiber. Protection against collars, obstacles, and enlargement is separate.”

The obstruction is to the **whole exterior trace** being cofinal at one ideal point, not to existence of a prime end with the required impression. Those are different assertions even for locally connected Julia sets. The proof below actually establishes the latter in the stated source class, and proves uniform shrinking of the physical cylinders at each fixed parameter, without assuming global local connectivity.

Use the native hypotheses of DIRECT_DECORATION_PULLBACK_COMPACTIFICATION, not arbitrary planar disk models:

* A quadratic f(z)=z²+c with connected K(f)=J(f), an actual KL direct noncentral decoration, and its dividing fixed point alpha of rotation number p/q, q>1.
* The KL escape time n is finite and the separator P=Y^((n−1)q+1) is the actual closed ray/equipotential puzzle piece. Its two selected disjoint closed puzzle pullbacks Q_L,Q_R inside P have inverse homeomorphisms phi_L,phi_R:P→Q_L,Q_R of g=f^q. Their attachment vertices are alpha and alpha'=-alpha; g(alpha)=g(alpha')=alpha. In particular phi_L(alpha)=alpha. These are the shallow q-time branches; no n-fold retiming is used here.
* The terminal **primitive** cycle K_i=f^i(K_0), 0≤i<r, r>1, consists of pairwise disjoint compact connected little filled Julia sets, 0∈K_0, with actual proper quadratic-like return f^r of degree two, connected filled set K_0 in the root gap. The first critical step is followed by r−1 degree-one steps. This is the cycle in FIRST_DECORATION_SCALE_ESCAPE_ATLAS and CRITICAL_BASE_CYCLE_EXCLUSION, not a putative virtually renormalized cycle.
* All P and Q_w used below are the actual KL puzzle pieces: their intersections with K(f) are connected, and their boundary in K(f) is a finite set of iterated preimages of alpha; the remainder of their boundary consists of landed rays and equipotential arcs. These are the ray/equipotential pieces from KL §2.1; they are not the arbitrary fullification/carrier enlargements of the later virtual stages.

For a word w=a_1…a_m, write phi_w=phi_{a_1}∘…∘phi_{a_m}, Q_w=phi_w(P), Q_empty=P. Write E_omega=∩_m Q_{omega|m}. This is exactly the physical end fiber, not the fiber of a freely chosen semiconjugacy. All cylinders are compact, and incomparable cylinders are disjoint. The source theorem gives E_omega⊂K(f): every g-iterate of each point in the intersection stays in P, hence its f-orbit is bounded.

The proof gives the following replacement:

**Theorem.** Under these native hypotheses, all E_omega are singleton points, and diam Q_w→0 uniformly over words of length m as m→∞ (at a fixed f). In the external Böttcher cylinder there is an exact, finite, nonempty limiting footprint A_omega of ideal angles. Every member has full prime-end impression equal to E_omega. The angle shift acts bijectively on the footprints along each specified inverse branch. The sector tracks are cofinal in the ordinary prime-end neighborhood filter when their limiting angle is interior at every finite level, and cofinal in the corresponding one-sided angular-height filter when the limiting angle is a sector endpoint. The whole trace is cofinal in the ordinary neighborhood filter of one prime end **if and only if** its limiting footprint is a singleton and the angle is interior at every finite level. In particular, eventually-left addresses have two distinct external prime ends in their footprint, so they defeat the original whole-trace clause. When n=1 there are two footprint angles for every address.

The only a priori bounds imported: **none**. Neither a uniform modulus, global Julia local connectivity, rigidity, a priori bounds for virtual scales, nor any parameter-space conclusion is assumed. In particular this does not treat satellite terminal cycles, attracting/Siegel cycles with K≠J, non-KL puzzle systems, or formal branches without a source-realized primitive terminal cycle. The proof remains at the first direct-decoration scale actually visible in the original f-plane.

## 2. Actual inverse branches shrink, without global LC

Put C=∪_{i=0}^{r−1} K_i. It is compact, forward invariant, and contains the closure of the forward critical orbit. Every finite critical value of every f^N belongs to C.

**Lemma 1 (exclusion from deep cylinders).** If |v|=r then Q_v∩C=∅.

Indeed, alpha cannot lie in any K_i: it is fixed, so if it lies in one K_i then it lies in all its forward phase images, contradicting the pairwise disjointness and r>1. No f-preimage of alpha can lie in C either. Thus C avoids the K(f)-part of every puzzle boundary, and its points are in K(f), not on the exterior rays/equipotentials. A connected K_i meeting Q_v must therefore lie entirely in int Q_v.

The return f^r on each K_i is not injective. For i=0, choose a value of K_0 other than the critical value of the proper degree-two quadratic-like return; both distinct preimages in its quadratic-like domain belong to K_0 by the definition of its filled set. For i>0, the tail f^(r−i):K_i→K_0 is a homeomorphism: the terminal proper degree-two return factors into the first degree-two arrow and the remaining degree-one arrows, as in CRITICAL_BASE_CYCLE_EXCLUSION. For completeness, the intermediate maps on the successive images of the quadratic-like domain are proper onto those images (properness of the composition forces each factor to be proper), their degrees multiply to two, and the factor containing 0 has degree at least two. The tail is consequently conformal on the corresponding open domains and one-to-one on K_i, and conjugates the two phase returns. Thus the same noninjectivity holds for every i. As f^r(K_i)=K_i, f^(qr) also fails to be injective there. But f^(qr) is injective on Q_v by its construction as an r-fold g-inverse branch. Contradiction. ∎

**Lemma 2 (uniform inverse shrinking on the terminal traces).** For each |v|=r, T_v=K(f)∩Q_v is nonempty, compact, connected and full. Moreover
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\ \sup_{|u|\ge N,\ |v|=r}\operatorname{diam}\phi_u(T_v)=0.
\]

Nonemptiness follows, for instance, from the pullback of the nonempty K(f)∩P; connectedness is the native KL puzzle trace assertion. Fullness: any bounded component of the complement of T_v cannot meet the connected unbounded complement of K(f), so would be an open subset of K(f), contradicting K(f)=J(f) and the empty interior of a Julia set. The compact sets T_v and C are disjoint by Lemma 1. Choose a bounded simply connected open V_v containing T_v, disjoint from C, with T_v compactly contained in V_v. For a nondegenerate full continuum take the interior of a sufficiently close exterior equipotential (these compact Jordan disks decrease to the continuum); for a point take a small disk. This is also the full-continuum neighborhood construction in virtual_cantor_pullback_compactification.md, Lemma 13.3.

The polynomial f^(q|u|) is an unbranched finite covering over V_v, since every finite critical value lies in C. It has a single-valued conformal inverse h_{u,v} over V_v selected by phi_u at one point of T_v. This agrees with phi_u on all of T_v: the set of agreement is nonempty and relatively closed by continuity, relatively open by local inverse uniqueness at a noncritical inverse image, and T_v is connected. This checks the actual sheet; no global inverse over a multiply connected set is asserted.

There is one escape radius R for all such inverses: enlarge a polynomial escape radius to contain the finitely many bounded V_v; if f^N(z)∈V_v then |z|≤R, since outside R the modulus increases and escapes. Suppose the diameters do not tend uniformly to zero. Choose |u_j|→∞ and, passing to a subsequence, fixed v with a positive lower diameter bound. The bounded holomorphic functions h_{u_j,v} on V_v have a subsequential locally uniform limit h (Montel). If h is nonconstant, select a small closed disk B compactly contained in V_v on which h is injective and whose image contains an open disk W disjoint from h(∂B). Rouché's theorem applied to h_{u_j,v}-z on ∂B, uniformly for z in a smaller W, implies W⊂h_{u_j,v}(B) for all sufficiently large j. Thus f^(q|u_j|)(z)∈B⊂V_v for all z∈W and arbitrarily large j. No such z escapes to infinity, so W⊂K(f), contradicting its empty interior. The limit is constant, and locally uniform convergence on the compact T_v implies the diameters tend to zero. This contradicts the positive lower bound. ∎

For m≥r write omega|m=uv with |v|=r. Complete invariance of K(f) and the homeomorphic inverse imply
\[
E_\omega\subset K(f)\cap Q_{uv}=\phi_u(T_v).
\]
Lemma 2 proves E_omega={e_omega}. To pass from trace diameters to actual cylinder diameters, suppose some words w_j of lengths→∞ have diam Q_{w_j}≥ε>0. Successively choose common prefixes and a diagonal subsequence, giving an infinite omega such that eventually w_j extends each fixed omega|k. For every k, diam Q_{omega|k}≥ε; nested nonempty compact sets in a common compact P have Hausdorff limit their intersection, so diam E_omega≥ε. Contradiction. This gives uniform shrinking of the *physical* cylinders, not only their angular lengths. Distinct infinite words have disjoint fibers, hence omega↦e_omega is a homeomorphism of the binary Cantor space onto its image (continuity by uniform shrinking). Also g(e_{a omega})=e_omega by the actual inverse homeomorphism. This strengthens §8 of virtual_cantor_pullback_compactification.md, which had left pointness open. The normal-family step is the same mechanism as the PCF second-stage singleton argument in open PR #155, generalized here by excluding the entire postcritical **cycle of continua** from a fixed finite depth; no finite-postcritical-set assumption is needed.

## 3. The actual angular lift, with all sectors retained

Normalize the exterior Böttcher coordinate by infinity, and write its inverse as Psi(theta,h), theta∈R/Z, h>0, so that
\[
f(\Psi(\theta,h))=\Psi(2\theta,2h).
\]
Since K(f) is connected this is a conformal exterior parametrization. Write d=2^q and D(theta)=d theta mod 1. The prime-end circle is exactly R/Z in this chart; its points are not identified by equality of planar impressions. Use a height H for the equipotential of P. The puzzle definition, cutting an equipotential disk along finitely many landed rays, gives exactly
\[
Q_w\setminus K(f)=\{\Psi(\theta,h):\theta\in A_w,\ 0<h\le H/d^{|w|}\}.
\]
Here A_w is the union of closed angular arcs of the equipotential boundary of Q_w, endpoints included. The rays at their endpoints are actual boundary rays. One can obtain the arcs by intersecting the closed puzzle piece with any one equipotential of positive height below its outer equipotential; membership along a radial segment is unchanged because the only lateral cuts are the designated rays. The arcs of A_empty=P's footprint are disjoint, nondegenerate, and finite in number s (one per sector, s=2^n for the source separator; the bound below needs only finiteness).

For each a∈{L,R}, g:Q_a→P is a homeomorphism. Complete invariance and the Böttcher identity show that D:A_a→A_empty is a bijection: at a common small positive height, two equal angular images would give equal physical images under g, and every exterior point of P has its inverse in Q_a outside K(f). Compactness implies a homeomorphism of these angular sets. In particular each parent-sector component J_j⊂A_empty has exactly one inverse arc in A_a. Fix real lifts of J_j and that inverse arc. The actual inverse is
\[
\lambda_{a,j}(t)=(t+k_{a,j})/d,\qquad k_{a,j}\in\mathbb Z
\]
on that sector, modulo one, with k_{a,j} read from the **actual** inverse arc (e.g. k_{a,j}=d times its chosen lift of the midpoint minus the parent midpoint). The integer is constant on the connected arc by continuity. This construction explicitly allows different integers on different components; no common affine lift is assumed. Composing these native inverse maps on their appropriate components gives A_w and is the angular part of phi_w, by the uniqueness of the inverse on Q_w, with compatible integer offsets at every concatenation. In particular each A_w has exactly s arcs; the length of each is one of the original sector lengths divided by d^|w|. For all w,a, A_wa⊂A_w. Incomparable angular sets are disjoint, since otherwise a point at sufficiently small positive height would belong to both physical cylinders.

For an infinite address define A_omega=∩_m A_{omega|m}. This is nonempty compact by nestedness; it has at most s points: s+1 distinct points have a strictly positive minimal separation, and for sufficiently large m each of the s arcs is shorter than that separation. More precisely it consists of the limits of the surviving nested sector tracks. A track is a sequence of components J_m of A_{omega|m} with J_{m+1}⊂J_m; each limiting angle has one unique such track since components are disjoint. The choice of component is genuine additional boundary information. For each a, the homeomorphism D:A_a→A_empty restricts to bijections A_{aw}→A_w and therefore to A_{a omega}→A_omega. This proves exact angular compatibility with the one-step physical shift, including inverse sheets.

## 4. Impressions and cofinality, including endpoints

For each angle theta, write its two one-sided impressions in the Böttcher chart as
\[
I^\pm(\theta)=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}
 \overline{\{\Psi(\theta+t,h):0<\pm t<\epsilon,\ 0<h<\epsilon\}}.
\]
Closures are in the physical plane; for small epsilon all images are bounded. Nested compact connected closures give nonempty connected impressions. The full prime-end impression I(theta) is I^+(theta)∪I^−(theta). Indeed the full impression uses both angular sides and the radial line; every fixed positive-height point on the radial line is approximated from either side by continuity of Psi. A sequence defining a full impression has a subsequence on one side or on the radial line, proving the asserted equality. These are prime-end impressions of the *global exterior*, not those of an independently uniformized virtual hull.

For theta∈A_omega at least one sign is permitted at every level: a sign is permitted if a positive one-sided angular interval at theta belongs to A_{omega|m}. At each level the component containing theta has positive length, so the set of permitted signs is nonempty; the sets are decreasing with m. For a sign permitted at all levels, sufficiently small one-sided angular-height wedges lie in each Q_{omega|m}; hence I^sign(theta)⊂E_omega. By §2 this is exactly {e_omega}. If theta is interior at all levels both signs are permitted, proving I(theta)=E_omega.

We now treat actual endpoint angles; it is *not* enough to take angular limits and presume the missing side has the same impression. The two bounding rays of the depth-zero critical sector land at alpha, and these are precisely the two alpha-rays in the boundary of P. The source rotation portrait consists of q rays at alpha in a cyclic orbit, permuted by f with rotation p/q; f^q fixes each angle. Since phi_L fixes alpha and extends continuously on P, the inverse of each of these two rays in Q_L lands at alpha. Its angle must be the same one: f^q fixes each of the q angles at alpha, and there is only one member of this portrait mapping to the specified angle. Thus those two boundary angles remain in A_{L^m} for all m, and their permitted signs are opposite (the inside of the critical sector is clockwise from one ray and counterclockwise from the other). Section 2 gives E_{L∞}={alpha}. Consequently each of these two rays has its P-side impression {alpha}.

For either sign and any k≥0 there is exact one-sided impression covariance
\[
 f^k(I^\pm(\theta))=I^\pm(2^k\theta).
\]
On small unwrapped angular-height rectangles, (theta+t,h)↦(2^k theta+2^k t,2^k h) bijects the two wedges and preserves the sign. Use the Böttcher identity, continuity on compact closures, and commutation of a continuous map with a decreasing intersection of nonempty compact sets to get equality. Since f cyclically permutes the q alpha-ray angles, the two opposite-sign singleton impressions just proved propagate, separately for each sign, to **all** q angles at alpha. In particular the missing sides at the two P-boundary rays are singleton impressions too.

Now let theta be any sector endpoint at any finite cylinder depth. By the actual puzzle boundary definition its ray lands at a preimage v of alpha, and its angle maps to an alpha-ray angle under some finite f-iterate. By covariance, each one-sided impression is a connected nonempty subset of the finite preimage of {alpha} under that iterate; it is a singleton. Both sides contain v by radial continuity and landing of the actual boundary ray, so both equal {v}. For theta in A_omega at least one sign has already been shown to equal E_omega, and hence v=e_omega. Thus all theta∈A_omega, including every endpoint, have full impression exactly E_omega. No local-connectivity theorem for the entire Julia set was invoked.

**Precise cofinality.** Work in the external prime-end compactification, using coordinates (theta,h) extending continuously as prime-end coordinates to h=0. At an angle interior to J_m for all m, the rectangles J_m×(0,H/d^m] eventually lie in every prime-end angular-height neighborhood, since diam J_m→0 and heights tend to zero. Each contains a smaller full angular-height neighborhood intersected with h>0. Thus this single sector track has the full neighborhood filter at theta (ordinary crosscut neighborhoods can be chosen cofinally within it in the cylinder). If theta is an endpoint at any level, all subsequent J_m contain an interval on the same single side, so their rectangles instead have the one-sided filter, including their boundary radial segment. They do not contain any full neighborhood at theta. This does not rule out an ordinary prime-end defining chain in the *ambient exterior* approaching theta, whose impression was just determined; it rules out identifying the one-sided puzzle track itself with that full neighborhood filter.

The whole exterior trace has angular cluster set exactly A_omega: each theta in it has arbitrarily small positive height in every cylinder, and any other angular cluster limit belongs to all closed A_{omega|m}. Therefore the whole trace can lie eventually in every full neighborhood of one prime end only when A_omega is a singleton. If singleton, compact nested A sets converge to it in Hausdorff distance. The reverse containment of a neighborhood at every finite level holds precisely when theta is interior at every level, by the angular-height formula. This proves the stated if-and-only-if; it separates a fundamental-chain claim from the equality of impressions.

There is also an exact endpoint classification. For L∞ the two surviving angles just exhibited are the only ones: every angle in A_{L∞} has impression {alpha}, and must therefore be one of the q actual alpha-ray angles; P lies in the depth-zero critical sector and contains only its two consecutive alpha-ray angles. By the angular shift bijection, any address eventually L∞ has exactly two angles. Both are boundary rays at some depth, as their actual inverse rays under the finite prefix land at the corresponding preimage vertex. Conversely if an angle of A_omega is a finite-level sector endpoint then e_omega is its landed puzzle vertex. Under sufficiently many g-iterates it maps to alpha (all these vertices are f-preimages of alpha, which is fixed); the shifted address is L∞, since alpha belongs to Q_L and the closed sibling Q_R is disjoint from it. Thus the eventually-L addresses are precisely the endpoint cases; all other tracks are interior at every level.

For n=1 the Jordan separator P has two vertices alpha and alpha', with two exterior sectors between the successive boundary ray pairs. Each of the two shallow pieces contains both boundary rays at its attachment vertex, by the inverse-homeomorphism argument: at alpha the q-ray portrait is fixed by g, and at alpha' the actual inverse under g of that portrait is bijective on the attachment rays. The two arcs of each A_a therefore occupy different parent sectors, one touching each of the two corresponding endpoints. Hence the action from target sector to parent sector is a permutation, for each a. Every finite word has one nondegenerate arc in each original sector, and nestedness preserves a track in each. Their two limits are distinct because the two original closed sectors are disjoint. This last conclusion can alternatively be read directly from the explicit real q=2 example in §5, which needs no general endpoint counting.

## 5. Exact actual quadratic counterexample and complete two-sector formula

Use the already certified nonattracting tip in period27_misiurewicz_tip_ray_access_20261106.md, Theorem A. Put P_0(c)=0 and P_{j+1}(c)=P_j(c)^2+c. Let c_tip be the unique real root of P_18(c)+P_9(c)=0 in the interval
\[
\left(\frac{-607918036626757496169199575185708826140}{2^{128}},
\frac{-607918036626757496169199575185708826139}{2^{128}}\right).
\]
The accompanying integer interval checker verifies opposite signs, nonvanishing derivative on the bracket, the pairwise distinct first 19 points x_0,…,x_18 of the critical orbit, and multiplier 3<(f^9)'(x_10)<4. Since x_18=-x_9, x_19=x_10; distinctness proves exact preperiod 10 and exact period 9. In particular this is an actual Misiurewicz quadratic and K=J (the classical postcritically finite repelling-orbit criterion). This is **not** the superattracting period-27 center. Use the **first ambient** decoration, not the later internal G=f^3 puzzle.

From kl_tip_primitive_puzzle_and_localization_20261106.md §2, the actual real dividing fixed point is alpha=(1−sqrt(1−4c_tip))/2, its two rays have angles 1/3 and 2/3, alpha'=-alpha has rays 1/6 and 5/6. The checker verifies 7/8<alpha'<1<x_2<beta, where beta is the other fixed point. Thus the first even iterate of 0 escapes P=Y^1 into the offcritical depth-one piece; the actual source q=2, n=1. The terminal primitive period-three cycle K_0=K_3 of nonattracting_tip_complex_two_scale_20261106.md is the compact root in the escape gap, by tip_kl_terminal_first_child_20261106.md §§1,4–6 (actual first child and closed separator branches). The cycle is pairwise disjoint, with the f³ return of degree two, not an identification of a center with a tip. These are previously supplied physical realizations; the checker here does not purport to re-prove their complex domain bounds. KL Lemma 2.1 at n=1 gives exactly the disjoint closed depth-three separator branches. The root-gap and homeomorphism hypotheses of §1 are thus met.

For clarity the actual ray/equipotential sectors can be read explicitly from the ambient puzzle. The root P has the angular sectors
\[
J_+=[1/6,1/3],\qquad J_-=[2/3,5/6].
\]
The two new real vertices ±t of the critical depth-three piece satisfy f²(±t)=alpha', with t∈(5/16,7/16). Here the intermediate real inverse u=-sqrt(alpha'−c_tip), t=sqrt(u−c_tip). The landed ray pairs are (7/24,17/24) at −t and (5/24,19/24) at t, as established in tip_puzzle_ray_tree_20261106.md §§1–3. Pulling back the two parent sectors by f² and selecting the actual closed pieces at alpha and alpha' gives the following exact table (upper/lower mean the angular sectors, not independent model ports):

| branch | source sector arc in Q_a | D=4 theta mod 1 image | lift integer k in chosen [0,1] representatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| L | [7/24,1/3] | J_+ | 1 |
| L | [2/3,17/24] | J_- | 2 |

The ray pairs specify the **physical** partition: the middle child has the two boundary pairs at −t,t, and the two side children have the attachment pair at alpha or alpha' and the adjacent new pair, respectively. This is the original KL q=2,n=1 puzzle, not an arbitrary choice of a branch of multiplication by four. The displayed integers explicitly demonstrate the failure of a common affine lift even at one step. The checker tests the four exact angular identities.

On the upper sector set U=6theta−1∈[0,1]; reflection theta↦1−theta identifies the lower sector with the same interval. Then the upper interval of the inverse branch is obtained by the maps
\[
T_L(U)=(U+3)/4,\qquad T_R(U)=(1-U)/4.
\]
The second formula is decreasing only because the actual holomorphic inverse swaps sectors and we use reflection to compare their upper representatives; it is not a reversal of the holomorphic Böttcher angle map. For any word w, the upper arc is (1+T_{a_1}∘…∘T_{a_m}([0,1]))/6, with the lower arc its reflection. The induction is immediate from the four sector identities. There are exactly two surviving prime ends for every infinite word, separated by circular distance at least 1/3. All those prime ends have the singleton impressions of §4.

In particular take omega=L∞. Its depth-m angular footprint is exactly
\[
[1/3-1/(6\,4^m),1/3]\ \cup\ [2/3,2/3+1/(6\,4^m)].
\]
Its planar end fiber is {alpha}, and its angular cluster set is {1/3,2/3}. These are two different points of the exterior prime-end circle, both with impression {alpha}. The traces cannot eventually lie in a neighborhood of either ideal point excluding the other, let alone constitute a fundamental chain cofinal there. In fact both tracks are one-sided here. This is an exact counterexample to the whole-trace cofinality clause as stated, even with local connectivity available at the example. Equality of impressions, even when singleton, does not identify distinct prime ends. If “at one prime end” were intended to mean merely existence of a selected prime end with the correct impression, then that weaker assertion, not the full-trace assertion, is proved by §4 in the stated native class.

## 1. Same-time, same-degree-word rigidity in arbitrary degree

Keep f(z)=z²+c with connected empty-interior K(f)=J(f)=X. A saturated m-cycle H consists of continua H_j (indices modulo m), with H_j a component of f⁻¹(H_(j+1)); assume 0∈H_0. The critical-source set
    D(H) = { j modulo m : 0∈H_j }
is exactly the set of indices with step degree two: by saturation 0∈H_j iff c∈H_(j+1). Each other step has degree one. The return P=f^m on H_0 has degree 2^|D(H)| counted by multiplicities.

**Lemma 1.** For fixed f, m and prescribed nonempty critical-source set D containing 0, there is at most one saturated cycle realizing D. If it exists, H_0 is the filled set of an actual polynomial-like restriction of f^m of degree 2^|D|.

The argument of CYCLIC_RIGIDITY.md §§2–3 does not require the degree to equal two. Nondegeneracy follows from 0,P(0)∈H_0, which are distinct. The component-tree argument identifies H_0 as a component of P⁻¹(H_0), and BOPT Theorem B applies (no attracting or parabolic cycles). The degree of its polynomial-like restriction is the preimage count above. Two candidates with the same D both contain the critical forward orbit at its indexed stages. Their coordinatewise intersection is nonempty and is a saturated cycle by the intersection lemma of PERIOD_INTERSECTION.md §1. It has the same D and hence the same degree. Its phase zero is completely invariant relative to either polynomial-like restriction because its preimage count is already the full degree. Connected straightening to a polynomial of degree 2^|D|≥2, preservation of empty interior, and Montel's omitted-three-points criterion prove equality exactly as in CYCLIC_RIGIDITY.md §3. This proves the lemma.

In particular if two saturated m-cycles H,F have critical phase zero and D(F)⊂D(H), their intersections have word D(F), hence by Lemma 1 they equal F. Thus
    F_j⊂H_j for every j  iff  D(F)⊂D(H).                (1)
The forward implication is immediate from critical membership. The reverse is the preceding intersection argument. This is a lattice-type inclusion law at a fixed physical time, not a claim that every subset D is realizable. The same argument shows that intersections of realizable critical words containing phase zero are realizable and their carriers are the corresponding physical intersections.

## 2. General forcing lemma: clocks generate critical encounters

Let I⊂X be any continuum containing a critical periodic continuum C with 0∈C and f^r(C)=C for some integer r≥1 (not necessarily a least period; no degree condition on C is needed for this lemma). Suppose for positive integers s_1,...,s_k the actual set containments
    f^(s_i)(I) ⊃ I
hold. Let H be any saturated m-cycle with I⊂H_0. Define
    d = gcd(m,r,s_1,...,s_k),
where an empty list of s_i is permitted.

**Theorem 2.** Every residue divisible by d modulo m is a critical source of H. Consequently
    |D(H)| ≥ m/d,      deg(f^m|H_0) ≥ 2^(m/d).         (2)

**Proof.** Since f preserves inclusions, repeated compositions of the covering containments show that f^S(I)⊃I for every nonnegative integer combination S of the s_i. Since C⊂I and f^r(C)=C, we have C⊂f^(S+tr)(H_0)=H_((S+tr) mod m) for all t≥0. In particular all those phases contain 0. In the finite group Z/mZ, the nonnegative combinations of given generators already form their generated subgroup: the inverse of any element is a nonnegative multiple of it by finite order. The subgroup generated by r,s_1,...,s_k is exactly the multiples of d modulo m. It has m/d elements. Saturation counts one quadratic step at each, yielding (2). QED.

If a genuine one-quadratic d-cycle K_d exists and contains I in phase zero, its repeated phase diagram to length m (d divides m) attains the lower bound. Its word is exactly the multiples of d. By (1), every candidate H contains this repeated cycle, and by Lemma 1 it is the unique minimizing carrier: equality in (2) forces exactly that same critical word. No pointwise lower bound on moduli or area is used.

## 3. Sharp all-time physical result at the actual 3/9 tip

Use precisely the actual interval I and terminal C=K9 in PERIOD_INTERSECTION.md §3, imported from the source-locked cell-tree and two-scale audits. In addition to the period-nine invariance f⁹(K9)=K9, the actual source decoration has an inverse branch φ_+ of f⁶ on I with φ_+(I)⊂I, as established in the cell-tree audit §4.1 (it in fact provides both φ_+,φ_-). Therefore
    f⁶(I) ⊃ f⁶(φ_+(I)) = I.                            (3)
This is a physical set containment, not the assertion that f⁶(I)=I or that f⁶ is a proper quadratic-like return on I. We use only the inclusion.

Theorem 2 with r=9,s_1=6 gives d=gcd(m,9,6)=gcd(m,3), hence the lower bound
    deg(f^m|H_0) ≥ 2^(m/gcd(m,3)).                     (4)

For every m this bound is attained: if 3 divides m, repeat the actual K3 cycle m/3 times, which contains I; otherwise repeat the whole J=K(f) cycle m times. Both are saturated actual phase diagrams. The second is the ordinary full polynomial return f^m on J (it also has a polynomial-like restriction to a sufficiently large equipotential disk pair). Thus the minimum is exact for all m, and its unique minimizing carrier is K3 when 3 divides m and X otherwise. More strongly every candidate contains that minimum carrier, by (1). If 3 does not divide m the forced word contains all phases, so no alternative carrier exists.

The least-root obstruction is not restricted to an artificial saturated-cycle hypothesis. If P=f^m has a polynomial-like restriction of any degree with connected filled set Y containing the entire native decoration inventory U (including its endpoints), then Y⊂X and [U]_X=I⊂Y. Its filled-set identity makes Y a global inverse component and its successive forward images are saturated one-step phases, by CYCLIC_RIGIDITY.md §2's component-tree proof. Since 0∈I, the degree of this restriction is exactly the product of its one-step degrees. Conversely every saturated candidate is an actual polynomial-like filled set by Lemma 1. Hence (4) and the sharp attainment apply directly to these analytic restrictions.

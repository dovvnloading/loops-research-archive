# H1_CELL_TREE_EXTRACTION — least Julia carrier and exact finite obstruction closure

**Target:** `H1_CELL_TREE_EXTRACTION`, under `H1_MARKED_CONSTRUCTION`; virtual_hull_realization.md §6.1(1–7) and saturated_virtual_seed_forest.md §8, VSC1–VSC5.

**Current status:** OPEN. Partial written proof on actual quadratic Julia sets; no promotion. VSC6 is not used.

**Claim:** For a quadratic polynomial with connected (K(f)=J(f)), finite prescribed connectedness packages with nondegenerate root hull, critical target requirements and finitely many compact connected obstacle records admit a least Julia-set saturated carrier whenever a carrier with the specified routing, location, degree and absorb-or-separate conditions exists; that carrier is obtained by the bottom-up hull recursion and finite forced-absorption algorithm below.

## 1. Hypotheses and minimal-hull lemma

Let f(z)=z²+c have connected filled Julia set J=K(f) with empty interior. All hulls below are **inside J**, not arbitrary planar connected hulls. A subcontinuum of J is full: its bounded complementary components, if any, must be contained in J because the connected unbounded complement of J lies in the unbounded complementary component of the subcontinuum. This contradicts empty interior. In particular the lollipop warning in virtual_hull_realization.md §6.1 cannot occur for these carriers.

Any two subcontinua of J with nonempty intersection have connected intersection. Indeed their union is a subcontinuum of J, hence full. The converse part of Janiszewski's plane separation theorem (for two continua with nonempty intersection) implies that a disconnected intersection would separate the plane. Alternatively, the reduced Čech Mayer–Vietoris sequence injects reduced H^0 of the intersection into H^1 of the union, which is zero by Alexander duality and fullness. Finite intersections of subcontinua all containing a common nonempty compact S are therefore connected. By compactness, their full intersection is connected. Write [S]_J for this intersection. It is a full continuum, possibly a point, and is the unique least subcontinuum of J containing S. This construction requires neither local connectivity nor an arc joining two points.

## 2. Least coherent seed forest and least saturation

Take a finite rooted tree of actual one-step f arrows u→v. An iterate label f^q is expanded into q physical steps, not identified with a single critical step. At each vertex prescribe a nonempty compact S_v⊂J consisting of the actual required atoms and critical-orbit labels (disconnected allowed); allow no invented branch data. Prescribe closed admissible localization L_v⊂J, required distinct sibling labels, and target-degree requirements. If a target must be quadratic, include c in S_v. If it must be univalent, test c∉H_v below. Contradictory degrees at a common target are impossible.

In leaf-to-root order define
```
D_v = [ S_v ∪ ⋃_{u child of v} f(D_u) ]_J.
H_r = D_r;
H_u = component of f⁻¹(H_v) containing D_u.
```
Assume D_r is nondegenerate (for example a nondegenerate root atom). The compact sets D_v are connected and f(D_u)⊂D_v. Complete invariance of the actual filled Julia set puts f⁻¹(H_v) in J. The component is unique, and saturated_virtual_seed_forest.md Theorem 3.1 gives full nondegenerate H_u, f(H_u)=H_v, and the degree-one/two alternative. Taking C_v=H_v provides connected nondegenerate forward-coherent seeds; taking D_v gives the minimal unsaturated connector except where it is a singleton. This proves VSC1 and §6.1(1) for these J-valued packages (no filling loss, no claim of width control).

**Minimality:** Any candidate saturated forest F_v⊂J with all S_v⊂F_v and f(F_u)=F_v must contain D_v, by bottom-up induction on its connectedness. Hence H_r⊂F_r. By top-down induction H_u⊂F_u: both contain D_u, and H_u is connected inside f⁻¹(F_v), of which F_u is the component containing D_u. Thus the constructed H is least coordinatewise. The same argument gives monotonicity under package enlargement.

**VSC2, exact test:** For the collision condition alone, two required siblings can be kept distinct iff the constructed components are distinct (simultaneously test all sibling pairs and the other constraints). If they coincide in H, every larger saturated forest puts the two connected anchors into the same pullback component. Conversely distinct siblings in H give disjoint same-depth carriers by induction. At a quadratic target c∈H_v there is exactly one inverse component, so no sibling separation is possible; at other targets the two components are exchanged by z↦−z. This is an exact component criterion at the least possible root, not a choice of an arbitrary large hull. For a prescribed degree-one target, failure c∈H_v is likewise irreversible; prescribed quadratic targets were inserted in the packages. If all tests pass and H_v⊂L_v, H itself is a witness for VSC1–VSC3 **with location understood as these independently given admissible Julia subsets**. If a test fails there is no witness in those localizations. Theorem 3.1 counts critical targets, not just total physical steps. The criterion does not by itself audit erased curve families when the permitted L_v is narrower than J or when protected accesses within L_v have to survive.

A further saturation identity makes the enlargement precise. For any compact nonempty S⊂J with H=[S]_J, any component W of f⁻¹(H) equals [W∩f⁻¹(S)]_J. If c∉H this follows from the inverse homeomorphism and minimality. If c∈H, W is the unique component and is symmetric under negation. Its lifted package is symmetric, so its least hull D is symmetric (negation maps J to J). f(D)⊂H is a continuum containing S, so f(D)=H. Symmetry forces D=f⁻¹(H)=W, since both square-root choices lie in D whenever one does. Repeating yields the identity for every selected iterated component. Thus saturation introduces only the full lifted package and its forced Julia connectors, not filled planar regions. It can nevertheless absorb marks; their protection is not inferred.

## 3. VSC4: exact finite forced-absorption closure

In addition to S_v, prescribe finitely many actual nonempty compact **connected** obstacle objects O at designated vertices (singletons allowed; a disconnected list is split into its declared connected constituents). Include every image f^k(O) at its ancestor k steps above, making finite lists `A_v`. These objects may initially lie outside J. The condition is that each object is wholly absorbed in H_v or wholly in a compact E_v disjoint from H_v, with f(E_u)⊂E_v∪H_v.

Start with §2. If any A∈A_v intersects H_v but is not contained in H_v, a separated solution must absorb all of A: any witness contains the current H_v and connected A cannot lie partly in each of two disjoint compact sets. Every image of an original object also lies wholly in one of the two sets by connectedness and forward closure. If A is not contained in J, this forces impossibility. Otherwise add A to S_v and recompute §2. Repeat until no straddling object remains. At most the total number of listed objects can be added; packages and H only enlarge. At any stage a required collision, forbidden critical value, or localization failure is permanent by minimality. Inductively every admissible solution contains every package added, hence contains the final H.

At termination set E_v equal to the union of the remaining disjoint objects. This is compact and disjoint from H_v. If A is in E_u, its image belongs to A_v and therefore either is absorbed or lies in E_v. Hence f(E_u)⊂E_v∪H_v. The separated forward-closed obstacle neighborhoods and complement inclusions follow from saturated_virtual_seed_forest.md Propositions 5.2–5.3. If no permanent failure occurs, this is an actual solution to VSC1–VSC4 for the stated finite obstacle problem. No hypothesis that a correct choice of connectors or obstacle split already exists is needed. The algorithm is a finite number of **set-theoretic hull** operations, not a numerical decision procedure for arbitrary compact Julia subsets.

There is also an exact non-finite-list case. For any compact family U⊂H_r of ambient Cantor atoms (including all accumulation fibers), put U_d=f⁻ᵈ(U). The finitely many full components of f⁻ᵈ(H_r) are disjoint compact sets. For a selected depth-d carrier H_v, take E_v^U=U_d∖H_v. It is the union of U_d intersected with the finitely many other components, hence compact and disjoint, regardless of the number of Cantor atoms. Every connected lifted atom is wholly in or out. Since f(U_d)⊂U_{d−1}, these remainder sets are forward closed up to absorption in the parent. They can be united with the finite E_v above. This discharges VSC4 for a whole compact ambient pullback inventory rather than merely finitely many sampled atoms. For a general compact inventory not lying in the common pullback, at a single vertex a disjoint compact remainder exists iff closure(U_d∖H_v) is disjoint from H_v; forward closure also requires the corresponding tests on all forward shadows. This is not an automatic statement about a native fjord list.

## 5. Finite cell-tree quotient of these physical saturated carriers

Let R_0={-a,x_9,x_18,a} in I. For any depth d, cut I at R_0. All physical critical values of f^d lying in I are among x_9,x_18. Each of the three open subintervals of I∖R_0 therefore has 2^d disjoint univalent inverse arcs, extending continuously to their endpoints by the local holomorphic normal form. At a point over a marked root vertex, the local form z↦z^e gives e arms over a root endpoint and 2e arms over an interior root point. The pullback is a finite embedded graph; its connected components are trees, since each lies in the empty-interior full Julia set and a circle in it would contradict fullness. The preimage vertices are the finite set (f^d)^{-1}(R_0) in each component. All leaves, branch points and critical points are among these vertices. On a component of degree 2^m there are at most 4·2^m marked vertices and at most 4·2^m−1 edges. Each physical arrow f maps edges homeomorphically to edges and vertices to vertices (an interior critical point would project to R_0).

To represent the ambient atom components by actual quotient fibers, on H_v at depth d collapse each connected component of U_d∩H_v to a point and leave all other points as singleton fibers. Connected components of a compact metric set give a closed equivalence relation: from pairs in one component converging to x,y, a Hausdorff-convergent subsequence of the compact components gives a connected subset of the same compact set containing x,y. If x=y there is no issue. Thus the quotient map π_v is continuous, monotone, onto a compact Hausdorff quotient, with upper-semicontinuous fibers. Distinct ambient atoms remain distinct because their root images are in different components of U, or they lie in distinct lifted components of the same atom. The root quotient is an interval since the singleton atoms ±a remain distinct.

Each fiber is a subtree of a finite tree (connected subsets contain the unique paths). Only finitely many collapsed fibers meet one of the finite graph vertices. Contract those subtrees first, with the ribbon order induced by the oriented plane along the boundary of a small tree neighborhood. Remaining collapsed fibers are intervals inside open edges. An interval with a closed equivalence relation by disjoint closed intervals has interval (possibly singleton) quotient: the quotient has the induced linear order, is compact and connected, second countable as a quotient of a compact metric interval by a closed equivalence, and therefore if nondegenerate is order-homeomorphic to a compact real interval. Equivalently choose a countable dense subset in the quotient order and its endpoints. The finitely many edge quotients glue with the same finite tree incidence. No new branch vertices can arise in edge interiors. The inactive fibers above K_w (w≠empty) and the unmarked end points avoid R_0's preimage; hence they lie in open edges and cannot merge marked vertices, introduce branch vertices, or merge the distinct surviving edge directions at a marked vertex. Fibers over K9 are explicitly clustered; fibers over ±a are singleton endpoints. After contraction every leaf is marked (image of an original marked vertex) and all unmarked valence-two subdivisions may be removed. The result is the minimal subtree spanning its marked cluster vertices and endpoint vertices. It has at most the preceding 4·2^m vertices and 4·2^m−1 edges. All bounds here are per actual *selected component*, not a redefinition of the original entropy/translation cell count.

The image under f of a connected component of U_d lies in a connected component of U_{d−1}, since f(U_d)⊂U_{d−1}. Hence each arrow descends continuously through the quotient. More precisely, on every uncontracted original graph edge f is a homeomorphism to the corresponding parent edge, with the component equivalence on subintervals preserved by exact preimage. The quotient arrow is cellular; in physical notation π_parent f=τ π_child. A step of degree one is a homeomorphism of carriers and preserves the cyclic order of incident surviving edge directions by holomorphic orientation (it is injective in a small neighborhood of the compact carrier by local injectivity and compactness). Quadratic steps are explicitly marked and have the branch data just described. Iteration gives the corresponding actual f^q semiconjugacies on these saturated domains. Thus §6.1(2), its component-level distinctness in (3), reduced-tree clause (5), polynomial semiconjugacy portion of (6), and the inactive-lift part of (7) hold for this physical inverse forest. This construction does not assert injectivity of arbitrary proof-used accesses on the clustered fibers. In particular two distinct accesses along the same contracted atom may not give two distinct surviving tree flags.

There is an exact growth warning already on this same polynomial. If the proof uses the two sector accesses at **each endpoint** of each root atom K_w with |w|≤s, then the root must record at least 4(2^{s+1}−1) distinct such vertex-end accesses. Indeed all K_w are nondegenerate pairwise disjoint real intervals, strictly inside I (the root gap excludes ±a, and their forward attachment images remain at a), and each distinct interior point of I has two distinct upper/lower accesses on its prime-end contour. The count refers to actual physical marked root accesses, not to a symbolic diagram. Even a version recording only the two surviving distinct edge directions at every contracted atom would require at least 2(2^{s+1}−1) flags. This is a necessary lower bound *if those ends are required*, not a proof that the native Wave proof actually uses them all. Consequently no theorem on critical-degree counts alone can give VSC5 for arbitrary such additional access inventories. Root endpoints of the intrinsic graph and endpoints exposed by a proof-used refinement must be distinguished.

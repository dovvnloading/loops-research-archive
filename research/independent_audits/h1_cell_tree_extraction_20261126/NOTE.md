# H1_CELL_TREE_EXTRACTION — least Julia carrier and exact finite obstruction closure

**Target:** `H1_CELL_TREE_EXTRACTION`, under `H1_MARKED_CONSTRUCTION`; virtual_hull_realization.md §6.1(1–7) and saturated_virtual_seed_forest.md §8, VSC1–VSC5.

**Current status:** OPEN. Partial written proof on actual quadratic Julia sets; no promotion. VSC6 is not used.

**Claim:** For a quadratic polynomial with connected (K(f)=J(f)), finite prescribed connectedness packages, critical target requirements and compact connected obstacle records admit a least Julia-set saturated carrier whenever a carrier with the specified routing, location, degree and absorb-or-separate conditions exists; that carrier is obtained by the bottom-up hull recursion and finite forced-absorption algorithm below.

## 1. Hypotheses and minimal-hull lemma

Let f(z)=z²+c have connected filled Julia set J=K(f) with empty interior. All hulls below are **inside J**, not arbitrary planar connected hulls. A subcontinuum of J is full: its bounded complementary components, if any, must be contained in J because the connected unbounded complement of J lies in the unbounded complementary component of the subcontinuum. This contradicts empty interior. In particular the lollipop warning in virtual_hull_realization.md §6.1 cannot occur for these carriers.

Any two subcontinua of J with nonempty intersection have connected intersection. Indeed their union is a subcontinuum of J, hence full. The converse part of Janiszewski's plane separation theorem (for two continua with nonempty intersection) implies that a disconnected intersection would separate the plane. Alternatively, the reduced Čech Mayer–Vietoris sequence injects reduced H^0 of the intersection into H^1 of the union, which is zero by Alexander duality and fullness. Finite intersections of subcontinua all containing a common nonempty compact S are therefore connected. By compactness, their full intersection is connected. Write [S]_J for this intersection. It is a full continuum, possibly a point, and is the unique least subcontinuum of J containing S. This construction requires neither local connectivity nor an arc joining two points.

## 2. Least coherent seed forest and least saturation

Take a finite rooted tree of actual one-step f arrows u→v. An iterate label f^q is expanded into q physical steps, not identified with a single critical step. At each vertex prescribe a nonempty compact S_v⊂J consisting of the actual required atoms and critical-orbit labels (disconnected allowed); allow no invented branch data. Prescribe closed admissible localization L_v⊂J, required distinct sibling labels, and target-degree requirements. If a target must be quadratic, include c in S_v. If it must be univalent, test c∉H_v below. Contradictory degrees at a common target are impossible.

In leaf-to-root order define
```
D_v = [ S_v ∪ ⋃_{u child of v} f(D_u) ]_J.
H_r = D_r;
H_u = component of f⁻¹(H_v) containing D_u.
```
Assume D_r is nondegenerate (for example a nondegenerate root atom). The compact sets D_v are connected and f(D_u)⊂D_v. Complete invariance of the actual filled Julia set puts f⁻¹(H_v) in J. The component is unique, and saturated_virtual_seed_forest.md Theorem 3.1 gives full nondegenerate H_u, f(H_u)=H_v, and the degree-one/two alternative. Taking C_v=H_v provides connected nondegenerate forward-coherent seeds; taking D_v gives the minimal unsaturated connector except where it is a singleton. This proves VSC1 and §6.1(1) for these J-valued packages (no filling loss, no claim of width control).

**Minimality:** Any candidate saturated forest F_v⊂J with all S_v⊂F_v and f(F_u)=F_v must contain D_v, by bottom-up induction on its connectedness. Hence H_r⊂F_r. By top-down induction H_u⊂F_u: both contain D_u, and H_u is connected inside f⁻¹(F_v), of which F_u is the component containing D_u. Thus the constructed H is least coordinatewise. The same argument gives monotonicity under package enlargement.

**VSC2, exact test:** Two required siblings can be distinct in an admissible witness iff the constructed components are distinct (simultaneously test all sibling pairs). If they coincide in H, every larger saturated forest puts the two connected anchors into the same pullback component. Conversely distinct siblings in H give disjoint same-depth carriers by induction. At a quadratic target c∈H_v there is exactly one inverse component, so no sibling separation is possible; at other targets the two components are exchanged by z↦−z. This is an exact component criterion at the least possible root, not a choice of an arbitrary large hull. For a prescribed degree-one target, failure c∈H_v is likewise irreversible; prescribed quadratic targets were inserted in the packages. If all tests pass and H_v⊂L_v, H itself is a witness for VSC1–VSC3 **with location understood as these independently given admissible Julia subsets**. If a test fails there is no witness in those localizations. Theorem 3.1 counts critical targets, not just total physical steps. The criterion does not by itself audit erased curve families when the permitted L_v is narrower than J or when protected accesses within L_v have to survive.

A further saturation identity makes the enlargement precise. For any compact nonempty S⊂J with H=[S]_J, any component W of f⁻¹(H) equals [W∩f⁻¹(S)]_J. If c∉H this follows from the inverse homeomorphism and minimality. If c∈H, W is the unique component and is symmetric under negation. Its lifted package is symmetric, so its least hull D is symmetric (negation maps J to J). f(D)⊂H is a continuum containing S, so f(D)=H. Symmetry forces D=f⁻¹(H)=W, since both square-root choices lie in D whenever one does. Repeating yields the identity for every selected iterated component. Thus saturation introduces only the full lifted package and its forced Julia connectors, not filled planar regions. It can nevertheless absorb marks; their protection is not inferred.

## 3. VSC4: exact finite forced-absorption closure

In addition to S_v, prescribe finitely many actual nonempty compact **connected** obstacle objects O at designated vertices (singletons allowed; a disconnected list is split into its declared connected constituents). Include every image f^k(O) at its ancestor k steps above, making finite lists `A_v`. These objects may initially lie outside J. The condition is that each object is wholly absorbed in H_v or wholly in a compact E_v disjoint from H_v, with f(E_u)⊂E_v∪H_v.

Start with §2. If any A∈A_v intersects H_v but is not contained in H_v, a separated solution must absorb all of A: any witness contains the current H_v and connected A cannot lie partly in each of two disjoint compact sets. Every image of an original object also lies wholly in one of the two sets by connectedness and forward closure. If A is not contained in J, this forces impossibility. Otherwise add A to S_v and recompute §2. Repeat until no straddling object remains. At most the total number of listed objects can be added; packages and H only enlarge. At any stage a required collision, forbidden critical value, or localization failure is permanent by minimality. Inductively every admissible solution contains every package added, hence contains the final H.

At termination set E_v equal to the union of the remaining disjoint objects. This is compact and disjoint from H_v. If A is in E_u, its image belongs to A_v and therefore either is absorbed or lies in E_v. Hence f(E_u)⊂E_v∪H_v. The separated forward-closed obstacle neighborhoods and complement inclusions follow from saturated_virtual_seed_forest.md Propositions 5.2–5.3. If no permanent failure occurs, this is an actual solution to VSC1–VSC4 for the stated finite obstacle problem. No hypothesis that a correct choice of connectors or obstacle split already exists is needed. The algorithm is a finite number of **set-theoretic hull** operations, not a numerical decision procedure for arbitrary compact Julia subsets.

There is also an exact non-finite-list case. For any compact family U⊂H_r of ambient Cantor atoms (including all accumulation fibers), put U_d=f⁻ᵈ(U). The finitely many full components of f⁻ᵈ(H_r) are disjoint compact sets. For a selected depth-d carrier H_v, take E_v^U=U_d∖H_v. It is the union of U_d intersected with the finitely many other components, hence compact and disjoint, regardless of the number of Cantor atoms. Every connected lifted atom is wholly in or out. Since f(U_d)⊂U_{d−1}, these remainder sets are forward closed up to absorption in the parent. They can be united with the finite E_v above. This discharges VSC4 for a whole compact ambient pullback inventory rather than merely finitely many sampled atoms. For a general compact inventory not lying in the common pullback, a disjoint compact remainder exists iff closure(U_d∖H_v) is disjoint from H_v; this is a necessary and sufficient separation test, not an automatic statement about a native fjord list.

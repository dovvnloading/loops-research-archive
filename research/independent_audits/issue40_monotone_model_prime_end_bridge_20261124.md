## 1. Monotone boundary extension theorem

Write S for the oriented sphere. Suppose M:S→S is a continuous monotone surjection (all point fibers connected), is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism in a neighborhood of infinity, and M^{-1}(∞)={∞}. Let A_0 be a nondegenerate full locally connected compact planar continuum, and A=M^{-1}(A_0). Then A is compact, connected and full: the inverse image of any connected subset under a proper monotone surjection is connected (restrict to compact connected subsets first, or use the closed-map separation argument); in particular the inverse image of the connected open complement of A_0 is connected. Write h_A,h_0 for exterior uniformizations, including infinity, and C_A,C_0 for their prime-end circles. A Jordan annular collar of either continuum has the same inner prime-end circle.

**Theorem 1.** There is a unique continuous surjective monotone degree-one map m_A:C_A→C_0 extending the interior map g=h_0^{-1} M h_A between exterior disks. If Imp_A(a) denotes the physical impression of a, and L_0 is the continuous landing map h_0|C_0, then

    M(Imp_A(a)) = {L_0(m_A(a))}.

No local connectivity of A is assumed.

Proof. The map g is proper, onto, monotone. Choose a physical null chain of crosscuts Q_n defining a, with nested shadows D_n not containing infinity. Diameters of Q_n tend to zero. After taking a subsequence their closures converge to a point x of A. M(Q_n) consequently tends uniformly to y=M(x). In exterior disk coordinates the images g(h_A^{-1}(Q_n)) lie arbitrarily near the boundary, by properness, and every cluster point there lands at y by continuity of h_0. Closures of these connected sets have connected Hausdorff subsequential limits. The set L_0^{-1}(y) is totally disconnected: any nondegenerate connected subset of the circle contains a nontrivial arc, on which h_0 would take the constant value y; boundary uniqueness for the bounded analytic function w(h_0(1/w)-y) on the disk contradicts nonconstancy. Thus, along a further subsequence, these images converge in Hausdorff distance to one point b of C_0.

For any sufficiently small round ball E centered at b in closed exterior-disk coordinates, the complement of E in the open exterior disk (including infinity) is connected. Its inverse image under g is connected, contains infinity and misses the selected crosscut once n is large. It therefore misses the entire corresponding shadow. Hence g maps that shadow into E. The nested shadows, with the crosscuts, are a neighborhood basis of a in the prime-end compactification; this proves the unique limit b of g at a, not just convergence along one curve. Extending at every a gives a continuous map on the compactification: a diagonal interior approximation proves continuity also along sequences of boundary points. Surjectivity on the circle follows by lifting sequences tending to each boundary point and using properness. A boundary fiber is connected because it is the intersection of the nested closures of inverse images of connected open collar balls about its target; properness and the established extension identify this intersection exactly with the boundary fiber. Its degree is one by the homotopy of circles to the neighborhood of infinity. Monotonicity together with positive degree gives a cyclic-order-preserving circle quotient. Finally the impression formula follows from cluster sequences in the physical exterior, continuity of M, and compactness. QED.

In the crosscut proof use genuine null chains (diameters in the physical sphere tend to zero), not merely small angular shadows. In the analytic uniqueness argument the pole at infinity is removed by multiplication by w. An arbitrary continuous map of the two physical compacta would not suffice; properness and connected point fibers on the complementary domains are essential.

**Corollary 2 (singleton-cut rigidity).** If b is an access landing at y and M^{-1}(y)={x}, then m_A^{-1}(b) consists of one prime end, whose impression is {x}. Indeed all its impressions are {x} by Theorem 1. If the connected fiber of m_A were nondegenerate, it would contain an arc on which h_A has constant boundary limit x, again contradicting analytic boundary uniqueness. Distinct accesses landing at y remain distinct. Moreover every physical access whose impression contains x is among these lifts. Thus finite collections of model cuts landing at singleton fibers lift bijectively, with their cyclic order. Neither equality of all physical/model prime ends nor global local connectivity follows.

## 2. The actual degree-two covering

Suppose B_0 is another full nondegenerate locally connected continuum, B=M^{-1}(B_0), and F is a holomorphic map on a neighborhood of B with F(B)=A and complete preimage B there. Suppose the map of the inner complementary collars is a degree-d covering, with circle map p:C_B→C_A. Suppose F_0 is its finite-to-one topological model on the compacta with M F=F_0 M. Denote the two boundary transfer maps by m_B and m_A.

**Theorem 3.** The actual circle covering descends uniquely to an orientation-preserving degree-d circle covering p_0:C_{B_0}→C_{A_0}:

    m_A p = p_0 m_B.

Proof. On any connected fiber m_B^{-1}(b), the continuous map m_A p has image contained in the model landing fiber L_{A_0}^{-1}(F_0(L_{B_0}(b))). This follows by taking cluster sequences in source collars; the physical map is defined on a neighborhood of the compacta. That landing fiber is totally disconnected by the proof of Theorem 1. Thus m_A p is constant on each fiber and factors continuously through the quotient m_B. The induced map has degree d by degrees in the displayed equality. It is nondecreasing in cyclic lifts since m_A,m_B are nondecreasing and p is a positive covering. If p_0 were constant on a nontrivial arc, L_{B_0} would map that connected arc into the finite set F_0^{-1}(y) for the target landing point y. It would be constant there, again impossible by analytic uniqueness. The monotone lift of p_0 is therefore strictly increasing, giving the asserted covering. QED.

If the model map also acts on complementary collars, this is its boundary map: lift approaching sequences through M, pass to a subsequence approaching a physical prime end, and apply the square. No quasiconformal or extremal-width equality is asserted.

For a finite target marking by singleton-fiber cuts, every inverse image of a marked cut under p_0 must itself have a singleton physical boundary fiber: that fiber is connected, and under p it maps into a singleton, hence is a singleton because p has finite fibers. This argument avoids a separate proof of preperiodicity of all pulled-back cuts. The two sets of cuts correspond exactly, and their cyclic lists and complementary marked atoms are matched. The whole and punctured circles must still be distinguished.

## 3. KKL input and saturation audit

The relevant source is Kahn–Kapiamba–Lyubich, *MLC for parabolically bounded primitive renormalization*, arXiv:2606.27272v1, §§2.1, 2.3, 2.5, 4 (Lemma 4.8), 6.1 (Propositions 6.5–6.9), Appendix A.3.1–A.3.4:
https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27272v1

Section 2.5 supplies the combinatorial Hubbard tree and positive core eigenvalue for the prime primitive case, and defines the non-LC objects via the natural projection to its rational-lamination model. For a periodically repelling quadratic polynomial, the relevant established model is Kiwi, *Real laminations and the topological dynamics of complex polynomials*, Adv. Math. 184 (2004), 207–267, Theorems 5.12–5.13, DOI 10.1016/S0001-8708(03)00144-0. The monotone plane quotient is injective outside the Julia set, is dynamically compatible, and the all-repelling case has a locally connected dendrite model. Kiwi, *Rational Rays and Critical Portraits of Complex Polynomials*, arXiv:math/9710212, Theorem 1, also gives the finiteness of impression angles at points and singleton impressions at preperiodic points under the all-repelling hypothesis. Do not assume local connectivity of the physical Julia set to use these inputs.

A preperiodic **model** point has a singleton physical fiber: its nonempty finite model external-angle class maps under doubling into the finite class of its eventual periodic image, so every angle in the class is rational. Indeed once the image point is periodic, the return doubling maps the finite class to itself and each forward angle orbit is eventually periodic; the same holds for preimages. The rational rays land at preperiodic physical points with singleton Kiwi fibers. This also explains why singleton fibers are not being assumed at arbitrary model points.

The exact bridge is for **saturated lifts** T=M^{-1}(T_0) and T'=M^{-1}(T'_0). It is not enough that a physical continuum merely projects onto T_0. Section 2.1's quadratic-like germ and straightening allow the polynomial topology to be transferred back to a neighborhood of the filled set (homeomorphism, not a conformal modulus identity). On that neighborhood, the immersion identifies the old carrier with its copy in the preimage carrier. This must be the actual carrier choice for application, not an independent replacement of the KKL wave surface.

A way to verify small Julia saturation is by the rational-ray characterization of a primitive renormalization: each little filled set is the intersection in the ambient filled set of the closed rational-ray sectors specifying its renormalization type. See Inou–Kiwi, *Combinatorics and topology of straightening maps I*, arXiv:0809.1262, and the early author preprint https://www.math.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~inou/preprint/straightening.pdf, equation (1), definition of lamination renormalizability and Theorem 4.6 (early-preprint numbering). Each such sector intersects the Julia set in a saturated set: fibers are connected, the separating rays outside the Julia set are injective under M, and their landing vertices are singleton fibers. No other fiber can cross these boundaries. Intersections preserve saturation. One still has to identify the exact canonical small Julia sets of the chosen KKL renormalization with these specified sets (matching type and period), rather than import the conclusion for an arbitrary forward-invariant subset.

In the model, finite contact sets of the little Julia vertices with incident graph edges are forward invariant as a cycle of finite sets whenever the little Julia vertex is a separated component of the preimage of the next vertex. To see this, take a short initial segment of an incident edge at a contact point. Its image is in the forward-invariant tree and is outside the next vertex for a sufficiently short segment, by the separated-component property and local injectivity off the critical point. The contact is consequently mapped to a contact. Thus these contact sites are preperiodic. The separate branch vertices and alpha are preperiodic in the source model. Under these conditions, every endpoint of the original finite edge-side and little-Julia-side marking is over a singleton fiber; Corollary 2 and Theorem 3 recover both target and covering markings. The separated-component property holds in the standard primitive quadratic-like small-Julia cycle description, but the marking identification for the exact pseudo-ql realization must be checked. No preperiodicity of an exceptional root in the interior of an atom is needed by this argument.

## 4. Attachment classification at marked cuts, including wild interior contacts

Let A_0⊂B_0 be subcontinua of a full locally connected dendrite model, with saturated lifts A⊂B. For a component X_0 of B_0\A_0, its closure in the dendrite meets A_0 at exactly one point y: uniqueness of arcs in a dendrite excludes two distinct contacts. The preimages of the components are exactly the components of B\A, by connectedness of inverse images under the proper monotone map, and projection of connected subsets. This remains true for infinitely many components; no enumeration of all wild physical impressions is used.

Use exterior coordinates for A_0. The closure Y of h_{A_0}^{-1}(X_0) in the closed exterior disk is connected and compact; its circle intersection is nonempty and lies in the landing fiber over y. It attaches to precisely one **angle** e_0. Here is a useful argument even when y has several accesses. The union of Y with the closed disk is full: it is a subset of the exteriorized full B_0 obstacle (the closed disk plus all attached material), and any bounded complementary component would be an open subset of that obstacle outside the disk. This is impossible since the attached material has empty interior and the whole obstacle is full. The intersection of the disk and Y is connected, by the reduced degree-zero/degree-one Mayer–Vietoris sequence for compacta in the plane and Alexander duality (both connected sets have connected full union, whose first Cech cohomology is zero). But the landing fiber is totally disconnected by analytic uniqueness. The intersection is therefore a singleton. This argument depends on the empty-interior dendrite hypothesis; it is not a rule for arbitrary planar subsets.

For the physical component X=M^{-1}(X_0), all limiting old prime-end coordinates of points of X approaching A belong to m_A^{-1}(e_0). This follows by applying M, using h_{A_0}^{-1} on exterior points, and using Theorem 1 at the limiting physical old angles. In particular:

* If e_0 is an old marked cut, its singleton boundary fiber gives exactly one physical old attachment access.
* If e_0 is strictly inside a model marked atom, its entire physical boundary fiber is inside the corresponding physical open atom. Nonuniqueness of physical accesses cannot move the attachment across a marked cut.
* The model and physical attached components are in bijection. At every original cut their complete contour contributions are assigned to the same marked host; for a component whose root angle is in an atom interior, the host remains that atom independently of internal access multiplicity.

There is also a direct old-access comparison on the part of C_B with model landing point in A_0. The model inclusion h_{A_0}^{-1}h_{B_0} has a unique circle limit there (connected cluster set and analytic uniqueness at the locally connected target). Every cluster angle for the physical inclusion at a corresponding prime end belongs to the m_A-fiber of that old model angle. This follows by the commutative interior coordinate identity and Theorem 1. At a marked old cut, the fiber is a singleton, so the physical inclusion has a unique limiting old access at such a source end. At an old atom interior, even if the physical map does not extend continuously to that end, all its old limiting accesses belong to one atom.

This gives an actual cut-level attachment and old-access census. It does **not** say that the physical immersion extends over attached material, nor that the entire inverse image of an unmarked angle is a single physical prime end. Nor does it identify all internal individual gate multiplicities of components sharing an old atom. The remaining proof obligation is to match the exact physical proper-path-subordination pullback (KKL A.3.2 and Prop. 6.5) with the model's complete marked contour blocks, including endpoint fibers. The source definition of pullback is not ordinary inverse image under M. In particular, merely citing the square in Theorem 3 would not prove the required identity for F_* = p iota^*.

A concrete way to finish that last comparison is to use all ambient rational rays landing at each old marked vertex. They form a finite cut system; singleton cut rigidity gives the same source and target accesses over the physical vertex, with their order. Their sectors are saturated and separate all components assigned to different marked hosts. To finish the exact identity, show the two extreme source gates of each attached block can be realized by the proper-path restrictions of A.3.2, including the case of several components in a host and the case of an endpoint birth. This is a finite collection of boundary-gate assertions, not a request to identify every unmarked wild prime end. The preceding theorem already controls their possible locations. It is important not to infer the proper-path assertions merely from connected fibers or from a geometric picture.

## 5. Conditional consequence for interval growth

Assume the remaining marked pullback identity just specified, on both open and closed marked multi-intervals. The following gives a precise checkable target for completing the repair.

Use the full model edge incidence matrix T_F, indexed with output edges in rows; let d(I) count each included original edge-side atom once (two distinct sides can contribute independently). For a proper connected image, the covering on the connected pulled-back interval is injective: a lift is an open interval of angular length at most 1/d; equality is the punctured-circle case, not a closed full circle. Every traversal of a model edge is represented by the corresponding side subarc in the covering-refined marking; the complete contour of an attached component contributes further atoms, not a subtraction. Thus the required same-source no-cancellation inequality is

    d(F_* I) >= T_F d(I).

This requires the **full traversal** correspondence, not a matrix of distinct edge names or counts with the two sides identified. A positive left Perron eigenvector gives strict weight growth whenever d(I) is nonzero. Since a proper marked interval has bounded edge-side mass, no periodic interval can have nonzero mass.

For zero-edge intervals, use the earlier atom-rigidity reduction: disjoint nonempty proper refined pullbacks imply that any periodic proper interval of constant length has all its atoms permuted along its period. A periodic edge-free atom cycle consists of whole little-Julia-side atoms. A proper single-atom step, using the source's end-proper assertion, is proper over the entire source atom. After one full return circuit, the corresponding little Julia external-coordinate map has degree two. In the actual straightened quadratic-like germ this circle map is conjugate to doubling; equivalently in conformal external coordinates the polynomial external map is doubling. If the whole-atom side charts are identified through the marked attachment comparison, an edge-free atom cycle would give a nonempty proper open interval carried bijectively onto itself by an iterate of doubling. This is impossible: an injective interval image under d-fold angular multiplication has d times the original length (or is the punctured circle at equality), so cannot have the same proper positive length. This is not a claim that arbitrary unmarked tiny subarcs escape uniformly.

With no proper atom cycles, the original growth bound follows by the strict pigeonhole count. If I has length k and the critical limb has r<=6b atoms, at most r+k-1 proper length-k intervals can meet it (count cyclic starting positions, retaining the missing cut for a punctured circle). At least 6b+k distinct visits, with no growth, force a repeated interval. For a saturated full circle, strict growth is impossible; the repaired conclusion must allow saturation, as already recorded in PR #38. For positive closed crests use the actual closed pullback and monotonicity. One cannot replace F_*(closure I) by closure(F_* I), because an endpoint can pull back to a whole attachment block.

The proof is not yet an unconditional proof of Proposition 6.9 or Lemma 6.4 for the full native setting: the remaining marked path/gate identity, source marking identification and entire little-Julia-side chart compatibility are explicitly hypotheses in this section. No inference from a finite physical slit test supplies these hypotheses. Nor would the resulting combinatorial statement alone prove width amplification or MLC.

## 6. Verification and open items

* Theorems 1–3 are independent of the KKL interval-growth claim and do not assume local connectivity of the physical carrier. The two analytic uniqueness arguments prevent a hidden collapse of a whole interval to a singleton physical landing point.
* Cut-level correspondence and attachment host invariance are stronger than counting finitely many contacts in a finite-contact model. In particular, an interior exceptional root need not be preperiodic.
* For actual KKL objects verify the saturation/straightening identification, the source proper-path extreme gates and complete blocks, and the edge-side/whole-vertex-side trace comparison. None is replaced by numerical checks.
* Beware of the orientation of T_F, open versus closed endpoints, singleton physical points with several accesses, and treating the immersion as a global map on the ideal circles.
* Theorems and source application here are written proofs/reductions for independent mathematical review; no new CI or external proof review is claimed.

# Issue #40 — source-authenticated saturated lift and local four-gate comparison

2026-11-24. This is the next analytic proof candidate following the monotone prime-end bridge, marked-gate sandwich and ambient-angle exactness notes. It removes the previous **global finite-host hypothesis**, identifies the canonical source marking from rational puzzle and vertex contact data, and separates the remaining path-semantics and wave-selection obligations. It imports the interval and component-union assertions of source Propositions 6.5–6.6; it is not an independent proof of the maximal-restriction definition for arbitrary ideal subsets.

## 1. Precisely which model and which carrier

Use [KKL v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27272v1), Theorem 2.1, §§2.3, 2.5 and 6.1: near the ambient filled set the immersion is injective, so the dynamics has a quadratic-like restriction. Transfer by the Douady–Hubbard hybrid straightening to a monic quadratic P with connected filled set K. The marked inner circles are unchanged topologically under restriction to smaller annular neighborhoods of the compact carrier; the hybrid neighborhood homeomorphism transfers prime ends, paths and relative homotopy there, not numerical conformal widths. One must still keep the original outer surface and immersion for the global path definition.

Since all periodic cycles are repelling, there are no bounded Fatou components (no wandering domains and the periodic Fatou classification). Thus K is the Julia set. Use Jan Kiwi, Real laminations and the topological dynamics of complex polynomials, Advances in Mathematics 184 (2004), 207–267, [DOI 10.1016/S0001-8708(03)00144-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0001-8708(03)00144-0), Theorem 2, Proposition 4.2, Lemma 4.4, Corollary 4.12, Theorems 5.12–5.13. The fiber-collapsing plane map H is proper monotone and is a homeomorphism off K, and H P = Q H for the degree-two model branched covering Q. Use the actual plane realization, not just an abstract tree map. The model K^m=H(K) is a nondegenerate locally connected full continuum with empty interior: any open subset of K^m would have nonempty open inverse image in K; fullness follows from the exterior homeomorphism. Hence K^m is a dendrite. Its external landing classes are finite by Corollary 4.12, so its branch valences, and those of its subdendrites, are finite.

The fiber over a preperiodic model point is a singleton. Indeed periodic model fibers contain periodic physical points by Lemma 4.4 and are trivial by Theorem 2; a preperiodic fiber is connected and lies in a finite iterated preimage of a singleton. The corresponding preperiodic physical point is also a singleton fiber directly by Theorem 2.

### Puzzle saturation

A closed rational-ray/equipotential puzzle piece intersected with K is H-saturated: its planar boundary meets K only in preperiodic landing points, whose fibers are singletons. If a connected fiber crossed from its interior to its exterior, it would meet that boundary, contradicting nonsingleton crossing. Intersections of such saturated sets are saturated. This uses connectedness of fibers, not local connectivity of K.

The standard Douady–Hubbard/Yoccoz first-renormalization puzzle construction gives the critical little filled set as the intersection of the nested closed return puzzle pieces. See M. Lyubich, [Dynamics of quadratic polynomials I–II](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~mlyubich/papers/acta.pdf), Acta Math. 178 (1997), 185–297, §2.5 and §3.4 (first renormalization by the central cascade; Proposition 3.2 and the construction around p. 207). The exact imported input is that the first prime primitive little filled set is the nonescaping set of that puzzle return, including its landing boundary points, not that the puzzles shrink to a point. KKL §2.3 identifies the small filled set uniquely in the prime case; its puzzle construction in §7 is consistent with this identification. This is the standard first-renormalization construction, not an assumption about arbitrary higher-period invariant continua.

Images of saturated sets are saturated. In fact for a model point y, the preimage P^{-1}(H^{-1}(y)) is the finite disjoint union of the compact fibers over Q^{-1}(y). These are precisely its connected components. Each component maps onto H^{-1}(y): by isolation its image is relatively open in that connected target (P is open), and compactness gives relative closedness. Thus P maps every fiber onto the corresponding image fiber. It follows that the entire canonical disjoint little Julia cycle K_j=P^j(K_crit) is saturated. Its model cycle L_j=H(K_j) consists of disjoint nondegenerate subdendrites; e.g. the little return has distinct periodic/preperiodic points, which cannot be collapsed together. Their preimage components in the model are exactly the images of the actual preimage components. Each K_j is an isolated component of P^{-1}(K_(j+1)): the small-cycle map has degree two at the critical step and one otherwise, and at a noncritical step the other degree-one preimage component is disjoint and compact. This also follows from a Jordan neighborhood of a target little filled set avoiding the critical value at noncritical steps. The same component assertion holds after H because inverse images of connected sets under the proper monotone map are connected.

Set A0=hull(L_j) inside K^m, B0=Q^{-1}(A0), A=H^{-1}(A0), B=H^{-1}(B0)=P^{-1}(A). These are compact full connected carriers; B0 is connected since A0 contains the critical value (or use connectedness of the physical full preimage). The full-preimage equality is important. This is the inverse-image lift of KKL §2.5; it is not a selected arc representative inside a nontrivial fiber. If an alternative meaning of the source's lifted tree is intended, the following comparison would require a new proof for that choice. The model hull is forward invariant: Q(A0) is a connected subset of A0 containing the cycle, so it equals A0.

## 2. The original cut points, not an arbitrarily refined marking

KKL §2.5 specifies vertices (little Julia sets, branch points outside the cycle and alpha), and the finite connecting graph, including its preimage graph. For each model little vertex L_j put

    D_j = L_j intersect closure(A0 minus L_j).

This is a finite set of edge-contact points. The union of these finite sets is forward invariant. To prove it, take x in D_j and points y_n of A0 outside L_j tending to x. The separated preimage-component property just proved implies Q(y_n) is outside L_(j+1) eventually. Q(y_n) belongs to A0 by forward invariance, so Q(x) is in D_(j+1). Therefore every x in the union is preperiodic. The other graph vertices are the periodic points identified in §2.5, including alpha. This proves preperiodicity of the graph-side cuts without assuming preperiodicity of every possible attachment root.

The original ideal side marking on the model consists of the cuts at the corresponding prime-end accesses over these finite contacts and singleton vertices. Apply the monotone prime-end extension and singleton-cut rigidity of issue40_monotone_model_prime_end_bridge_20261124.md to the saturated A and B:

    m_A:C_A -> C_A0,   m_B:C_B -> C_B0.

Every model cut c0 of the original side marking has exactly one physical access c over it. Distinct model accesses over the same point give distinct physical cuts. Every physical access over a marked landing has singleton impression and corresponds to one such model access, by the impression correspondence. In the complement of these cuts, the inverse image of a model side is a single open arc of the physical circle, all of whose impressions lie in the corresponding lifted vertex or edge type. Thus the original side marking induced from the source's §2.5 lift corresponds to precisely these arcs; no cut is inserted merely because an unmarked fiber is nontrivial. This is not a claim that every physical prime end is singleton, nor is it a construction of a finer marking that would be incompatible with the original length count.

The actual degree-two covering p on C_B descends to p0 on C_B0 with m_A p=p0 m_B, as in the companion prime-end bridge. The preimage marking cuts for the covering likewise have singleton physical boundary fibers (connected fibers mapping by a covering to a singleton). Their model landing points are preperiodic. The immersion is a different map, locally the inclusion B containing A, and has no claimed global proper extension across added pieces.

## 3. Local finite hosts near marked cuts

This removes the global finite-attachment/finite-host assumption of issue40_marked_gate_sandwich_20261124.md. The larger model carrier B0 has finitely many preimage vertices (the components of Q^{-1} of the finite vertex family) and finitely many preimage edges (two per old edge). At an original marked landing x, the preimage vertex containing x is either its original little vertex L_j or a singleton preimage vertex. This follows from §1 and the forward invariance of the singleton vertex family in §2.5. All other preimage vertices are compact and disjoint from this vertex. Choose a small neighborhood of x avoiding them. Inside that neighborhood the part of B0 outside A0 can only be in the finitely many preimage edges, since the entire preimage vertex containing x is already in A0.

An open preimage edge has intersection with A0 a connected interval or the empty set: any subcontinuum of a dendrite contains the unique arc between two of its points. Its complement in the edge has at most two components. Consequently only finitely many components of B0 minus A0 can meet this chosen neighborhood. This assertion is local to marked landings. There may be infinitely many extra components in a preimage vertex away from all such neighborhoods; no global finiteness assertion is used.

Let r0=r_(B0,A0) be the full nested model contour collapse from issue40_ambient_angle_exactness_20261124.md. For each component X0 of B0 minus A0 let e_X denote its unique old host angle (not just its landing root). Its physical counterpart X=H^{-1}(X0) is exactly one component of B minus A, by proper monotonicity and saturation. In exterior coordinates of A, every limiting old boundary angle of X belongs to m_A^{-1}(e_X): apply the coordinate identity for H and use uniqueness of the model host. In particular a component whose host is a marked cut can accumulate only at that one physical angle.

Fix a marked cut c over c0, with singleton physical impression. Old exterior-coordinate approaches to c have physical limit x_phys, and model limit x. Thus any added components having points arbitrarily near c in the exteriorized coordinate belong to the finite local list just proved. Their host fibers on C_A are disjoint compact intervals or points; only a fiber over c0 can contain c, and this fiber is a singleton. Shrink a circular neighborhood of c to avoid all the other fibers in the finite list. At every point of each of the resulting two punctured one-sided arcs, the exteriorized physical added material stays away in some neighborhood of that point. Otherwise a sequence from the finite local list approaching that point would give another host fiber through the point. Compactness/localization in this argument follows also from the singleton impression: the physical map from the exterior coordinate has uniform limit at c, even though it need not be continuous everywhere on the circle.

It follows that on those one-sided arcs the actual inclusion of annular complements has a proper boundary chart. Indeed in old exterior coordinates their boundary is just an unobstructed analytic circle arc; local reflection or crosscuts identify the corresponding arcs of C_B with those of C_A. No uniform chart size at c, global properness, or smoothness of the physical carrier is asserted.

Write R=r0 m_B. On each regular one-sided chart R is exactly m_A of the old physical boundary coordinate. By monotonicity and continuity of R, the source limits of the charts are the two extreme gates of the fiber R^{-1}(c0). This holds for a singleton fiber as well as a whole inserted interval. At either extreme gate the model landing is the old landing x, hence its physical impression is singleton. Approaches from the source complement then have limiting old ideal angle c: model coordinate comparison forces all old-angle cluster points into m_A^{-1}(c0)={c}. Thus the actual immersion has the required proper boundary limit **at that end**, including the outer extreme gates for closed input. This pointwise extension does not purport to extend properly over the attached interior.

## 4. The marked four-gate sandwich

Here use explicitly the connected-interval and component-union assertions of KKL Prop. 6.5, and the nonempty open marking/refinement statement of Prop. 6.6. The comparison is for the source's defined restriction, not an alternative definition replacing A.3.2 on arbitrary sets.

Let I0 be an original marked open arc with distinct end cuts, or its closure. I=m_A^{-1}(I0). The candidate full restriction is

    J=R^{-1}(I0)=m_B^{-1}(r0^{-1}(I0)).

At each cut there are an inward and outward regular chart from §3. For the open interval, proper old images in I on the inward charts are included in the restriction by Prop. 6.5; their points approach the two inward extremes of J. For the closed interval the two outer extreme gates of J are also included via their proper old limits from §3. On the outward regular charts there are excluded points arbitrarily close to the outward edges of the respective cut fibers (for open input, exclude the attachment at an omitted cut by its omitted host, as described below). These outward points have actual proper old image outside I and cannot belong to an inserted component contour: in the exteriorized chart there is no attached material there. To guard against a purely planar reading of component incidence, choose points avoiding the finitely many local model root landings; they can be found arbitrarily near each extreme since no nondegenerate model prime-end interval has constant landing value, by boundary uniqueness. Their impressions cannot contain a root in that finite list.

For clarity the open-cut step uses the access-sensitive component-union assertion: any added contour at the omitted cut has host c0, with unique physical old angle c; it is not inserted from I. Old proper inverse points in that fiber have old limit c and are also excluded. This is the literal open-versus-closed endpoint rule required in Prop. 6.5 and A.3.4, not an equality between closure and image. For roots at other accesses over the same planar point, the cyclic gate location distinguishes the access; selecting by the planar point alone would not suffice.

With the excluded outward points fixing which connected interval is used, connectedness forces inclusion of the entire gap between inward limits. The omitted endpoint fibers in the open case, and included extreme gates in the closed case, give equality:

    iota^* I = m_B^{-1}(r0^{-1}(I0)).                       (4.1)

For closed input the proof includes all points of the endpoint fiber by connectedness, not just its two endpoints. Interior attachment fibers cannot move the outer boundaries of the connected interval. No count of the potentially wild interior impressions is needed.

The full circle is covered by closed original atoms, so its restriction and image are full. For a punctured circle I0=C_A0 minus {c0}, the omitted-host convention gives exactly (4.1). Even without that endpoint convention for this special case, monotonicity gives inclusion of the right side by covering I0 with shorter open marked arcs with distinct endpoints (M>=3 here). The resulting lower inclusion is all that the saturation argument needs. An open interval containing every marked atom need not be the full circle; the puncture must not be discarded.

### Component census and its remaining limitation

For an individual component X0 of B0 minus A0, its unique root y yields one branch of B0 minus {y}, with relative open contour O_X0 in C_B0 between consecutive accesses over y. This follows by cutting the model exterior along the finitely many rays to y and using the dendrite unique-arc property. Its physical open contour is m_B^{-1}(O_X0): by the impression correspondence these are precisely source ends whose impressions meet the physical component X=H^{-1}(X0), and those impressions lie entirely in X. This identifies each distinct component, multiplicity one, and its cyclic contour order. At a marked host, its physical root is singleton and each model gate over that root has singleton physical boundary fiber. The marked closed input therefore includes the whole relative closed contour there, including its individual root gates. At an interior host, extreme gates of the pulled-back model open contour are determined by the corresponding boundary fibers. A possibly nonsingleton root-access fiber must not be reported as a single physical prime end. Formula (4.1) determines the full marked interval nevertheless.

A stronger individual assertion in the proof of Prop. 6.6 says the contour of each extra component is itself a covering-marked multi-interval (the displayed marking notation there appears to confuse I with I'). If interpreted as a statement about each individual *open* contour, its boundary gates are covering cuts and its model root is preperiodic, hence all those root-access fibers are singleton. This cannot be used circularly as an independent proof of that stronger statement. A direct derivation of the individual closed-gate allocation at wild interior hosts from the maximal-set definition A.3.2, independent of the Prop. 6.5 union assertion, remains an explicit review obligation. The local marked sandwich does not require global preperiodicity of all attachment roots.

### Independent path test: what can be removed from the hypotheses

The earlier four-gate argument above imports the connected interval and full component-union assertions of Prop. 6.5. In particular it cannot be used as an independent proof of those assertions. A direct argument in the embedded physical slit case has now replaced that import in [the original PR #119 note, §2](issue40_native_slit_sector_escape_20261124.md). Its maximal-lift endpoint lemma also applies here: for any proper target path, every maximal parametrized lift under the holomorphic immersion of the two annuli is proper. The proof uses local inverse continuation and Fatou/Privalov boundary uniqueness in round-annulus coordinates. It does not use a boundary extension of the immersion.

Assuming the previously constructed model contour collapse and monotone transfers, there is a useful independent **admissibility** conclusion for general immersed carriers. Let J=R^{-1}(I0), with R=r0 m_B as above. In the target exteriorized model coordinates use the sphere map collapsing each attached group to its host from [the nested-contour proof, §1](issue40_ambient_angle_exactness_20261124.md). Compose with angular projection and the model projection to get a circle-valued map L on the target complement. The hybrid neighborhood chart may be extended to a plane homeomorphism outside a Jordan neighborhood of the filled set; no global dynamical conjugacy there is used. This L has degree one, extends on the target inner boundary as m_A, and is constant, equal to the host, on each physical component of B minus A (view B in the target via the local embedding). Its composition with the immersion extends on the source inner boundary as R, by the actual local coordinate identity and the contour construction. It need not extend on the outer ideal circle.

Suppose a target proper path gamma has exactly one maximal proper restriction gamma' subordinate to J. If gamma' starts or stops at an interior parameter time, its endpoint is on the inner source boundary, hence its image lies in the added physical carrier B minus A. Every target path portion before or after gamma' must stay in B minus A: otherwise a component of times outside B adjacent to that carrier would have a lift by the actual embedded neighborhood of the filled set, and maximal continuation would give a second proper restriction by the endpoint lemma. Both target endpoints are inner (B is compact in the target disk). Under L these initial and terminal portions are constant. Since gamma' is subordinate to J and L composed with the immersion extends as R, the angular homotopy class of L(gamma) lies in I0. Since m_A has a nondecreasing degree-one lift and I=m_A^{-1}(I0), the lifted endpoints and winding of gamma lie in a single lift of I. Equivalently gamma is subordinate to I, using the annular collar description. This proves that **J satisfies the defining implication of Appendix A.3.2**, without importing the union conclusion of Prop. 6.5.

This is not yet equality with the printed maximal-subset restriction in every pseudo-ql map. If that subset is a greatest admissible set, the implication gives J contained in it, sufficient for the eventual-saturation lower comparison. Merely being inclusion-maximal is not the same assertion; no greatestness is deduced from admissibility alone.

**Exact embedded locally connected subcase.** When the map is an ordinary quadratic-like inclusion between compactly nested Jordan disks and A subset B are locally connected dendrites, one can go further and independently prove greatestness. For an excluded source inner end b with R(b) outside I0, take a small proper loop with its two ideal endpoints equal to b, subordinate to {b}. If b lands in A, it has a unique old angular limit (the nested-contour theorem) and the included loop already gives a proper target path based at that old access. If b lands in an added component X, prepend the unique arc in the dendrite from its root to the landing and append its reverse. Apart from the root that arc lies entirely in X; in old exterior coordinates it approaches the unique host angle of X. The resulting target path has just one maximal restriction, the loop, and has both target endpoints at the excluded old host. The §1.4 definition allows coincident ideal endpoints; the A.3.2 test uses proper paths, not only admissible marked leaves. An excluded outer source end is ruled out by the same loop construction with a connecting exterior arc from the target outer boundary, possible for the nested Jordan disks. Thus every excluded end separately violates the defining test. In this case J is the greatest admissible subset of the whole ideal boundary, and (4.1) follows without invoking Prop. 6.5. With locally connected carriers the physical model is the carrier itself, so this gives the direct contour/path identity for ordinary quadratic-like maps beyond the real slit, subject to the previously proved nested-contour theorem. No added global finite-host hypothesis is required.

**Remaining failure of the attempted general proof:** in a genuinely noninjective immersion, the exclusion loop can have further proper lifts on other sheets; compactness and injectivity near the filled set only control the distinguished lift. The uniqueness antecedent of A.3.2 can then fail. For non-locally-connected attached continua one also cannot assume the arc from a landing to the root used in the embedded locally connected proof exists. Therefore the general upper/exclusion argument, or a different proof of greatestness, remains unproved. This is the precise point at which the direct proof stops. The earlier use of Prop. 6.5 cannot be counted as eliminating it.

## 5. Native interval growth and same-source edge comparison

Let Phi(I0)=p0(r0^{-1}(I0)). The covering square and equality of positive covering degrees give p(m_B^{-1}(S))=m_A^{-1}(p0(S)) for every model source subset S (the two actual inverse images of each target point belong one to each of its two model inverse-image fibers, by monotonicity/degree on the circle). Thus (4.1) intertwines the original physical marked restriction-then-cover action with Phi, including closed endpoint births. Even if the punctured-circle convention is left unresolved, the lower inclusion for that case suffices.

By nested contour coherence of the ambient-angle note,

    Phi(I0)=q D(q^{-1}(I0)),
    Phi^n(I0) contains q D^n(q^{-1}(I0)),

with q the onto ambient-to-carrier contour collapse and D the exact degree-two external dynamics (it is a factor of actual angle doubling). Every nonempty open I0 has nonempty open inverse image. This eventually fills the ambient circle, so every nonempty original open marked physical interval eventually saturates the whole circle, as does every closed one containing a complete atom. The physical action is inclusion-monotone by the source's restriction/combinatorial action. Consequently there is no proper positive marked periodic interval. This is a proof of the nonreturn mechanism for the specified source realization, not a finite-state simulation.

For comparison to T_F, subdivide every model old edge by the finite preimage vertices. Intersections with the old arc are connected intervals, so the subdivision is finite. Each intervening segment is a whole preimage edge, and maps onto its corresponding original edge. At an interior point of this segment there are two distinct model contour sides, and the local orientation-preserving unbranched map carries them to the two sides of the target edge. The r0 contour restriction places each in its corresponding old side, so the exact marked restriction and covering comparison now transport precisely these complete side segments, with multiplicity. These are the same edges used in the definition of T_F in §2.5, not an unrelated adjacency matrix. With row/column convention fixed as in issue40_weighted_trace_and_crest_audit_20261124.md, the physical side count vector v satisfies v(F_*I) >= T_F v(I) for a proper output. Indeed on the connected pullback the degree-two boundary cover is injective whenever its image is proper, so different traversed sides do not collide. Pairing with the positive Perron left eigenvector gives strict weighted growth for v(I) nonzero. The angular argument covers the edge-free case, without any assumption that a little Julia side has positive graph weight.

For the original combinatorial length, Prop. 6.6 gives nonempty disjoint refined atom pullbacks; proper covering output is injective on the connected restriction, hence length never decreases before saturation. At a plateau of fixed positive length k, the number of proper connected marked intervals meeting the critical limb is at most 6b+k-1 (including the punctured-circle convention). Therefore 6b+k distinct visits with no growth would force repetition, contradicting eventual saturation. The repaired conclusion of Prop. 6.9 is **saturation or strict length growth**. It also handles zero-edge sectors. No growth assertion from a saturated circle, or uniform bound on escape of arbitrarily tiny unmarked subarcs, is made.

## 6. Status, scope and independent checks

This proof candidate discharges the previous global finite-host assumption and supplies source-specific arguments for canonical saturation and singleton original marking. It uses the standard first-renormalization puzzle identification, the full inverse-image interpretation of the source lift, and the existing monotone-extension and nested-contour lemmas. The stated source propositions about interval connectedness, component unions, access-sensitive incidence and refinement are explicitly imported. Their maximal-path semantics must not be advertised as independently proved for all wild individual closed gates. Those dependencies and the earlier analytic lemmas require independent specialist review.

A separate, genuine obstruction remains in the literal wave definition: see issue40_native_short_wave_full_base_counterexample_20261124.md. A short disconnected wave can have the entire circle as unique base and nonunique positive crests. The previous blanket large-q proper-base inference is withdrawn. The repaired bounded-visits count applies to actual common-crest positive closed intervals. Proving the required width-preserving selection under §§6.2–6.3 remains necessary for the full Wave Lemma. No CI result, independent validation, or MLC proof is claimed.

# Issue #40: full-base short waves, nonunique positive crests, and the common-crest correction

2026-11-24. Written source-level audit; not a numerical experiment. This note **corrects** the blanket large-rotation proper-base inference in the earlier short-wave notes. It is independent of the marked interval nonreturn argument.

## 1. Literal definitions being tested

KKL v1, [Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27272v1), allows the entire ideal boundary component as an interval. A marked path cannot be subordinate to a multi-interval of length at most two. A wave is a lamination whose leaves are subordinate to one multi-interval; its base and two end intervals are defined by minimality, and its crest is the base minus the union of the ends. No connected-support, single-arc-class, or disjoint-end hypothesis appears in this definition. At the beginning of §6 shortness is defined leafwise, and the source then asserts that short waves are automatically 12b-controllable.

The distinction is substantive: there are actual disjoint rectangle foliations in the annulus, not just formal endpoint incidence lists, for which this assertion and/or the uniqueness of the specified crest fails under the literal definition.

## 2. A cyclic packet construction on the actual annulus

Let C be the inner ideal circle of the actual surface U minus T, with M cyclically consecutive marked open atoms I_0,...,I_(M-1). Suppose M >= 10 and each limb contains at least three atoms. Use all original cuts, not a refinement. Choose cyclic anchors a_0,...,a_(N-1) among the atoms, with successive cyclic index differences 2, except for one difference 3 if M is odd. Thus N=floor(M/2). In each anchor atom select two small, disjoint closed endpoint subarcs in its interior, ordered incoming then outgoing. For each j take a foliated quadrilateral in a thin conformal collar of C connecting the outgoing subarc at a_j to the incoming subarc at a_(j+1). The quadrilateral can be chosen to lie over the closed circular arc between these two subarcs; the arcs for different j are separated by small gaps strictly inside anchor atoms. For example work in round-annulus coordinates near the inner circle, draw a boundary-parallel disk with the prescribed disjoint boundary subarcs, and use its rectangle uniformization. Every foliation has strictly positive finite conformal width. Their finite disjoint union is an actual lamination S of simple proper marked paths.

Every leaf has the boundary-parallel homotopy support from one anchor to the next in cyclic order. Its least marked base consists of three atoms, or four for the exceptional spacing. No other boundary-parallel direction gives the same homotopy rel endpoints (lift the annulus to a strip). In particular every leaf is an admissible marked path: no multi-interval of length at most two supports it. Its own crest has one or two whole atoms. Since all limbs have at least three atoms, no leaf crests over a limb. Hence S is short according to the leafwise definition.

Every original marked cut lies in the interior of the homotopy support of at least one packet. Consequently no proper marked multi-interval can support every leaf. The base of S is uniquely the entire C. This remains true even though there are genuine unmarked gaps in the physical supports inside anchor atoms.

## 3. End intervals and genuine positive crest ambiguity

For any anchor a let E_a be the connected open multi-interval beginning with the next anchor and ending with the preceding anchor, in positive cyclic order. It contains every anchor except a, and excludes the gap of atoms around a between its two neighbors. A connected end interval meeting every packet can omit at most one anchor: the omitted anchors are consecutive, and two consecutive omissions would leave the packet between them with neither endpoint in the end interval. Thus E_a is inclusion-minimal. If M is even, all E_a have length M-3 and are globally shortest among possible individual ends. If M is odd, the anchors adjacent to the spacing-three gap give two globally shortest ends, of length M-4.

For adjacent anchors a,b, the pair (E_a,E_b) admits a distinct endpoint in each end for every leaf. The packet a-to-b has its endpoint at b in E_a and its endpoint at a in E_b; packets incident to only one omitted anchor have the evident assignment; all other packets have both endpoints in both ends. The pair is minimal in each coordinate: deleting either extreme atom of E_a (respectively E_b) removes the only endpoint of a packet incident to a (respectively b).

The complement C minus (E_a union E_b) is the closed marked gap between a and b. It has one or two complete open atoms and is nonempty. If M is even, changing the adjacent pair changes the crest even when both ends have globally minimum length. Hence the issue is not simply two alternative supporting bases or an empty crest. Here the base is unique, and multiple equally minimal pairs give different positive closed crests.

### Concrete native 10-atom instance

The certified physical 3/9-tip carrier of PR #74 has ten original atoms in cyclic order and two limb blocks of lengths seven and three. Apply the collar construction with anchors I_0,I_2,I_4,I_6,I_8 (rotate indexing arbitrarily). The packets have least bases (0,1,2), (2,3,4), (4,5,6), (6,7,8), (8,9,0). They are jointly a short wave in that very marked annulus. E_0=(2,3,4,5,6,7,8) and E_2=(4,5,6,7,8,9,0) give crest closure(I_1); E_2 and E_4=(6,7,8,9,0,1,2) give crest closure(I_3). Each choice is equally minimal (length seven per end). The two crests are different. This example uses the native ideal marking and actual positive-width foliations, not an artificially assigned contour map.

## 4. Failure of the blanket high-rotation assertion

For a prime primitive rotation-1/q stage with q>1 and disjoint small Julia cycle, alpha is outside the cycle, and each of the q limbs contains a cycle vertex and its connection to alpha. The two sides of a connecting edge and at least one side of a little Julia vertex give at least three atoms per limb in the full original side marking. If using a different marking, state this lower bound as a hypothesis. Lemma 4.8 gives the upper bound 6b, not a bound on the spread of a disconnected lamination.

At any actual such stage with q >= 24b, the construction applies with M >= 3q. All its leaves are short, but every end meeting all packets has length at least M-4, which exceeds 12b. Its unique base is the entire circle. This contradicts the automatic controllability assertion at §6 under the literal allowance of overlapping ends and arbitrary multi-class laminations. If instead the intended convention requires disjoint ends, this lamination has no admissible such ends (each must contain all but at most one anchor), so Appendix A.1 needs that additional restriction. Either way the assertion cannot be deduced from leafwise shortness and the printed unrestricted definition alone.

This explicitly withdraws the earlier blanket conclusion that large q alone forces *all* short waves to have proper bases, insofar as that conclusion used the automatic end bound. It does not disprove the bounded-visits statement for the intended proof-selected class, the amplification theorem, or MLC. No lower bound on the packet widths comparable to the source's wide-wave threshold is asserted. The high-q conclusion is a construction on every native stage meeting the stated marking hypotheses, not a separately certified high-q parameter.

## 5. Narrow correction that restores the crest argument

A sufficient corrected domain is a nonempty lamination of boundary-parallel simple marked paths having a **common actual crest**: there is a marked cut or closed marked interval lying in the crest of every individual leaf in its own relative homotopy class. Equivalently for use in a critical visit, require the actual nonempty interval over which the wave is taken to lie in every leaf's crest, not merely in a set difference for an arbitrarily chosen pair of overlapping end intervals.

Choose a lift of a point of the common crest to the ideal line in the universal strip. Lift each leaf using the boundary-parallel support containing that point. Any two support intervals are nested: if they overlap without nesting, their four endpoint positions alternate, forcing intersection of the lifted proper arcs by separation in the strip. Endpoints at marked atom interiors do not equal the common marked cut; coincident limit endpoints cause no problem. Finite marking gives a largest marked hull of the nested supports, realized at the level of its outermost endpoint atoms by one of the leaves. For a short leaf its marked support can meet at most two consecutive limb blocks: otherwise an entire intermediate limb lies strictly between the two endpoint atoms. At high q (in particular q>=24b) the largest hull therefore omits a limb and is a proper base. Its two end intervals are the hulls of the left and right endpoint atoms. A nonempty gap cannot be only a marked cut: some extreme leaf of the left end and some extreme leaf of the right end meet the adjacent atoms across the gap, and nesting forces one leaf joining those adjacent atoms. That leaf is inadmissible. Thus the common positive crest is a regular closed marked interval with 1 <= length < 12b, since it contains no entire limb and meets at most two consecutive limbs of size <=6b.

The proper-base count in the previous notes consequently remains valid for this corrected class. A separately sufficient assumption is that the ends are disjoint and that the proof uses the corresponding common crest rather than the full circular complement: in a proper base this follows from separation and minimality. The common-crest formulation is preferable for controlling actual restriction and pushforward operations.

## 6. Precisely what remains

For Lemma 6.4, a chain of genuine common-crest short critical visits with positive closed marked crests has the correct inclusion of closed intervals; the ambient-angle exactness and native marked realization notes then give the desired bounded-visit count. But §§6.2–6.3 and the extraction lemmas must be audited to show the proof-selected waves can always be chosen in that corrected class **without losing the width bounds or the required pushforward inclusion**. A disconnected union of packets cannot be silently turned into one common-crest wave. This is a real source-level definition/selection obstruction, not an issue with Perron growth.

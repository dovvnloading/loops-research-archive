# Non-LC contact-angle surgery and the periodic-atom obstruction (Issue #40)

2026-11-24. Follow-up to the physical slit computation in PR #119. This note gives a conditional analytic repair on full continua, not a synthetic finite transition matrix. It does **not** claim the unrestricted native KKL realization or close #40.

## 1. Ambient impressions descend to subcontinua at a singleton contact

**Lemma.** Let K be a nondegenerate full compact plane continuum with empty interior, A a nondegenerate full subcontinuum, and x in A. Assume that the set of prime-end angles of the exterior of K whose impressions contain x is finite, and every such impression is the singleton {x}. Then the set of prime-end angles of the exterior of A whose impressions contain x is finite, with cardinality at most the corresponding cardinality for K. No local connectivity of A or K is required elsewhere.

**Proof.** Use exterior Riemann maps h_K and h_A from |w|>1, normalized at infinity. Prime ends here can equally be computed in annular neighborhoods of the compacta. The map g = h_A^{-1} h_K is holomorphic and injective on |w|>1, with |g|>1.

For an ambient angle ζ with impression {x}, every unrestricted interior approach to ζ has h_K(w) → x. Consequently |g(w)| → 1: any sequence bounded away from the unit circle either has a convergent subsequence in the exterior, which would map outside A, or escapes to infinity, which would map to infinity.

We claim g has a unique angular limit at ζ. Otherwise it has two different cluster points a,b on the unit circle. The sets W_ε = {|w|>1} ∩ B(ζ,ε) are connected for small ε. Their images under g are connected, lie in annuli of thickness tending to zero, and contain points approaching a and b. Thus their angular projections contain one of two fixed nondegenerate compact subarcs separating disjoint small neighborhoods of a and b (take a subsequence ε_n → 0 on which the same subarc J works). For each η in J choose w_n in W_{ε_n} with g(w_n)=r_n η, r_n→1 from above. Then h_A(r_n η)→x. Radial limits of h_A exist almost everywhere: ξ(h_A(1/ξ)-x) is bounded analytic on the unit disk, with its singularity at zero removed by the Laurent expansion at infinity. On almost every angle in J this function has boundary value zero (in inverted coordinates). Boundary uniqueness for bounded analytic functions implies it is identically zero, contradicting univalence. This proves existence of a unique limit g_*(ζ). No global boundary extension is asserted.

For any A-angle η whose impression contains x, take z_n = h_A(w_n)→x, w_n→η. Since K has empty interior, approximate z_n by z'_n outside K, sufficiently closely that z'_n→x and h_A^{-1}(z'_n)→η. A subsequence of h_K^{-1}(z'_n) converges to some ambient angle ζ; its impression contains x, so is {x}. Then g_*(ζ)=η. This proves the claimed cardinality bound. In particular, one must count angles, not distinct subsets that happen to be equal as impressions. The conclusion does not say that the A-impressions themselves are singletons. ∎

**Source application.** Jan Kiwi, *Rational Rays and Critical Portraits of Complex Polynomials*, [arXiv:math/9710212v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9710212), Theorem 1(a),(b), printed p. 2, gives precisely the ambient hypotheses at preperiodic points of polynomials with connected Julia set and all cycles repelling (the angle count is the finiteness of E(z) in the proof of the impression theorem). These hypotheses transfer to a verified hybrid-straightened quadratic-like germ: a neighborhood homeomorphism transports null chains and impressions. The exterior access circle is independent of the enclosing Jordan collar. This is a way to handle actual non-LC filled Julia sets without replacing their physical impressions by a rational lamination quotient. It still requires checking the dynamical status of each actual contact point. The mere fact that all additional components meet at one planar point does not show that point is preperiodic.

## 2. Finite-angle attachment surgery, with arbitrary wild attached continua

**Theorem (finite-contact version).** Suppose A ⊂ B ⊂ K are nondegenerate full compact continua, K has empty interior, and B\A has finitely many connected components, each with closure meeting A. Assume the total contact set cl(B\A) ∩ A is a finite set of points satisfying the ambient singleton-contact hypothesis in §1. Then:

- Every added component attaches at exactly one angle of the old exterior A, although several components may attach at different angles of the same physical point or at the same angle.
- The old prime-end circle minus finitely many contact angles embeds as disjoint open arcs of the new prime-end circle in the same cyclic order.
- Each contact angle is replaced by one closed nondegenerate gap arc, accounting for the entire attached material over that angle. This yields a continuous monotone degree-one contour collapse r: C_B → C_A. This is **not** an extension of the inclusion of the two physical exteriors over their entire ideal boundaries.

**Proof.** Exteriorize by h_A. The new compact obstacle in this coordinate is L = closed unit disk ∪ h_A^{-1}(B\A). It is compact: its complement in the sphere is h_A^{-1}(sphere\B), including infinity; all additional material is bounded. Its outside-the-disk part has empty interior, and its exterior is connected.

For an added component X, let Y_X = cl(h_A^{-1}(X)). This is compact connected. Its intersection with the unit circle is nonempty (use a sequence in X converging to A), and every angle in that intersection has an A-impression containing a point of the total contact set. By §1 the possible angles form a finite set. The union Y_X ∪ closed unit disk is full. Indeed any bounded complementary component would be contained in L, because the exterior of L is connected to infinity, but would lie outside the closed disk, contradicting empty interior there. Two intersecting compact connected sets with nonseparating union have connected intersection in the plane: the reduced Čech H^0 of the intersection injects into Čech H^1 of the union by Mayer–Vietoris, and the latter vanishes by Alexander duality. Thus Y_X intersects the circle in precisely one angle.

Distinct Y_X can intersect only on the unit circle: components are closed relative to B\A, and h_A is a homeomorphism there. Group them by contact angle e. The resulting finite groups Y_e are compact connected, disjoint for different angles, and meet the disk only at e. Finiteness here is important. It ensures positive separation between groups, and excludes collective accumulation of new material on an otherwise regular old arc.

Let E be the actual finite set of contact angles. Along each complementary open arc of the old circle, L has a locally analytic boundary (locally there is no attached material). Schwarz reflection gives a one-to-one local prime-end correspondence with its new exterior. These correspondences glue to embeddings of the complementary arcs, whose cyclic order agrees with the original order: crosscuts or disjoint connecting arcs to a common exterior Jordan circle preserve their oriented order. The complement consists of the closed intervening arcs G_e, one for each e. For one contact angle the old complement is a punctured circle, and the same description uses its two one-sided ends. With no added material the map is the identity.

For clarity, the following impression argument takes place in the **exteriorized coordinate**, not the original physical plane. The union of impressions over a closed intervening arc G_e is connected compact: it is the intersection of nested closures of connected exterior collars approaching that arc, equivalently the union of their pointwise cluster sets by compactness. It contains e by taking limits along the adjacent regular arc. No other regular circle point can occur in such impressions: the inverse conformal coordinate extends at each smooth local boundary point and assigns it its unique prime end. All remaining boundary points are in the disjoint compact groups Y_e. Connectedness forces the union of impressions over G_e to lie in Y_e. Conversely every outside-the-disk point of Y_e is a boundary point, hence belongs to an impression; that impression cannot be on a regular arc or another gap. Thus the union is exactly Y_e. Each nontrivial group has at least two distinct accessible outside-the-disk boundary points (accessible points are dense on the boundary of a plane domain, by the first intersection of a segment from an interior point to a nearby boundary point). They determine different prime ends, so G_e is nondegenerate.

Define r on each regular new arc by the inverse regular correspondence and collapse G_e to e. The order and one-sided limits give continuity, surjectivity, connected fibers and degree one. This is an explicit contour code constructed from actual conformal domains; over an attachment, the physical inclusion can take approaches to old interior points, so the code cannot be interpreted as its continuous boundary map. ∎

**Exact endpoint behavior of the contour code.** If I is a connected open arc with distinct endpoints, its full inverse image contains every entire G_e with e in I. For a contact at an endpoint of I, cl(r^{-1}(I)) contains just the gate at that endpoint approached from inside I. In contrast, r^{-1}(cl I) contains the entire G_e, including the other gate. Precisely,

r^{-1}(cl I) = cl(r^{-1}(I)) ∪ ⋃_{e ∈ E ∩ ∂I} G_e.

For a punctured circle with missing contact angle e, both gates are in the closure while the gap interior may be missing. If the puncture is not a contact, the closure is the entire circle. The full circle itself is fixed by inverse image. For an actual degree-two boundary covering p, one has p(cl J)=cl(p(J)), so any additional closed-image contribution is exactly the image of these extra endpoint fibers, less whatever the open-image closure already covers. In particular, one cannot assert that the contribution remains distinct after saturation.

**Native interpretation and remaining boundary.** The theorem handles non-locally-connected carriers and attachments with finite total preperiodic contact and finite component census. It produces a unique angular attachment for each component and a complete *grouped* ideal-boundary census when multiple components share an angle. It does not separately count individual sector gates of multiple components at the same angle. For the KKL carrier pair (old T, full preimage T'), it furnishes a conformal derivation of the complete-block insertion rule, provided these hypotheses are checked and the actual proper-path incidence at the angle is identified with the source rule in Proposition 6.5. Under that identification the contour pullback of a connected marked interval is r^{-1}(I), with precisely the endpoint formula above. This last statement uses Proposition 6.5 for proper-path subordination; it is not a new proof of all its path-level assertions. Also, if working with an immersion rather than an inclusion, a common physical carrier neighborhood must first be justified. In particular, neither preperiodicity of the exceptional root nor finite total contact and component hypotheses are being inferred just from the one-root statement in the proof of Proposition 6.8.

## 3. Single-atom rigidity: a reduced native obstruction

Source: Kahn–Kapiamba–Lyubich, [arXiv:2606.27272v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27272v1), §6.1, Propositions 6.5–6.6, and Appendix A.3.2–A.3.4. The following reduction takes their actual connected open interval pullback, nonempty disjoint end-proper atom pullbacks and covering-refinement assertions as hypotheses. It does not assume the conclusion of Proposition 6.9.

Let C be the finite originally marked inner circle, C' its degree-two covering circle with the lifted marking, and p:C'→C the covering. Let P_A be the pullback of an original open atom A. For an original connected open marked interval I, its open pullback J contains P_A for each A in I, by monotonicity. These P_A are nonempty disjoint unions of covering-marked atoms and possibly internal cuts. If p(J) is proper, p|J is injective: in degree-two circle coordinates the length of an open lift of J is at most a half turn. A lift exactly one half turn long maps injectively to a punctured circle, not the whole circle.

Thus the number of original atoms contained in p(J) is at least the number contained in I, also when the image is the full circle. Equality in a proper step forces each P_A to consist of exactly one covering atom, with p(P_A) exactly one original atom. It also forbids any additional covering atom contributed by internal cuts. The old atoms in I correspond bijectively to the atoms of p(J). By the source end-properness, each single-atom P_A is proper on that atom, not merely at its ends.

In a proper periodic connected marked interval orbit, length must be constant (saturation is absorbing). Composing the bijections of atoms around a period gives a permutation of the starting atom set. Hence every atom of the interval is itself periodic, possibly with a multiple of the interval period. Conversely any periodic atom is a periodic interval. Consequently:

**No proper periodic original marked intervals if and only if there are no cycles of individual original marking atoms.**

This reduces the native nonperiodicity problem to the geometry of single-atom proper steps. There is no need to prove a full dual WAD inequality on arbitrary intervals merely to obtain this implication. The full-circle counterexample to the literal Proposition 6.9 is already in PR #38 and is not a new result here. A periodic-atom exclusion still yields the corrected alternative: saturation or strict length growth after more critical visits than the number of possible proper marked intervals of the starting length meeting the critical block.

## 4. An analytic chart on a vertex-side arc without a local-connectivity hypothesis

Let K_j ⊂ T be full nondegenerate compacta. Write h_T,h_j for exterior maps, and take an open arc U of C_T such that the entire impression of each angle in U lies in K_j. This is a *support hypothesis on impressions*, not a claim inferred from a combinatorial label. Then g = h_j^{-1} h_T extends analytically across U, restricts to an injective local analytic diffeomorphism there, and identifies U with a nondegenerate open arc of the small Julia exterior angle circle.

Indeed |g|→1 at every point of U, locally uniformly on compact subarcs by the cluster-set characterization of impressions. In a small half-disk at any point of U, log|g| is positive harmonic and has continuous zero boundary data on the circular boundary segment. Odd harmonic reflection followed by a harmonic conjugate extends the logarithm and hence g analytically. The Hopf boundary lemma gives nonzero normal derivative of log|g| at the segment, hence nonzero derivative of g. If two distinct angles of U had the same image, their analytic half-neighborhoods on the exterior side would have overlapping images, violating univalence in the open exterior. The restriction is therefore an injective open map onto an arc (possibly a punctured circle), with the induced orientation. This argument does not require that the impressions avoid the attachment point. ∎

**Application to periodic zero-edge atoms, conditional on actual side fidelity.** Suppose a proper single-atom cycle consists entirely of small-Julia vertex sides, with the following native properties checked:

1. The full impressions of a vertex-side atom are supported on its designated small Julia continuum, so the chart just proved applies.
2. On each cycle step, the proper immersion pullback gives a bijection of these actual old/source access intervals, and the holomorphic covering and the small Julia map agree in these local prime-end charts. In the finite-contact contour theorem, this is the regular correspondence when the atom contains no attachment fiber; a single proper pullback cannot contain a genuine extra piece mapping into the old interior.
3. The return on the small Julia set is the actual degree-two quadratic-like germ with a verified hybrid straightening.

After a whole number of small-Julia circuits, the proper cycle therefore induces a self-homeomorphism, given by the return dynamics, of one nondegenerate external angle arc of that small Julia set. The hybrid conjugacy gives the external degree-two map in the polynomial coordinate; an iterate over s circuits is conjugate on the circle to θ ↦ 2^s θ mod 1. No nondegenerate proper arc can be mapped bijectively onto itself by this map: on an injective lift the image length is 2^s times the input length, which is incompatible with equality of positive lengths. This excludes the all-vertex atom cycle under the three stated conditions. It does not assume global local connectivity or a conformal extension of the immersion at exceptional contacts. It is not a uniform bound on escape times for arbitrarily short unmarked angular intervals.

For an edge-side atom, the reduced condition needed is: if its image is one original atom on a proper step, that atom is an edge side and the corresponding physical Hubbard-edge incidence column has exactly one entry 1 and all others zero, counting traversals with multiplicity. If this is verified for the actual edge traces, an edge-side atom cycle gives a directed cycle of deterministic incidence columns. The resulting set of edges is closed under outgoing transitions. For an irreducible edge matrix with PF eigenvalue >1 this is impossible: a proper closed subset contradicts irreducibility, and if it is the full set the matrix is a permutation and has spectral radius 1. The finite-contact atlas alone does not establish this multiplicity condition, which is why the edge case is explicitly left conditional.

## 5. Exact open gates after this advance

- Determine the actual dynamical status of the exceptional attachment roots, including preperiodicity if the Kiwi argument is to be invoked, and verify finite total contact and finite added-component hypotheses in the native carrier embedding. The statement of a common planar attachment point is not itself this proof.
- Identify the complete grouped angular gaps with Proposition 6.5's proper-path incidence in the actual immersed category, including individual gate multiplicities if several branches share an angle.
- Verify the support and chart commutation of vertex-side atoms on a hypothetical *single-atom cycle*, and the single-atom edge traversal multiplicity condition above. These suffice for the reduced nonreturn theorem; they are narrower than requiring a general weighted inequality on every proper interval.
- Use monotonicity with actual closed crests, not unconditional commutation with closure. The finite-contact endpoint formula shows precisely when an additional fiber is present; it does not by itself prove Wave positivity, shortness or the selected proper-path realization in §6.

The previous physical slit proof and its five-step bound remain restricted to its explicitly constructed contour. Neither this note nor that bound proves the general native Wave amplification theorem. This is a written analytic argument, pending independent mathematical review, not a formal verification or a new CI result. Issue #40 remains open.

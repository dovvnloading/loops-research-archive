# Local near-bank cap for remote auxiliary gaps and localized initial Waves

Continuation of [GUARD_FREE_ANGULAR_JUMP](GUARD_FREE_ANGULAR_JUMP.md),
[ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE](ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE.md),
and [ANCHORED_SHORT_JUMP](ANCHORED_SHORT_JUMP.md).

**Advance.** The global anchored maximum is not necessary for the
comparison on the proof-used near-critical packets. Under the exact
proper-middle and corrected geometric marking assumptions, auxiliary
raw gaps at remote median probes of a parent having a near-bank end
are themselves near-bank crested, after at most a two-way split.
This replaces the remote auxiliary cap by a finite near-bank
supremum. In addition, every localized input direction can be
compared to this near-bank supremum by the one-sided angular
replacement, without assuming its leaves are short.

Together with the guard-free short program, this reduces the
width estimate for the original fixed-control initial class to
one finite **near-bank expansion gap**, not the difference between
a global remote supremum and a near-bank supremum. This does not
bound that expansion gap. The result is a conditional alternative
route, not closure of the source native theorem.

## 1. Banks and fixed local cap

Use the actual native rotation 1/q and parabolic b-boundedness,
with all the actual corrected contour, saturated hull, first/last
old-proper fine atoms, canonical occurrence, critical deck-pair,
selected inverse-limb and two-bank host/fine-side hypotheses of
ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE and GUARD_FREE_ANGULAR_JUMP.
In particular assume the full old-proper noncritical middle
chart of the latter note, including the homeomorphic old ideal
limit and the actual injective boundary covering on
BL_[b,q-b-2]. This is stronger than the formal limb shift.

Put h=b+2, H=h+2, c=b+12, C=C_2, N=h+12b*2^h and set
c^+=c+H+4. Work in the high-type range q>=2c^++4; this
guarantees the proper middle and all near-bank intervals
used below are in their indicated cyclic positions. For
0<=a<=c^+ write B_a=BL_(-a)..BL_a.
Use C_* as in ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE: actual Borel
laminations of simple proper distinct-inner-ended marked
cap paths sharing a whole original crest atom. Define

    M_a = sup { W(A): A in C_* and some common
              crest atom of A belongs to B_a },
    M = M_(c^+),
    Delta_loc = M_(c^+) - M_c.

These suprema are finite for a fixed actual map by the
radial angular envelope (there are finitely many positive
length marking atoms). They are nonnegative and monotone
with the bank. They do not depend on a fixed-control Wave
quantifier. No cap on arbitrary remote C_* packets is
assumed in what follows.

## 2. Localization of the remote median auxiliary packets

Consider an actual stage-j simple parent in the common
primary sequence, with at least one ideal end in B_c.
Use the proper middle subcorridor

    J_old=BL_[c+1,q-c-1],

including its internal dividing cuts and excluding its
two outer limiting cuts. Let J' be its actual pulled
interval on the one-step source ideal circle. By the
proper middle hypothesis, every point of J' has a
continuous proper old-boundary limit in J_old, the
old limit is a homeomorphism, and the boundary covering
maps J' injectively onto BL_[c+2,q-c]. In particular
an inner ideal end of a maximal raw gap in J' can occur
only at an original endpoint time of the stage-j
parent. At an interior parent time the parent is in
X=U minus T and has an interior value; a proper old
limit in T would contradict the immersion lift
identity. This is the canonical raw endpoint argument,
and does not require singleton outputs.

It follows that the two raw ideal ends of any complete
inner-ended raw gap cannot both be in J'. Otherwise
both are original endpoint times, and both actual old
ideal ends of the parent would belong to J_old, which
is disjoint from B_c. Remove the finite marked-cut
landing exceptions at width zero by the usual
point-singularity metric and occurrence descent.

Now consider any of the good auxiliary gaps over one
of the fixed old-proper first/last fine probes used by
the bounded-probe median argument. Its actual simple
cap contains the whole probe fine atom strictly between
its ends, and its covering image is simple by
fixed-anchor confinement. This holds equally for a
positive or negative raw gap (negative refers only to
the completed parent's orientation). If the probe's old
atom lies in B_(c+2), its fine covering image is in
B_(c+3): on a noncritical limb it translates by one,
and on a critical old-proper atom it is in B_b by
CRITICAL_PHASE_LOCALIZATION. This is already a common
near-bank atom in the output packet.

Otherwise the probe old atom is strictly interior to
J_old, outside the first and last limbs of J_old.
Its actual old-proper fine probe belongs to J'.
A connected proper cap base containing this probe
and with at least one endpoint outside J' must, in
one of its two directions from the probe, traverse
the whole first or last pulled limb block of J':
BL_(c+1) or BL_(q-c-1). The fine-marked cuts are the
actual cuts of these whole blocks; the endpoints of
the gap lie beyond the traversed block in the cap
direction, modulo the excluded cut exceptions.
Consequently the simple covering cap contains all
the constituent atoms of BL_(c+2) or BL_(q-c)
strictly between its ends, by the actual injective
covering on the cap. These limbs lie in B_(c+3).

Disjointize the two cases by first eligible side.
For each fixed remote probe the actual good output
packet splits into at most two Borel sublaminations,
each with a fixed whole near-bank crest atom (choose
one atom of the indicated whole boundary limb).
There are no generic image-intersection or phase
questions here: within the probe's one fixed
covering chart the output support is already
injective on its actual chosen gap occurrences.
Its two subfamilies have disjoint Borel supports.
Each has width <=M; their sum is <=2M. Across
different probes we use a sum of separately chosen
admissible energies, not width additivity of their
possibly coincident images.

**Local auxiliary cap.** For the bounded signed-probe
median Shift of ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE, whenever
the tested parents each have an actual end in B_c,
all n actual auxiliary probe packets have total
width at most 2 n M. If the probes are already in
B_(c+2), the smaller cap n M suffices. No bound on
the global remote anchored supremum is required.

The proper middle and original endpoint hypotheses
are essential. A remote anchor in an arbitrary
abstract cover is not a certificate of near-bank
output control. Nor do we infer that arbitrary
raw gaps with no inside probe have such a crest.

## 3. Local nonshort charge

Let A be an actual C_* packet of width w with a
common atom in B_c. Construct the common positive
raw primary sequence through H as in
ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE. The original span
discard, zero-width exceptions, incomplete H-lifts
and per-step fixed-anchor discards give

    E=1+2^H V+H C,
    W(G)>=w-E.

At each selected step, the target common atom
moves one limb for a noncritical old-proper
anchor, or into B_b at a critical one.
Consequently all the actual primary outputs
D_j have a common atom in B_(c+H), and belong
to the class capped by M. Their supports and
original-parent correspondence are injective as
before. Put d=M-w+E>=0 and Q_n=sqrt(n M d).

In the nonshort proof, every auxiliary series
comparison against a fixed whole D_j uses a sum
of actual output packet widths. For the median
probes use the local cap of section 2, and for
the other probes retain the former near-critical
geometric arguments. This gives the following
fully local version of that proof.

At stage 0 any away nonshort cap containing
the common atom in B_c must have an endpoint
in B_c: if both ends were outside B_c, the
connected actual cap arc meeting the bank would
traverse the whole bank, including BL_0. This
contradicts being away. All the caps are actual
simple proper oriented intervals with marked
endpoint atoms; we do not use a disconnected
aggregate base. At stage h the tracked positive
residue crests over BL_(h+1), which is in B_c.
The identical argument gives an actual endpoint
in B_c for its away class too. Thus at both
stages at most

    K=6b(2c+1)

localized modules suffice (disjointize by first
eligible ending atom). This bound does not need
to grow with the position of a remote median
probe.

For each such localized-away family the old
median split and six-probe order proof of
ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE section 3 is
unchanged. There are at most 2K directional
modules and <=12K probes in the test of their
upper halves. Prune bad gaps for 12K C.
The good disagreements have the distinct
auxiliary raw windows used by that proof,
and section 2 bounds their total energy by
24K M. Hence the union disagreement charge
is Q_(24K), from series against the entire
D_(j+1). The same alternating four-electrode
bound on the two median halves yields

    A_away = 4K+24K C+2Q_(24K).                (1)

This uses the actual one-step proper middle
old-limit chart for the auxiliary localization,
not the previous separate global cap.

The whole-critical-crest charge U=2C+Q_2,
the precritical charge Z=4C+2Q_2, and the
advancing full-limb charge 2C+Q_2 use only
old-proper probes on the specified near-critical
limbs and the actual critical-vertex deck pair.
Their target atoms are in BL_0,..,BL_(h+2)
or B_b, hence in B_(c^+).
The full suffix coherent atoms and two-bank
separator packets of SUFFIX_ANCHOR_RESPONSE
and AUXILIARY_GAP_RESPONSE likewise have
actual target crest atoms in BL_1,..,BL_(h+1)
(the two bank images in BL_1 and BL_2).
These are the original explicit target
anchor identifications; they do not depend
on arbitrary remote endpoint locations.
Thus every auxiliary cap other than the
median cap already uses M with no factor two.

Track the nonshort portion of A exactly as in
ANCHORED_PHASE_FREE_RESPONSE sections 4--5:
initial over-critical, away and precritical
charges; whole positive-crest advancement;
terminal away, over-critical, and orientation
charges; selected critical suffix/deep contact
or actual auxiliary; two-bank hits at cost 4R.
No singleton or unbreakability is needed.
In particular the tracked subfamily's charges
do not remove it from the entire primary-good
comparison G before calculating the gain.
All auxiliary metrics descend through the
good prefix charts on literal disjoint
parent windows.

Write

    E_a=sum_(r=1)^h C_(2^r)+12b*2^h*C_(2^h),
    B=E+8K+(48K+12+2h)C+E_a+4R,
    J=max(24K,N,2),
    a_0=max(2,(h+11)*sqrt(J)).

Collecting the previous nonshort accounting
with (1) in place of the old away estimate
gives the **local-cap nonshort inequality**

    W(A_nonshort)
        <= B+4Q_(24K)+(h+6)Q_2+Q_N
        <= B+a_0 sqrt(M(M-w+E)).              (2)

Every comparison width used is M_(c^+),
not M_*; the only new geometric ingredient
is the proper middle endpoint localization
of section 2. This is proved for A anchored
in B_c. It is not a claim about all packets
anchored anywhere in B_(c^+).

## 4. Local short program and expansion-gap estimate

Use the adaptive terminal destination and
guard-free width replacement from
GUARD_FREE_ANGULAR_JUMP. Every actual
macro-stage starting common short interval
has an atom in B_c. This remains true if
the initial maximizing atom is in any part
of B_c: in the positive middle jump range
we immediately use the jump, after the
nonshort filtering; negative-bank starts
translate toward the critical block; a
critical proper image has a near-critical
atom by the old-proper critical anchor
image. Its actual short interval may meet
one or two limbs, both within B_(c+1).
The conceptual intermediate short
intervals in a long jump are used only
for the deterministic orbit and visit
count, not as extra cap comparisons.

For an ordinary proper-image propagation
step on such a short input, both first/last
end probes have their old atoms in B_(c+1),
so their target atoms are in B_(c+2) or
B_b. Their raw packets and the common
primary image are capped by M. For the
saturation guard, choose the common
outside old atom **inside B_c** and outside
the union of possible short bases.
Such an atom exists: the union for a
common short atom is in at most three
consecutive limbs, and B_c has more than
three distinct limbs in this high-q range.
The same first/last/outside signed
response proof of ANCHORED_SHORT_JUMP
then uses only the local cap M.

The ordinary proper-image and saturation
charges are therefore those of the earlier
proof with M in place of M_*. The short
jump has cost 40(V+1) and output in B_c,
with no angular guard. Use the exact
interval nonreturn and nondecreasing
proper atom count from the earlier
geometric orbit theorem. The same bound
L_macro=(12b(18b-1)+2)(2b+16) on
macro-stages applies. A terminal whole-limb
crest has a near-bank atom at its first
appearance: on a noncritical shift it
is the translated near anchor, and on a
critical step its critical old-proper
atom image belongs to B_b. Equation (2)
applies at that stop. The saturated-image
stop is excluded by the local outside
guard after short filtering.

For clarity, define

    A_abs=max(E,B,2V+3C,40(V+1),2 Delta_loc,1),
    p=2 L_macro+4,
    t_p=1/(64(a_0+1)^2),
    t_i=t_(i+1)^2/(64(a_0+1)^2)  (i=p-1,...,0).

For M>0 normalize each current width by
u=(M-w)/M>=0 and eta=A_abs/M.
Equation (2), ordinary propagation, and
the guard-free jump give the same one-step
envelope

    u_next<=u+eta+a_0 sqrt(u+eta).

If u,eta<=t_i, the elementary backward
tolerance calculation gives u_next<=t_(i+1).
At every terminal test u,eta<=t_p, the
upper nonshort or saturation fraction is
<1/2 while 1-u>1/2. The zero-width and
empty radial-witness alternatives also
contradict those tolerances. If
M>A_abs/t_0, choose an actual near-bank
input A with

    w>M_c-t_0 M/2.

Since Delta_loc/M<t_0/2, its starting
deficit is <t_0. The finite macro program
would produce the contradiction just
described. Thus

    M_(c^+) <= C_b (R+V+1+Delta_loc).          (3)

The fixed constants depend only on b.
There is **no remote global supremum gap**
or terminal angular guard in (3). This is
a finite near-bank expansion-gap reduction,
not an assertion of uniform near-bank
maximality. In particular a packet with
its anchor in the added outer bank
B_(c^+) minus B_c cannot simply be used
as a new starting packet for the same
cap M; its primary sequence might leave
that larger bank. That is why Delta_loc
remains in the inequality.

## 5. Direct input-width comparison for localized Waves

Here use the exact initial control typing
of the earlier notes: a Borel simple proper
input A decomposed into at most k localized
modules with a common original ending atom,
and one module **leafwise** crested over
the whole BL_0. The latter qualification
is not inferred from an arbitrary aggregate
crest. Remove essential equal-ideal-end
loops at width <=1/2 using the span-one
band metric; contractible loops in a single
atom are not marked-admissible. Remove
marked-cut endpoints at width zero.
Disjointize the modules on original
supports.

In each localized module partition by
the actual oriented cap direction from
its specified ending atom E_0. Since a
marked-admissible path cannot be subordinate
to a run of at most two marked atoms,
the immediately next atom A_0 in this
direction is a strict crest atom. This
also holds for the long same-atom route.
The ending atom is on a fixed side of
the common crest A_0 and its limb is
either that of A_0 or a neighbor. Thus
each direction is a C_* packet with
one fixed **one-sided endpoint bound**.
We use the one-sided comparison even
when its other endpoint is arbitrarily
remote and its leaves are nonshort.

If A_0 belongs to B_c its width is
already <=M_c. Otherwise the anchor
limb k_0 satisfies c<k_0<q-c. Use the
actual proper six-limb band
BL_[k_0-2,k_0+3] and inner four-limb
band BL_[k_0-1,k_0+2], and jump to the
adaptive near-precritical k_* of the
guard-free theorem. The actual ending
atom E_0 is in the inner band on the
fixed side of the crest, so section 3
of GUARD_FREE_ANGULAR_JUMP applies.
The side needed here is dictated by
the localized end; it need not be
the side of the largest padding limb.
We use its general ratio estimate,
not the guard-free balanced version.

The destination crest and both padding
limbs belong to B_c. Let p_T be the
actual angular length of the relevant
padding limb, and g_T the distance
from the appropriate inner cut to the
midpoint of the target crest. Then
0<p_T<=1 and 0<g_T<=1. The ratio
allowance satisfies

    (2/pi) log(1+g_T/p_T)
      <= (2/pi) log(2/p_T)
      <= 2 M_c + (2/pi)(log 2+log(1/(2s))).

For the last inequality use the radial
lower witness over the whole padding
limb, of width m(p_T)<=M_c, and
log(1/p_T)<=pi m(p_T)+log(1/(2s)).
The witness over the actual whole
translated common atom interval is
also near-bank and has width <=M_c.
The one-sided forward inequality
therefore gives for this directional
localized packet

    W(packet) <= 3 M_c+40(V+1).               (4)

(The constant 40 safely absorbs the
log clipping costs and the 32(V+1)
absolute error in that inequality.)
This does not require a full transport
of all its paths. It holds in the
presence of extremely small terminal
padding: small padding itself gives
a wide near-bank radial packet, which
is enough for this constant-factor
initial comparison.

The leafwise over-critical module is
anchored in BL_0 and costs <=M_c.
There are at most 2k directional
localized modules. Subadditivity on
the original family gives

    W(A) <= (6k+1) M_c+80k(V+1)+1/2.          (5)

Thus the initial Wave-width application
does not require relocation of an
arbitrary maximizing remote C_* packet.
The source's input controllability k
may be fixed at its proof-required
value; the inequality is explicit
in k. It does not establish that a
chosen source wave has this leafwise
decomposition or Borel realization
without their stated native typing.

Combining (3) and (5) yields

    W(A) <= C_(b,k) (R+V+1+Delta_loc).         (6)

If Delta_loc is O_b(R+V+1), this proves
the desired conditional vertical-width
alternative at sufficiently large
absolute input width relative to R+1.
Downstream native source use and the
exact marking realization still require
verification.

## 6. The remaining local angular test

The angular envelope of
ANGULAR_ANCHORED_ENVELOPE applies to any
two nested banks. Put ell_c=min length
of any actual atom in B_c and
ell_plus=min length of any atom in
B_(c^+). Then

    | Delta_loc -
      (1/pi) log(min(2s,ell_c)/min(2s,ell_plus)) |
      <= C_env.

Both banks have a number of limbs and
marking atoms bounded only in terms
of b. In particular no global remote
maximizing atom or reflected long-range
padding ratio is needed in (6).
If the smallest atom of the enlarged
bank is already in the smaller bank,
the gap is <=C_env immediately.

The remaining native quantitative task
is now comparability of the minimum
atom sizes in these **two bounded
near-critical banks**, or another
response to their large gap. The fact
that the bank sizes are bounded does
not itself give angular comparability.
A local cap on B_(c^+) cannot be
silently assumed stable for primary
starts at its outer boundary. Nor is
there a proof here of the original
literal maximal-subset A.3.2 operation.
The actual proper middle charts,
saturated carrier and two-bank host
realizations, exact interval orbit,
initial witness typing and downstream
amplification are still explicit
hypotheses/acceptance gates.

This is a narrower conditional route
than the global M_* bound. It does not
promote a canonical ledger entry,
stationary closure or MLC. The finite
checker tests the corridor endpoint
logic, the local bank indices and the
algebra; the analytic/native arguments
are written proofs. AI-assisted research;
independent specialist review pending.

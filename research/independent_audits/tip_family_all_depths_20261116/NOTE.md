# Primitive-tip tuning towers: what propagates, what does not, and one depth-three certificate

**Additive audit, 2026-11-16.** This note does **not** announce a depth-uniform KKL width theorem. Its unconditional new machine-checkable result is the rational interval certificate for **one** depth-three chart `(3,3,3)` (§4), together with an all-depth *conditional* marking lemma, and an application of *published* complex bounds to any **genuinely infinitely renormalizable real** limit. The general finite-depth physical geometry, a uniform modulus on the **preselected physical finite-tip round disks**, and a depth-uniform canonical-width sandwich remain **open**. The program proves inequalities about explicit polynomials and disks; it does not prove straightening, tuning, prime-end identification, KKL, or elliptic-integral extremal-length theorems.

## 1. The object and the exact induction hypotheses

Write `F_c(z)=z²+c`, `x_j(c)=F_c^j(0)`, `P_n(c)=x_{6n}(c)+x_{3n}(c)`, and `A_n(c)=∏_{j=1}^{n-1}2x_j(c)`. For a finite word `w=(p₁,…,p_k)`, each `p_j≥3`, let `n_j=∏_{i=1}^j p_i`. The **primitive tuning** parameter `c(w)` means the real parameter obtained from the designated primitive copies, with the successive renormalization straightenings prescribed by the remaining suffix. For example the existing depth-two audit defines `c(p₁,p₂)` by the `p₁`-return straightening to `F_{c_{p₂}}`. The last return is required to carry the selected real tip portrait. The existence and identification of such copies and their inverse straightenings are **Douady–Hubbard tuning/straightening inputs**, not consequences of `P_n=0` alone. Do **not** impose `P_{p₁}=…=P_{n_k}=0` at a single parameter: the outer tip is replaced by a tuned *copy*, not repeated as an exact orbit identity.

In particular a depth-three **algebraic** candidate satisfies `P_{n₃}(c)=0` on its chosen chart, not simultaneously `P_{n₁}(c)=P_{n₂}(c)=0`. For finite words in general, neither an arbitrary solution of `P_{n_k}=0` nor a bare list of periods defines the intended marked tuning branch; one needs the designated primitive copies, signed kneading, and the central quadratic-like restrictions.

We make the induction obligations explicit. For a given `c(w)` and each `j≤k`, let `G_j=F_c^{n_j}` and require **H(j)**:

1. A symmetric central polynomial-like restriction `G_j:V_j→U_j`, with `0∈V_j⊂⊂U_j`, proper of **degree two** (not degree `2^{n_j}`), with pairwise disjoint phase domains and nesting inside the preceding central component. For these **preselected** domains the modulus is positive but its value may depend on `w,j`; the *optimal modulus over all quadratic-like extensions*, in contrast, enjoys the universal bound of §3.
2. The real critical itinerary and branch orientation agree with the corresponding marked suffix under real straightening. For the terminal tip `j=k`, also require `P_{n_k}=0`, the strict O1/O2 cut order, a central proper *three-return*, and the period-three primitive real Hubbard portrait (the latter is a named external dynamical identification, not mere sign arithmetic).
3. For a *physical* KKL width assertion, additionally require actual slit prime ends, proper central embedding of the **whole closed round gate disk**, the correct KKL A.1/A.3.2 family, and the conformal full-plane-slit extremal-width comparison. These are **W(j)**, stronger than H(j) and than a positive annular modulus.

Here is the exact logical statement, rather than an unjustified unconditional all-depth claim.

**Conditional all-depth marking theorem.** If H(1),…,H(k) hold with the specified primitive real terminal tip, the **last** renormalization has the same *local* KKL 10-target/20-source marked combinatorial type as the original level-one and nine depth-two packets: one attachment, the matrices and proper paths in §2. This applies for any finite `k` and any `p_i≥3` satisfying those hypotheses. If W(k) also holds, its physical canonical widths satisfy the *case-specific* inequalities of §3. These statements neither assert W(k) for all words nor claim a uniform numerical width interval. For `k=1` the hypotheses and numeric sandwich are established by [THEOREM.md](../../tip_family_theorem/THEOREM.md); for `(p₁,p₂)∈{3,4,5}²` they are established by the [merged depth-two audit](../tip_family_renormalization_depth_20261116/NOTE.md); for `(3,3,3)` the new arithmetic and imported identifications are detailed in §4.

**Induction proof (qualified).** The abstract finite tower of primitive degree-two restrictions is supplied by classical **iterated Douady–Hubbard tuning**, and its *optimal* moduli are uniformly bounded below, **also for finite words**, by the published complex-bounds theorem in §3: the period at every selected step is at least three, so the doubling-cusp exception cannot arise. This is an external theorem consequence, not a new disk certificate. The base for the *specified physical-chart geometry* is the all-`p` single-level theorem: its explicit source-disk Rouché gap exceeds `1/25`, and the *separate normalized* `3p`-return gap exceeds `1/8`. At step `j→j+1`, the designated primitive tuning copy and the Douady–Hubbard straightening theorem provide the *topological/combinatorial suffix*, including the identified real kneading, once H(j+1)'s polynomial-like restriction is available. Primitive disjointness and the real tip Hubbard tree then identify the five real cuts and prime ends as in the cited single-level proof. The KKL marked-circle theorem is applied to that local slit, and the finite table calculation is independent of the number of earlier ancestors. **This is not an induction proof of the missing *physical-domain* premise**: the all-`p` bounds are in the original `c≈−2` physical chart and do not give uniform Rouché error, root-chart or gate bounds for a successive renormalized, non-polynomial quadratic-like germ. The **abstract** arrow “existing primitive renormalization → positive *universal optimal modulus*” is supplied by published theory; the **unproved** arrow is “universal optimal modulus → uniformly controlled *chosen round disks, marked cuts and whole KKL Waves*.” In particular, `H(j) → W(j+1)` is not established; assuming it would be circular. Classical tuning gives some polynomial-like restrictions, not the explicit universal disks or margins of this audit, nor uniform canonical widths.

## 3. Complex bounds, physical widths, and **quantified loss**

**Infinitely renormalizable real limits.** Let `𝓜_w` be nested designated **real primitive** Mandelbrot copies for an infinite word `p=(p₁,p₂,…)`. The nested compact sets have nonempty intersection; select `c_∞` there. When the point is a nondegenerate infinitely renormalizable real quadratic with the indicated primitive combinatorics (classical tuning/rigidity input), **Lyubich–Yampolsky prove an *absolute* complex a priori bound**, not merely a parameter-dependent one. Their *Dynamics of quadratic polynomials: complex bounds for real maps*, *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* **47** (1997), 1219–1255, **Theorem 1.1 and the explicitly stated “Complex Bounds Theorem” on p. 1220**, DOI [10.5802/aif.1598](https://doi.org/10.5802/aif.1598), state (combining their result with the earlier large-essential-period work) that there exists a **universal** `μ₀>0` such that for every infinitely renormalizable **real quadratic polynomial** and all its renormalization levels `j`,

`mod(R^j F_c) ≥ μ₀`,

where `mod` means the *supremum over admissible quadratic-like extensions*. In fact their **Complex Bounds Theorem is stated for any finitely `j`-times renormalizable real quadratic as well**, with the sole exception when the last renormalization is a near-cusp **doubling** type. The exception is absent for the chosen **primitive** tuning stages `p_j≥3`, including the terminal period-three tip renormalization. Consequently, *conditional on the standard tuning construction identifying the consecutive primitive stages*, **the same absolute `μ₀` bounds the optimal moduli for all such finite tuned parameters, all levels `j`, and all infinite real limits**. For infinitely renormalizable maps the authors explicitly remark that no near-cusp exception occurs. Their theorem covers unbounded real combinatorics as well as bounded, but it **does not give an effective numerical value of `μ₀` or exhibit uniform margins on our preassigned round disks**. This improves the weaker map-by-map statement `∃ μ(c)>0` but does not yield the KKL width target. The same universal modulus is known by the independent real-complex-bounds approaches of **Levin–van Strien**, *Local connectivity of the Julia set of real polynomials*, *Annals of Mathematics* **147** (1998), 471–541, DOI [10.2307/120958]. Their headline conclusion is **JLC**, not MLC. Related real bounds and bounded-type foundations: **Sullivan**, “Bounds, quadratic differentials, and renormalization conjectures,” *AMS Centennial Publications* **2** (1992); **de Melo–van Strien**, *One-Dimensional Dynamics* (Springer, 1993), **Chapter VI, §2** on real bounds and **Chapter IV** on distortion/Koebe. Do not silently identify Sullivan's bounded-type result alone with Lyubich–Yampolsky's general result.

If the nested tuning copies shrink to one parameter, then the sequence of uniquely specified finite tuned points converges to it; *singleton intersection* is a rigidity/shrinking input, not deducible solely from the compact intersection or the margin `1/25`. The published **absolute `μ₀` covers both finite non-doubling towers and infinite real limits**, but solely for the **maximal modulus among possible extensions**, not the specific physical disks `U_j=D(0,R_j)`. In particular, even this all-depth universal modulus does **not** control the marked source/target prime-end rectangles, the actual radial gate or the unrestricted supremum over KKL Waves; it does **not** supply a uniform canonical Wave width.

**What a quasiconformal comparison would actually yield.** Suppose *additionally* that for a fixed word the marking and admissible path families are transported at each step by a quasiconformal map with measured dilatation `K_j≥1`. The standard extremal-length inequality on a **specified carried curve family** is

`(∏_{j=1}^{k−1} K_j)^{-1} Width(Γ) ≤ Width(h(Γ)) ≤ (∏_{j=1}^{k−1} K_j) Width(Γ)`.  (★)

This is a precise multiplicative accounting of any depth loss for that comparison. If all `K_j≤K>1`, its worst-case bounds deteriorate at most as `K^{k−1}` above and `K^{−(k−1)}` below; for `K_j=1+ε_j`, the product is at most `exp(Σ ε_j)`. Thus transporting `0.25<L≤W≤U<0.775` by only (★) would give **at best** `0.25/(∏K_j)< W_carried < 0.775(∏K_j)`, not the same sandwich. Moreover, an arbitrary hybrid conjugacy need **not** carry the marked slit prime ends or map the *entire* A.1 Wave class bijectively onto the next one; (★) alone is **not** an inequality for the two unrestricted suprema `W_j*`. Neither a uniform `K_j`, a summable `Σ log K_j`, nor the required functoriality is proved in the source audits or in this note. Hence **no numeric rate of decay, no depth-uniform lower/upper constants, and no limit of optimal canonical widths are established**. Real complex bounds should not be mistaken for this much stronger transport estimate.

With the physical terminal cuts and gate established *for a given word*, the previously merged canonical-width sandwich gives the **case-dependent** valid inequality

`W_rad(w) ≤ W_{w;2}* ≤ W_w* ≤ Q_plane(w)`,

where the lower value is the full-disk round witness with **both** proper A.3.2 children, and the upper value is the complete lifted-plane-slit extremal envelope, **not** a selected round annulus. The universal numerical `0.25…0.775` step is therefore a **separate physical certification problem**; it is not inherited from a modulus bound.

The published **Dudko–Lyubich**, “MLC at Feigenbaum points,” *Publ. Math. IHÉS* **143** (2026), 97–141, DOI [10.5802/pmihes.2](https://doi.org/10.5802/pmihes.2), covers **bounded-type** infinitely renormalizable quadratic parameters; Lyubich's large-type real theory covers the complementary cases under its stated hypotheses. This audit is entirely **within existing MLC developments for real parameters**. It makes **no new assertion of local connectivity of the Mandelbrot set** at any parameter, and in particular no claim about MLC for unrestricted complex combinatorics.

## 4. New bridge lemma: no residual A.1 or width identification hypothesis

**Lemma (selected marked-slit identity).** For every \(p\ge3\) in
(1), the identity maps from the *explicit model* slit surfaces to
the *actual KKL* surfaces extend to isomorphisms of their marked
Carathéodory ideal closures. They induce inverse bijections between
A.1-admissible proper paths, relative-endpoint arc classes, simple
laminations and Waves with specified bases/ends/crests. For each
corresponding leaf family \(\mathcal F\),
\[
 W_{\mathrm{KKL},\Omega_p}(\mathcal F)
    =W_{\mathrm{model},\Omega_p}(\mathcal F),\qquad
 W_{\mathrm{KKL},\Omega'_p}(\mathcal F)
    =W_{\mathrm{model},\Omega'_p}(\mathcal F).
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular the physical A.1 parent and independent source-child
suprema are **exactly** the \(P_p,C_p^+,C_p^-\) defined in the earlier
gain notes, without a new assumption.

**Proof.** First, both descriptions of the domain have the *same
point set and same complex coordinate* by (1). They have locally
connected boundary (round \(\partial U_p\), Jordan \(\partial V_p\),
straight compact slit). Cutting the annulus by a crosscut produces a
Jordan-type slit domain; Carathéodory extension, followed by regluing
the crosscut, identifies its prime-end compactification with the
KKL ideal circles \((\partial\mathbb D\setminus\Lambda)/\Delta\).
T(iii) identifies the **actual** marked inner circles, including
their cyclic order, and the unique unmarked outer whole-component
circle. Consequently the ordinary identity on the open surface
extends to the identity on these *same* marked ideal circles, not
just to an unspecified cyclic permutation.

KKL Appendix A.1 defines a proper path by its ideal endpoints and
allows it only if it is not subordinate to any multi-interval of
length at most two. Subordination means homotopy relative to the
endpoints into **every** collar neighborhood of that ideal arc.
The marked-closure identity carries all collar neighborhoods,
relative-endpoint homotopies and atom counts to themselves, both
ways. It therefore preserves precisely the A.1 path set.
Simplicity, disjointness and the lamination property are unchanged.
The *smallest* subordinate multi-interval, the two end
multi-intervals, and their set difference (the crest) are therefore
unchanged. This applies to **all** admissible candidates, not just
the one finite family of crossing isotopy classes counted in T(iv).
Taking unions and suprema gives the same admissible-Wave optimization
sets, including the independent rather than simultaneous source
suprema.

For the width statement, the map is conformal (indeed the identity)
rather than merely topological. For a conformal change of coordinate
\(w=\phi(z)\), transport an admissible density by
\(\rho_w(\phi(z))=\rho_z(z)/|\phi'(z)|\).
Then \(\int_\gamma\rho_z|dz|=\int_{\phi\gamma}
\rho_w|dw|\) and
\(\int\rho_z^2\,dA_z=\int\rho_w^2\,dA_w\).
Taking infima over admissible densities in both directions proves
(5). In particular the gain notes' positive rescaling
\(\xi=\lambda_pz\), \(\lambda_p=-A_p>0\), does not alter widths
or reverse cyclic real order. The **negative** scale \(A_pz\)
does reverse real *order of cut coordinates* (though it is
holomorphic); this is why the gain proofs always distinguish the
two normalized conventions. QED.

**Boundary of the lemma.** The statement would be **false** if one
replaced (1) by an arbitrary homeomorphic slit surface: extremal
width is not invariant under general homeomorphisms. Nor does a
finite atom table prove (5) in the absence of (1). The new bridge is
precisely the analytic/topological implication that was not stated in
the identification theorem's list (i)–(iv).

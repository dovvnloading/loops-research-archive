# Actual primitive-tip family: one and only one collapsing response eigenvalue

**Status:** written analytic deduction from the archive's all-period actual realization and the new [rank-one theorem](RANK_ONE.md), with exact rational enclosure checks. H2_RESPONSE_REALIZATION and H3_COERCIVITY stay OPEN. No infinite renormalization tower is constructed.

## 1. Input and precise result

Use the actual selected real primitive-tip parameters of [all-period realization, (9), (13)–(19)](../real_primitive_tip_family_all_p_20261116/NOTE.md) for every p≥13, and the actual four-whole-component surface of [canonical width exclusion, §1](../canonical_width_attack_20261124/NOTE.md). Put q=4^(−p), A_p equal to the source's signed quadratic coefficient, w_j=A_p f_c^(jp)(0), and R_p=|A_p|2^(−p). The three slit electrodes are

    K_(p,0)=[−w_3,w_3],
    K_(p,1)=[−w_4,−w_1],
    K_(p,2)=[−w_2,−w_5].

The identity w_6=−w_3 is exact. In this coordinate the actual surface is D_(R_p) minus the union K_p of these three slits, and (3/20)2^p<R_p<(17/100)2^p. The canonical buffered WAD of this *minimal whole-component marking* is identically zero by the cited canonical exclusion theorem. We consume these archive results as written; we do not reprove their complex-dynamical or hyperbolic source inputs.

Let τ_* be the unique zero of g_τ^6(0)+g_τ^3(0) in J=[−1.795,−1.785], where g_τ(w)=w²+τ. Put u_j=g_(τ_*)^j(0) and define K_* from the same three interval formulas with u_j instead of w_j. Write A_* for its exact exterior inner-electrode response and m_* for its harmonic value at infinity, as in RANK_ONE.md (the symbol A_* here is a quadratic form, not the signed return coefficient). Write c_*=c(K_*) and k_p=2π/log(R_p/c_*). Define on the original four-terminal space

    Q_p^*(y,x)=A_*(y)+k_p (x−m_*(y))².

**Theorem.** There exist C<∞ and p_0, independent of the terminal potential, such that for all p≥p_0,

    (1−C4^(−p)) Q_p^*(y,x) ≤ E_p(y,x)
                            ≤ (1+C4^(−p)) Q_p^*(y,x).       (1)

Here E_p is the exact physical response, not the canonical WAD or a graph proxy. On the Euclidean orthogonal complement of the constant vector let its eigenvalues be 0<λ_1(p)≤λ_2(p)≤λ_3(p). Then:

* λ_2(p), λ_3(p) ≥ 1/(320π) for every p≥13; they converge to the two positive eigenvalues of A_* on the inner zero-sum space.
* λ_1(p) = (4/3) k_p − (4/3) S_* k_p² + O(k_p³), where S_*≥0 is the explicit inner-response invariant in §5.
* Consequently p λ_1(p) → 8π/(3 log 2). The small eigenline converges to the line generated by the centered outer-electrode vector (−1,−1,−1,3)/4. In particular the loss of a spectral floor in this actual family has precisely one direction; it is the outer-neck direction, not an inner-electrode collapse.

The expansion uses the actual R_p; no asymptotic limit of the normalized return coefficient A_p/4^p is asserted.

## 2. Quantitative orbit geometry and the fixed limiting shape

The all-period source equations (15)–(18) give endpoint signs for F(τ)=g_τ^6(0)+g_τ^3(0), F'>2 on J, |d/dτ g_τ^j(0)|<25 for j≤5, and orbit errors

    E_0=0,   E_(j+1)=4E_j+88q.

These input bounds are for the exact normalized return on the source chart. At τ_p=A_p f_c^p(0), the true w_6+w_3=0. Hence |F(τ_p)|≤E_6+E_3=121968q. The intermediate value theorem, strict derivative lower bound and mean value theorem give

    |τ_p−τ_*| ≤ 60984q,
    |w_j−u_j| ≤ E_j+25·60984q    (1≤j≤5),
    |w_6−u_6|=|w_3−u_3|.

Thus all endpoints of K_p converge to the corresponding endpoints of K_* at rate O(q).

The endpoint geometry must not be inferred from strict inequalities at each finite p. The accompanying exact checker covers the *whole* interval J by 1000 equal rational closed cells. On each cell [a,b], start [l,h]=[0,0] and iterate exact interval squaring plus [a,b]; then enlarge the j-th resulting interval by E_j computed with q=4^(−13), its largest value in the admitted range. The resulting uniform strict enclosures (units 1/1000) are

    w_1 in (−1796,−1784)
    w_2 in (1400,1428)
    w_3 in (170,250)
    w_4 in (−1754,−1736)
    w_5 in (1220,1290)
    w_6 in (−306,−122).

The identical bounds contain the model orbit u_j (its error enlargement can only be smaller). These yield lengths of each of the three slits greater than ℓ=1/40, gaps between consecutive slits greater than ℓ, and containment in (−9/5,9/5). For example K_1 has length w_4−w_1>30/1000, K_2 has length w_2−w_5>110/1000, and K_0 has length 2w_3>340/1000. Thus K_* is a regular nondegenerate slit domain, not merely a formal limit with touching boundaries.

## 3. Uniform inner coercivity on the exact physical family

This estimate holds for any three disjoint real slit intervals of length≥ℓ lying in (−2,2). For a pair with centers a<b, take the upper semicircles centered at (a+b)/2, with radii s in [(b−a)/2−ℓ/4,(b−a)/2+ℓ/4]. They have endpoints on the two slits and their interiors are obstacle-free in the upper half-plane. The radii are positive because disjointness implies b−a≥ℓ, and are <2 because both endpoints lie in (−2,2). The entire semicircles lie in D_2: a circle centered on the real line has maximal modulus at one of its real endpoints.

For a trial potential u with the stated constant slit traces, the angular Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on almost every such semicircle gives

    ∫_0^π |∂_θ u(s,θ)|² dθ ≥ (y_i−y_j)²/π.

This follows first for smooth functions and then for Sobolev functions by the trace theorem and Fubini on the half-annulus; the two straight trace segments lie strictly inside the slit banks. Integrating the angular part of polar energy yields

    E(u) ≥ (y_i−y_j)² ∫ ds/(πs)
         ≥ ℓ/(4π) (y_i−y_j)².

Apply this separately to pairs (0,1) and (0,2). The carriers need not be disjoint; taking the maximum, which is at least half the sum, is valid without adding overlapping energies. For z=y−mean(y)·1, the elementary identity 3||z||²=Σ_(i<j)(y_i−y_j)² and (y_1−y_2)²≤2[(y_0−y_1)²+(y_0−y_2)²] give

    A_(K_p)(y) ≥ δ||z||²,    δ=ℓ/(8π)=1/(320π).             (2)

The identical bound holds for K_*. It holds also directly for E_p since the half-annuli lie inside D_2. On the four-terminal quotient the two-dimensional subspace {(z,0):Σz_i=0} therefore has Rayleigh quotient≥δ; the max-min principle gives λ_2,λ_3≥δ. The remaining direction cannot also be uniformly coercive because the earlier radial trial, or §5 below, gives λ_1→0.

## 4. All-potential exponential comparison with one fixed shape

We give an actual domain identification, not pointwise convergence of abstract matrices. Let d_p(s) be the continuous piecewise affine displacement on the real line taking the six ordered slit endpoints of K_* to those of K_p, and taking value zero at both anchors ±9/5 and outside the anchors. Endpoint convergence and fixed positive distances between consecutive interpolation nodes imply ||d_p||_∞+Lip(d_p)=O(q). Let χ(t)=max(0,1−2|t|) and

    Φ_p(s+it)=s+χ(t)d_p(s)+it.

For sufficiently large p, the derivative of its first coordinate in s is positive a.e. and bounded below by 1−Lip(d_p)>0. At fixed t it is a strictly increasing surjection of R onto R, and the inverse is Lipschitz by the same lower bound and the Lipschitz dependence on t. Thus Φ_p is a global bi-Lipschitz orientation-preserving map, sends each labeled slit of K_* exactly onto its counterpart, and is the identity outside [−9/5,9/5]×[−1/2,1/2], strictly inside D_2. Almost everywhere ||DΦ_p−I||=O(q); the two singular values lie in [1−O(q),1+O(q)] and their ratio is 1+O(q). Consequently change of variables in planar Dirichlet energy, in both directions, gives distortion at most 1+O(q), uniformly on all trial functions. The slit tips do not affect the change of variables (a.e. interior statement); the bank traces are preserved by the bi-Lipschitz identification along each open slit. The analytic compactification and ordinary planar finite-energy formulation agree here: local square-root coordinates preserve two-dimensional Dirichlet energy; bounded clipped trials are H¹ there, and removing finitely many tip points costs no energy (logarithmic cutoffs). Equivalently the variational comparison can be taken on bounded trials, since clipping to the electrode range never increases energy.

In particular, writing E_(R_p,K_*) for the exact Dirichlet response of the fixed shape with outer radius R_p, the identification preserves all four electrode labels and is the identity at the outer circle, giving

    (1+O(q))^(-1) E_(R_p,K_*) ≤ E_p ≤ (1+O(q)) E_(R_p,K_*).

The rank-one theorem with r=2 bounds E_(R_p,K_*) between Q_p^* and coth(log(R_p/2)) Q_p^*. Its relative error is 2(2/R_p)²/[1−(2/R_p)²]=O(4^(−p)), by the actual radius estimate. Combining the inequalities proves (1). No prior control on the smallest eigenvalue is used; the estimate holds uniformly for p-dependent potentials, not just fixed test vectors.

## 5. Spectrum and second term

Let e=(−1,−1,−1,3)/4, so ||e||²=3/4. Every vector in the four-terminal zero-sum space uniquely decomposes orthogonally as u e/||e||+(z,0) where Σz_i=0. Put α=4/3. On the two-dimensional inner zero-sum space let H be the symmetric matrix of A_* in an orthonormal basis. By (2), H≥δI. Let w be the Riesz vector of m_* on that space: m_*(z)=w^T z. The maximum principle gives ||w||≤1. Since m_* fixes constants, Q_p^* in these coordinates has matrix

    [ α k_p         −sqrt(α) k_p w^T ]
    [ −sqrt(α)k_p w   H+k_p ww^T    ].                     (3)

The limit as k_p→0 is diag(0,H), so the two larger eigenvalues converge to the eigenvalues of H and the smallest tends to zero (min-max, or the operator norm of the O(k_p) perturbation). Its smallest eigenvalue μ_p is positive and at most αk_p by the trial (u,z)=(1,0). For p sufficiently large αk_p<δ/2. The bottom block minus μ_p I is then invertible with inverse norm≤2/δ. The eigenvector has u≠0, since a pure bottom-block eigenvector has eigenvalue≥δ. Eliminate z from the exact eigenvector equations:

    μ_p = α k_p − α k_p² w^T (H+k_p ww^T−μ_p I)^(-1)w.

The resolvent identity shows that the inverse in this formula differs from H^(-1) by O(k_p), since μ_p=O(k_p). Hence with the intrinsic invariant

    S_*=w^T H^(-1)w≥0,

we get μ_p=αk_p−αS_*k_p²+O(k_p³). The min-max principle applied to (1) gives |λ_1(p)−μ_p|≤C q μ_p=O(qk_p); this is absorbed in O(k_p³) since log R_p=p log 2+O(1) and q decays exponentially. It also gives convergence of the two larger eigenvalues. The actual small eigenline converges to the e line: for a unit minimizing eigenvector decompose as above, (2) gives δ||z||²≤λ_1(p)→0, so |u|→1. The spectral gap separates that line for sufficiently large p. Finally k_p=2π/(p log 2+O(1)) proves the stated limit.

A further scalar corollary of (1), substituting (y,x)=(0,1), is

    E_p(0,1) = [2π/log(R_p/c_*)] (1+O(4^(−p))).

Thus the shape-dependent capacity supplies the constant correction to logarithmic outer resistance: 2π/E_p(0,1)=log R_p−log c_*+O(p4^(−p)). This is stronger than a first-order 1/p estimate and makes the coupling to the fixed limiting physical geometry explicit.

# Adversarial audit

This file attacks the proof architecture. A failed attack does not by itself prove a
claim; it records which obvious counterexamples have been excluded. A successful
attack becomes either a no-go theorem or a new explicit hypothesis.

## Detailed audit: finite-horizon inflation

### Attack FH-1 — inclusion failure

For closed stage markings \(\Upsilon_j,\Upsilon_{j+1}\), the claimed inclusion
\[
  \widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus f^{-1}(\Upsilon_{j+1})
  \hookrightarrow
  \widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\Upsilon_j
\]
exists exactly when
\[
  \Upsilon_j\subset f^{-1}(\Upsilon_{j+1}).
\]
Thus a finite list of orbit points is insufficient unless it is forward closed through
the declared horizon. This is a set-theoretic prerequisite, before covering theory or
complex geometry enters.

### Attack FH-2 — wrong lift

Even when the complement inclusion exists, an immersion between chosen relative covers
is not automatic. If the source and target relative covers correspond to subgroups
\(G'_j\le\pi_1(X'_j)\) and \(G_j\le\pi_1(X_j)\), the inclusion lifts only if
\[
  (i_j)_*(G'_j)\subset G_j
\]
after choosing compatible basepoints. DL25 obtains the relevant compatibility from its
local-saturation setup. A virtual construction must reproduce it at each stage.

### Attack FH-3 — extra core lift

A lift can exist while failing to be \(\iota\)-proper: another sheet may map into the
declared core. The construction must prove
\[
  \iota^{-1}(H_j)=H'_j
\]
on the selected source, not merely \(\iota(H'_j)=H_j\).

### Attack FH-4 — width freedom

For \(R>1\), set
\[
  U_R=\mathbb D_R,\qquad V_R=\mathbb D_{R^2},\qquad
  f_R(z)=z^2,\qquad\iota_R(z)=z,
\]
and use \(K=\overline{\mathbb D}\). Then
\[
 f_R^{-1}(K)=K=\iota_R^{-1}(K),
\]
and this is an ordinary quadratic-like map, hence stronger than the requested
finite-horizon topology. Nevertheless,
\[
 \operatorname{mod}(V_R\setminus K)
   =\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(R^2)=\frac{\log R}{\pi},
\qquad
 W_R=\frac{\pi}{\log R}.
\]
The topology and combinatorics are constant while \(W_R\) is unbounded as
\(R\downarrow1\). Therefore no topological finite-horizon theorem can include a
universal width bound.

### Attack FH-5 — horizon leakage

For every Wave, Wanderers, quasi-additivity, or covering estimate, list:

- the precise pullback length;
- the selected source component at each step;
- all critical values met;
- the image of every boundary access;
- the covering-embedding property invoked;
- the extremal family before and after pullback.

“The stopping time is long enough” is not an audit entry.

## Constant attack protocol

Every proposed transition estimate is tested against:

1. \(s_n\) growing faster than any prescribed function;
2. continued-fraction entries alternating between \(1\) and extremely large values;
3. accumulated entropy positive but divided into arbitrarily many tiny increments;
4. unboundedly many neutral/primitive chart changes;
5. a long virtual-molecule chain with unbounded raw marking cardinality;
6. rescaling an otherwise identical ql model as in Attack FH-4.

Any constant depending on period, rotation number, horizon, corridor length, or number
of chart changes is recorded explicitly and is not advertised as universal.

## Circularity attack protocol

Reject a proof step if it:

- obtains virtual-hull compactness from the a priori bound currently being proved;
- uses shrinking parapuzzle pieces to prove the phase–parameter theorem needed to make
  them shrink;
- invokes no invariant line fields before constructing the controlled conjugacy to
  which the theorem applies;
- selects a bounded-width localization by minimizing over a compact family whose
  compactness is the desired conclusion;
- imports the MLC consequence of a source as an input to the a priori-bound part.

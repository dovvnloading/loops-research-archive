## 3. Frozen direct-decoration pair

Fix one of the two Kahn--Lyubich direct-decoration branches.  Write

\[
  Q=Q_a,\qquad
  P=Y^{(n-1)q+1},\qquad
  g=f^q|_Q:Q\longrightarrow P ,
  \qquad a\in\{L,R\}.
\]

By construction, \(Q\) is a pullback component of \(P\), \(Q\subset P\),
and \(g\) is univalent and onto \(P\).  Let

\[
  K_Q=Q\cap K(f),\qquad K_P=P\cap K(f).
\]

Let \(\mathbf Q,\mathbf P\) be the pseudo-pieces of `psiql.tex`.  Naturality
gives two maps with the same source and target:

\[
  \mathbf g:\mathbf Q\longrightarrow\mathbf P,
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol\iota:\mathbf Q\longrightarrow\mathbf P.
\]

Here \(\mathbf g\) is the covering induced by \(g\), and
\(\boldsymbol\iota\) is the immersion induced by the inclusion \(Q\subset
P\).  Because \(g\) has degree one, \(\mathbf g\) is a degree-one
holomorphic covering, hence a biholomorphism.  This identifies the covering
pullback \( \mathbf g^*\mathcal I\) once a target ideal marking is fixed.
It says nothing by itself about
\(\boldsymbol\iota^*\mathcal I\).

We retain the orientation

\[
  \mathbf Q
  \overset{\mathbf g}{\underset{\boldsymbol\iota}{\rightrightarrows}}
  \mathbf P.
\]

All subsequent statements in this file concern this fixed branch.  No
uniformity in \(q,n\), satellite period, or a complete Wave horizon is
implicit.

## 4. Fixed-branch embedding obstruction and conditional criterion

### Proposition 4.1

For the fixed direct-decoration branch in Section 3, the component map
from source tiles to target tiles is bijective.  If, in addition,

\[
 \tag{TI}
 L_i:\widehat A_{s,i}\longrightarrow\widehat A_{t,j(i)}
 \quad\text{is injective for every source tile }i,
\]

then the glued immersion
\(\boldsymbol\iota:\mathbf Q\to\mathbf P\) is an interior holomorphic
embedding.  Full reflection of the entire target overlap,

\[
 L_i^{-1}\!\left(\tilde e_{j(i)}(P_{j(i)})\right)=e_i(Q_i),
\tag{FOR}
\]

is not required.  The checked KL source proves that the \(L_i\) are
immersions and that the overlap square commutes on \(e_i(Q_i)\), but does
not prove `(TI)` for a general \(\psi\)-ql stage.

This proposition remains a valid conditional gluing theorem.  Its
antecedent is false for the full canonical-localization stage identified
in Section 1.  Nothing in the proof below converts it into a
full-canonical embedding.

### Proof

We separate the proof into the component, universal-cover, and quotient
steps.

#### Step 1: the dynamical branch bijects the complementary components

The map \(g:Q\to P\) is a homeomorphism.  We claim

\[
  g(K_Q)=K_P. \tag{4.1}
\]

If \(x\in K_Q\), forward invariance of the filled Julia set gives
\(g(x)\in K(f)\cap P=K_P\).  Conversely, let \(y\in K_P\).  Because
\(g:Q\to P\) is onto and one-to-one, there is a unique \(x\in Q\) with
\(g(x)=y\).  Complete backward invariance of the filled Julia set along a
defined polynomial branch gives \(x\in K(f)\), hence \(x\in K_Q\).
This proves (4.1).

Restricting the homeomorphism therefore gives

\[
  g:Q\setminus K_Q \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} P\setminus K_P. \tag{4.2}
\]

The two complements have finitely many components by the pseudo-piece
construction.  Thus (4.2) induces a bijection

\[
 \pi_0(Q\setminus K_Q)\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
 \pi_0(P\setminus K_P). \tag{4.3}
\]

For the inclusion \(Q\subset P\), KL define a component map

\[
 j:\pi_0(Q\setminus K_Q)\longrightarrow
   \pi_0(P\setminus K_P)
\]

and prove that it is surjective.  The finite source and target sets have
equal cardinality by (4.3).  Hence

\[
 j\ \text{is bijective}. \tag{4.4}
\]

This is special to the fixed univalent pullback pair.  It does not assert
that the component map for an arbitrary nested pair of puzzle pieces is
injective.

For later use, let \(P_j\) be a target complementary component.  Since

\[
 Q\setminus K_Q=Q\cap(P\setminus K_P),
\]

we have

\[
 Q\cap P_j
 =
 \bigcup_{\{k:\,j(k)=j\}}Q_k.
\tag{4.4a}
\]

Indeed, every point on the left lies in \(Q\setminus K_Q\), hence in one
source component whose inclusion component is \(P_j\); the reverse
inclusion is the definition of the component map.  Bijectivity of \(j\)
therefore gives

\[
 Q\cap P_{j(i)}=Q_i.
\tag{4.4b}
\]

#### Step 2: the missing tile-injectivity premise

Let

\[
 A_s=\mathbf U^{\operatorname{depth}Q}\setminus K(f),
 \qquad
 A_t=\mathbf U^{\operatorname{depth}P}\setminus K(f).
\]

The earlier version of this proof asserted a physical inclusion

\[
  h:A_s\hookrightarrow A_t \tag{4.5}
\]

and then used injectivity of \(h\) to prove injectivity of every lift.
That assertion is not licensed in the general KL setting.  KL II,
`psiql.tex`, lines 198--206, supplies an ambient **immersion** and induced
tile immersions.  It does not identify the bold domains with nested
physical subsets.

If an independently established physical inclusion (4.5) is available
and preserves the positively oriented winding generator, then

\[
 h_*:\pi_1(A_s)\cong\mathbb Z
 \longrightarrow \pi_1(A_t)\cong\mathbb Z
\]

is the identity after compatible orientation choices, and in particular
is injective.

For a source component \(Q_i\), let

\[
 L_i:\widehat A_{s,i}\longrightarrow
       \widehat A_{t,j(i)}
\]

be the induced tile immersion.  Under the additional physical-inclusion
hypothesis, suppose \(L_i(x)=L_i(y)\).  Projection and injectivity of
\(h\) imply that the source projections of \(x,y\) coincide.  Hence

\[
  y=T_s^k x
\]

for a source deck transformation \(T_s^k\).  Equivariance of the lift gives

\[
  L_i(y)=T_t^{h_*(k)}L_i(x).
\]

The target deck action is free.  Since \(h_*\) is injective and
\(L_i(y)=L_i(x)\), we obtain \(k=0\), so \(x=y\).  Therefore

\[
  L_i\ \text{is injective for every }i. \tag{4.6}
\]

This proves `(TI)` only in the additional physical-inclusion model.  For
a general KL \(\psi\)-ql immersion, the base map in the projection square
is only an immersion.  The deck calculation itself can nevertheless be
completed: `actual_kl_tile_injectivity_gate.md`, Theorem 4.1 proves that,
in degree one, the universal-cover tile lift is injective if and only if
the bold base-annulus immersion is injective.

For the full canonical primitive localization, that equivalence is now a
negative certificate rather than an open reduction.  The
excess-component class

\[
 [\gamma]\in a_*^{-1}(H_t)\setminus H_s
\tag{4.6a}
\]

forces noninjectivity of the full localized bold base-annulus map and its
full tile lifts; it does not make the underlying physical inclusion
noninjective.  Kahn I, `psiql2.tex`, lines 87--98, proves injectivity only
after restricting to a sufficiently thin inner collar.  Such a collar
remains a conditional replacement, not evidence for global tile
injectivity.

#### Step 3: `(TI)` excludes all quotient collisions

Use the KL quotient presentations

\[
 \mathbf Q=Q\sqcup_{e_i}\widehat A_{s,i},
 \qquad
 \mathbf P=P\sqcup_{\tilde e_j}\widehat A_{t,j}.
\]

We check every possible collision between representatives.

1. **Central--central.**  On the central pieces,
   \(\boldsymbol\iota\) is the planar inclusion \(Q\hookrightarrow P\), so
   it is injective.
2. **Same tile.**  A collision inside one source tile is excluded by
   `(TI)`.
3. **Different tiles.**  By (4.4), different source tiles map to different
    target tiles.  KL prove that distinct target tile images are disjoint
    outside their central overlaps, so no new collision occurs there.
4. **Tile--central.**  Suppose a representative \(x\) in source tile \(i\)
    and a central point \(z\in Q\) have the same target point.  Such a
    collision can occur only in the target gluing overlap
    \(P_{j(i)}\).  Thus there is \(p\in P_{j(i)}\) with
    \[
      L_i(x)=\tilde e_{j(i)}(p)
    \]
    and whose target central representative is the inclusion image of
    \(z\).  The target central copy embeds in the quotient, and the
    central map is the literal inclusion, so \(p=z\).  In particular,
    \[
      z\in Q\cap P_{j(i)}=Q_i
    \]
    by (4.4b).  Commutation on the declared overlap gives
    \[
      L_i(e_i(z))=\tilde e_{j(i)}(z)=L_i(x).
    \]
    Condition `(TI)` now gives \(x=e_i(z)\).  Hence \(x\) and \(z\) were
    already equivalent in the source quotient.

Under `(TI)`, every collision in the target is therefore already a source
gluing equivalence.  Thus \(\boldsymbol\iota\) is globally injective.  KL
prove it is a holomorphic immersion.  An injective holomorphic immersion
between Riemann surfaces is a holomorphic embedding onto its image.  This
proves the conditional assertion. \(\square\)

The argument proves the exact collision-relative equality

\[
 L_i^{-1}\!\left(\tilde e_{j(i)}(Q_i)\right)=e_i(Q_i).
\tag{4.4c}
\]

The larger equality `(FOR)` may fail at a point lying over
\(P_{j(i)}\setminus Q\) without creating a quotient collision: the target
central mate of that point is not in the image of the source central
piece.  The complete equivalence-relation and relative fiber-product
formulation is proved in `kl_relative_overlap_cartesianity.md`.

### Proposition 4.2: degree one and immersion do not imply `(TI)`

For every \(h>0\), there is a taller strip \(\Pi(H)\) and a
\(\mathbb Z\)-equivariant locally univalent holomorphic map

\[
 F:\Pi(h)\longrightarrow\Pi(H),
 \qquad
 F(z+1)=F(z)+1,
\]

which is not injective.  Hence its annular quotient is a degree-one
holomorphic immersion with a same-tile self-collision.

### Proof

Choose \(a>e^{2\pi h}\) and put

\[
 F_a(z)=z+\frac{a}{2\pi i}e^{2\pi iz}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then \(F_a(z+1)=F_a(z)+1\), while

\[
 F_a'(z)=1+a e^{2\pi iz}.
\]

For \(0<\operatorname{Im}z<h\),
\[
 |a e^{2\pi iz}|=a e^{-2\pi\operatorname{Im}z}
 >a e^{-2\pi h}>1,
\]
so \(F_a'\ne0\).

For \(0<d<1\), define

\[
 r(d)=\frac{\pi d}{a\sin(\pi d)}.
\]

As \(d\downarrow0\), \(r(d)\to1/a<e^{-2\pi h}\), while
\(r(d)\to+\infty\) as \(d\uparrow1\).  Continuity therefore gives a
\(d\in(0,1)\) with

\[
 e^{-2\pi h}<r(d)<1.
\]

Choose \(z\) modulo \(\mathbb Z\) by

\[
 e^{2\pi iz}
 =
 -\frac{2\pi i d}{a(e^{2\pi id}-1)}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The modulus of the right side is \(r(d)\), so (4.8) has a solution with
\(0<\operatorname{Im}z<h\); the same is true of \(z+d\).  Direct
substitution gives

\[
 F_a(z+d)-F_a(z)
 =
 d+\frac{a}{2\pi i}e^{2\pi iz}(e^{2\pi id}-1)
 =0.
\]

Since \(d\notin\mathbb Z\), these are distinct points even on the
annular quotient.  Finally, the imaginary part of \(F_a\) is bounded on
one closed fundamental strip.  Adding a vertical constant and choosing
\(H\) larger than its oscillation places the image in \(\Pi(H)\) without
altering local univalence, equivariance, or the collision.  \(\square\)

This countermodel refutes degree plus immersion alone.  It is not asserted
to satisfy every additional KL end condition.  The exact degree-one
lift/base equivalence and the sufficient full-properness criterion are
proved separately in `actual_kl_tile_injectivity_gate.md`; the checked KL
sources supply neither global base univalence nor full annulus
properness.  The later forced-collision theorem goes further for the full
canonical primitive stage: it supplies an actual dynamically generated
non-base coset and point collision.

The later exact finite-Blaschke-quotient construction in
`kahn_annulus_global_injectivity_no_go.md` closes the abstract end-condition
question: Kahn's analytic inner-boundary extension, degree-one inner
circle, local-univalence, and modulus-relation hypotheses still do not
imply global injectivity.  That construction is not an actual
dynamically generated KL stage.  It is now only an independent negative
control; the source-specific full canonical gate is closed negatively by
`kl_canonical_localization_forced_collision.md`.

## 5. What Proposition 4.1 does and does not prove

Let

\[
  \Omega=\boldsymbol\iota(\mathbf Q)\subset\mathbf P.
\]

Under `(TI)`, Proposition 4.1 identifies \(\mathbf Q\)
biholomorphically with the open subsurface \(\Omega\).  Without `(TI)`,
that conclusion does not follow; for the full canonical map it is known to
be false.  In either case the proposition does not
assert:

- that \(\Omega\) is relatively closed in \(\mathbf P\);
- that \(\boldsymbol\iota\) is proper;
- that the continuous KL boundary extension is injective;
- that a source ideal point maps to the target ideal boundary;
- that the KKL pullbacks
  \(\boldsymbol\iota^*I\) exist or are pairwise disjoint;
- that \(\mathbf g^*\mathcal I\) refines
  \(\boldsymbol\iota^*\mathcal I\);
- properness on every complete end-interval;
- a physical dynamical-plane collar; or
- any statement uniform in \(q,n\).

The source itself explicitly allows
\(\boldsymbol\iota(\partial_i\mathbf Q)\) to enter the target interior.
If independently proved, interior injectivity removes global sheet
ambiguity for this fixed pair; it does not remove proper-path shadowing by
the internal boundary of \(\Omega\).

## 6. Tile-interior homogeneous trace theorem

The following is the strongest collar statement that does not require a
global identification of the entire glued KL boundary with the
uniformizing prime-end circle of the full pseudo-piece.

### Theorem 6.1

Let \(\widehat A_i\) be one universal-cover tile in the KL pseudo-piece
construction, and let \(J\) be a compact nondegenerate subarc contained in
the relative interior of one ideal-boundary tile arc.  Then:

1. \(J\) has an intrinsic collar which is a bounded Lipschitz
   quadrilateral in a conformal coordinate;
2. its homogeneous minimum-energy trace space is Hilbert; and
3. a conformal isomorphism of one *selected collar* onto another, carrying
   the designated ideal side onto the designated ideal side in prime ends,
   induces an exact isometry of the homogeneous trace spaces.

### Proof

The tile \(\widehat A_i\) is the universal cover of an annulus, hence is
simply connected and hyperbolic.  Choose a uniformization

\[
  u:\widehat A_i\longrightarrow\mathbb D.
\]

Because \(J\) is compactly contained in the relative interior of one tile
arc, its image lies in a proper circular arc with two distinct endpoints.
After a disk automorphism and an angular coordinate, choose

\[
  Q=\{\,\rho e^{i\theta}:
          r<\rho<1,\ \theta_-<\theta<\theta_+\,\},
  \qquad
  0<r<1,\quad \theta_+-\theta_-<2\pi,
  \tag{6.1}
\]

 whose outer side is \(u(J)\).  The domain
\(Q\) is a bounded connected Lipschitz quadrilateral.  Its pullback under
\(u\) is the required intrinsic collar.  This is a collar in the abstract
pseudo-piece coordinate; no regularity of the projected Julia boundary is
used.

Define the homogeneous Dirichlet space

\[
 \mathcal D(Q)=
 \left\{
   v\in H^1_{\rm loc}(Q):
   \int_Q|\nabla v|^2<\infty
 \right\}/\mathbb R. \tag{6.2}
\]

On a bounded connected Lipschitz domain, Poincaré's inequality identifies
\(\mathcal D(Q)\) with \(H^1(Q)/\mathbb R\), so it is a Hilbert space.
For the designated ideal side \(J_Q\), define

\[
 \dot{\mathcal T}_Q(J_Q)
   =\operatorname{Tr}_{J_Q}\mathcal D(Q)
 \tag{6.3}
\]

with quotient norm

\[
 \|[\tau]\|_{\dot{\mathcal T}_Q(J_Q)}^2
 =
 \inf_{\operatorname{Tr}_{J_Q}v-\tau\in\mathbb R}
       \int_Q|\nabla v|^2. \tag{6.4}
\]

The Lipschitz trace map

\[
 \mathcal D(Q)\longrightarrow H^{1/2}(J_Q)/\mathbb R
\]

is continuous and has closed kernel.  Hence its range with the quotient
norm (6.4) is the Hilbert quotient by that kernel.  Standard trace extension
identifies it, with equivalent norm, with the corresponding homogeneous
\(H^{1/2}\) restriction space.

Finally let

\[
 F:(Q',J_{Q'})\longrightarrow(Q,J_Q)
\]

be a conformal isomorphism of the selected collars with the stated
prime-end extension.  Composition by \(F\) bijects the admissible extensions
in (6.4), and two-dimensional Dirichlet energy is conformally invariant:

\[
 \int_{Q'}|\nabla(v\circ F)|^2
 =
 \int_Q|\nabla v|^2. \tag{6.5}
\]

Taking the two infima proves exact isometry of the homogeneous trace
quotients. \(\square\)

### Scope

The theorem does not produce a canonical collar.  Independently selected
collars on the same arc generally give equivalent, not identical, norms,
and the equivalence constants need not be universal.  A conformal embedding
into a proper subcollar generally yields only restriction, not an isometry.
A full norm detecting constants needs a bounded gauge transported with the
collar, and multiple transport routes need a holonomy audit.

The theorem also does not identify an arbitrary proof-used KKL carrier
interval with a KL tile arc.  That identification is part of analytic
realization.

## 7. Conformal ideal boundary is proved, but the global collar is not

The later source-locked proof in
`embedded_disk_accessible_footprint_theorem.md` and
`direct_decoration_outer_port_footprint.md` closes the conformal
ideal-boundary identification for the two audited pseudo-pieces
\(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q_a\).

The open-cover point requires care because the physical puzzle piece is a
closed Jordan domain.  The proof uses the central interior
\(U_0=\operatorname{int}Y\) and the open universal-cover tile images
\(T_i\).  Boundary vertices lie in \(S_Y\) and are absent from the path
surface; every other central-boundary point lies in one tile.  Moreover
\(U_0\cap T_i=\operatorname{int}Y\cap Y_i\) is path-connected: a path in
the simply connected \(Y_i\) can be pushed off
\(\partial Y\setminus K_Y\) through a Schoenflies collar.  Repeated van
Kampen therefore gives simple connectivity.  Liouville/Picard exclude
parabolic uniformization, and Kahn's uniformization definition together

## 8. Analytic counterexample to the finite-label hull

### Proposition 8.1

There is a holomorphic embedding of one Jordan disk in another and a
two-interval target ideal marking for which the Research Push-VIII
deletion-and-seed hull of one target interval retains both open source
atoms, including a specified nonproper subarc, while no KKL immersion
pullback of that interval can contain that subarc.  The endpoint predicate
is separate and no claim about the two source vertices is needed.  In
particular, the formal label hull is not the KKL immersion pullback.

### Construction

Let

\[
  V=\mathbb D,\qquad
  \Omega=\mathbb D\cap\{\operatorname{Im}z>0\}.
\]

The boundary of \(\Omega\) is the union of

\[
 I=\{e^{it}:0<t<\pi\}\subset\partial\mathbb D
\]

and the open diameter

\[
 C=(-1,1)\subset\mathbb D,
\]

together with the endpoints \(\{-1,1\}\).  Let
\(\phi:\mathbb D\to\Omega\) be a Riemann map.  Since \(\Omega\) is a Jordan
domain, Carathéodory's theorem gives a homeomorphic extension
\(\overline\phi:\overline{\mathbb D}\to\overline\Omega\).  Define

\[
 \iota:\mathbb D\xrightarrow{\phi}\Omega\hookrightarrow\mathbb D.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

This is a holomorphic embedding.  Put

\[
 E=\overline\phi^{-1}(I),\qquad
 N=\overline\phi^{-1}(C)
\]

on the source ideal circle.  The immersion is proper on \(E\), while the
source ideal arc \(N\) maps to the target interior.

Give the target circle the two-interval marking

\[
 \mathcal I=\{I,J\},\qquad
 J=\{e^{it}:\pi<t<2\pi\}.
\]

In the Push-VIII finite label model, \(E\) has label \(I\) and \(N\) has
label \(\bot\).  There is no proper label outside \(I\) to delete.
The \(I\)-seed \(E\) and the adjacent nonproper run \(N\) remain connected
in the circular source chain.  Therefore the formal deletion-and-seed hull
retains both open atoms:

\[
 E\cup N\subset H_\iota(I). \tag{8.2}
\]

No claim is made here about the two intervening source vertices: their
retention belongs to the separate endpoint predicate in the Push-VIII
finite model.  The no-go below needs only \(N_0\subset N\subset H_\iota(I)\).

### Proper-path witness

Choose distinct \(a,b\in C\), and let \(N_0\Subset N\) be the source
boundary arc between \(\overline\phi^{-1}(a)\) and
\(\overline\phi^{-1}(b)\) that maps to the corresponding diameter segment.
Choose two distinct target ideal points
\(\zeta_-,\zeta_+\in J\).

There is a simple proper target crosscut

\[
 \gamma:(0,1)\longrightarrow\mathbb D
\]

with ideal endpoints \(\zeta_-,\zeta_+\) such that:

1. \(\gamma\) enters \(\Omega\) through \(a\);
2. \(\gamma\cap\Omega\) is one crosscut \(\delta\) from \(a\) to \(b\),
   chosen in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of the diameter segment
   \([a,b]\); and
3. \(\gamma\) exits through \(b\) and otherwise lies in
   \(\mathbb D\setminus\overline\Omega\).

This is obtained by joining \(\zeta_-\) to \(a\) and \(b\) to
\(\zeta_+\) in the lower half-disk and joining \(a\) to \(b\) by a
crosscut immediately above \(C\), with the three arcs chosen disjoint
except at \(a,b\).

Because (8.1) is an embedding,
\(\iota^{-1}(\gamma)\) has exactly one component that is proper in the
source disk:

\[
 \delta'=\phi^{-1}(\delta). \tag{8.3}
\]

The closure of \(\delta'\) has its two endpoints on \(N_0\).  Since
\(\delta\) can be chosen in every neighborhood of \([a,b]\), the source
crosscut \(\delta'\), up to homotopy rel endpoints, belongs to every
neighborhood of \(N_0\) in the source ideal closure.  Thus

\[
 \delta'\ \text{is subordinate to }N_0. \tag{8.4}
\]

On the other hand, \(\gamma\) is not subordinate to \(I\): its two ideal
endpoints lie in the relative interior of \(J\), and a sufficiently small
neighborhood of \(I\) in the ideal closure omits them.  Homotopy rel
endpoints cannot change this.

Now apply the KKL defining implication.  Any candidate source subset
\(X'\) for \(\iota^*I\) that contains \(N_0\) would have a target proper
path \(\gamma\) with a unique source proper lift \(\delta'\) subordinate to
\(X'\), while \(\gamma\) is not subordinate to \(I\).  Such \(X'\) is
inadmissible.  Consequently

\[
 N_0\not\subset\iota^*I
\]

whenever the KKL maximal pullback is defined, whereas (8.2) contains
\(N_0\).  Hence

\[
 H_\iota(I)\ne\iota^*I. \qquad\square
\]

### Exact lesson

The failure is not noninjectivity, local-connectivity failure, or
prime-end monodromy.  The map is a holomorphic Jordan embedding.  The
missing datum is the **proper-path shadow/exclusion relation** of the
internal boundary arc \(C\).  A nonproper symbol \(\bot\), even together
with its two flanking proper labels, cannot recover that relation.

## 9. Geometric-restriction reformulation, conditional on embedding

Assume `(TI)`, so Proposition 4.1 supplies an embedding
\(i:V'\hookrightarrow V\), and put \(\Omega=i(V')\).  For a target proper
path \(\gamma\), every parameter component of
\(\gamma^{-1}(\Omega)\) is a traversal of the geometric trace.  If the
ideal extension `(PE)` is available, the light-boundary-fiber argument in
`embedded_disk_accessible_footprint_theorem.md` makes each such traversal
a KKL proper source path.

One must not identify these parameter components bijectively with the
**entire** KKL restriction \(i^*\gamma\).  At a self-intersection or a
retraced segment of the target trace, its geometric preimage can contain a
proper path that switches branches and is not any single parameter
traversal.  KKL's uniqueness hypothesis concerns that entire geometric
restriction.

The exact admissibility test is therefore:

\[
\left.
\begin{array}{c}
i^*\gamma=\{\delta\}\text{ as a full geometric restriction},\\[1mm]
\delta\text{ is subordinate to }C
\end{array}
\right\}
\Longrightarrow
\gamma\text{ is subordinate to }I
\tag{9.1}
\]

for every target proper path \(\gamma\).  Under `(PE)`, the
embedded-disk accessible-footprint theorem proves that (9.1) holds
exactly when every full complement-access footprint of a point of \(C\)
lies in \(I\).  For a revised KL pair this application requires a separate
proof of `(TI)`.  It applies to a separately embedded retained collar, not
to the full canonical map, for which `(TI)` is false.

This reformulation is diagnostic:

- **absorption** requires full footprint containment for every point of
  the proposed source set;
- **separation** requires one explicit target proper path whose entire
  geometric restriction is the declared source path;
- paths with multiple or branch-switching restrictions do not test the
  uniqueness implication, but their sheet data remain necessary for
  relative-cover and enlargement audits.

The formal label table records none of these facts.

## 12. Correct conditional Beck--Chevalley interface

The pull--push word no-go does have a precise replacement once actual
collared correspondences exist.

Let

\[
 X\xleftarrow{a}M\xrightarrow{b}Y,
 \qquad
 Y\xleftarrow{c}N\xrightarrow{d}Z
\]

be two collared correspondence spans.  Form the actual fiber product

\[
 P=M\times_YN
\]

with projections \(p:P\to M\), \(q:P\to N\).  If the collar square is
Cartesian and its restriction and finite covering transport operators obey

\[
 c^*b_*=q_*p^*, \tag{12.1}
\]

then

\[
\begin{aligned}
(d_*c^*)(b_*a^*)
  &=d_*c^*b_*a^*\\
  &=d_*q_*p^*a^*\\
  &=(d\circ q)_*(a\circ p)^* .
\end{aligned}
\tag{12.2}
\]

Equation (12.2) is formal after (12.1); proving (12.1) is the geometric
work.  At minimum it requires:

1. an actual Cartesian square of selected collars;
2. proper immersion collar branches and finite covering legs;
3. a component/fiber bijection including multiplicity;
4. the same connected core and trace compatibility relation on both sides;
5. homogeneous trace quotients, or full gauges transported coherently;
6. whole-end properness;
7. exclusion or separate retention of every nonproper middle arc; and
8. no component birth or loss under enlargement.

A commuting non-Cartesian square is insufficient.  For the finite-set
square

\[
 X=\{x_0,x_1\}\to Y=\{y\},\qquad
 X'=\{x'_0\}\to Y'=\{y'\},
\]

where \(x'_0\mapsto x_0\) and the primed square omits \(x_1\), take
\(\varphi(x_0)=0,\varphi(x_1)=1\).  Pullback after fiber-sum gives \(1\);
fiber-sum after pullback gives \(0\).  The missing point is precisely the
failure of Cartesian fiber completeness.

This section proves only the conditional algebraic identity.  It does not
assert that the direct-decoration collars satisfying (12.1) have been
constructed.

## 13. Final-root enlargement no-birth condition

Commutation on the old subdiagram and preservation of old endpoints do not
prove final-root naturality.  Consider:

\[
 S_{\rm old}=\{s_0\},\qquad
 S_{\rm new}=\{S_0,S_{\rm new}\},
\]

and one old target interval \(I\) enlarged to \(I^+\).  Map \(s_0\) to
\(S_0\), preserve its endpoint data, and let both \(S_0,S_{\rm new}\) lie
over \(I^+\).  The old square commutes, yet

\[
 A'\bigl(\iota_{\rm old}^{-1}I\bigr)=\{S_0\}
 \ne
 \{S_0,S_{\rm new}\}
 =\iota_+^{-1}(A I). \tag{13.1}
\]

Thus an enlarged theorem requires an actual component bijection:

\[
\{\text{old proper lifts over }I\}
\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
\{\text{new proper lifts over }I^+\},
\tag{13.2}
\]

with the based cover subgroup, deck phase, proper/nonproper status,
subordination class, and collar component preserved.  Equality of endpoint
inverse images controls only the old boundary skeleton; it cannot imply
(13.2).

For the based localization cosets, the later exact algebraic test is:

\[
 K\cap\varphi^{-1}(H_s^+)=H_s
\quad\text{and}\quad
 K^+=\varphi(K)H_s^+.
\tag{13.3}
\]

These conditions are necessary and sufficient for bijectivity of the
induced based coset map.  They are not sufficient for (13.2).  If all
groups are trivial, (13.3) holds automatically, yet a simply connected
new collar lobe can create a new proof-used path or access component.
Thus (13.2) still requires the full Cartesian component/path square,
obstacle and nonproper-bridge audit, and prime-end or direct proper-path

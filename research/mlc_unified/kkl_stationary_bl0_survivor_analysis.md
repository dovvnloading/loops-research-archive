## 3. Native stationary setting

Fix a parabolically \(b\)-bounded stationary KKL block and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 X_0&=\mathcal U\setminus\mathcal T,\\
 X_h&=\mathcal U^h\setminus\mathcal T^h,\\
 N&=b+3,\qquad m=N-1=b+2,\\
 P_h&=f^h:X_h\longrightarrow X_0,\\
 I_h&=\iota_h:X_h\longrightarrow X_0 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

On these surfaces, \(P_h\) is an unbranched covering and \(I_h\) is a
holomorphic immersion.  The only safe global degree statement is

\[
 D_h:=\deg P_h\le 2^h,\qquad 1\le h\le b+2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

A smaller degree may be used only after its sheet bound is proved
uniformly over the entire actual ending multi-interval.

Let \(S\) be a Borel lamination of nonshort ambient survivors in
\((X_0,\mathcal I)\) such that:

1. \(S\) is a sublamination of a stationary Wave ending at \(BL_0\);
2. every leaf remains a single ambient path through the declared Wave
   horizon;
3. no leaf has already been assigned to the native ambient-break,
   ambient-short, vertical, Shift, or small-Julia-incidence bucket; and
4. the selected marked replacement at horizon \(h\) again projects to
   a Wave ending at \(BL_0\).

The word "ending" is essential.  A Wave ending at \(BL_0\) has an
endpoint in \(BL_0\).  A Wave over \(BL_0\) has \(BL_0\) in its crest.
KKL lines 1513--1521 bound only the latter class.

For the line-1603 configuration, take \(b\le n<N-1\) and \(h=n+1\).
If a leaf \(\alpha\) avoids \(K_0^n\), the intended two portions are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \gamma^-_\alpha &: BL_0\rightsquigarrow L_0^h,\\
 \gamma^+_\alpha &: L_0^h\rightsquigarrow
   \partial_{\rm C}\mathcal T\setminus BL_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Formula (3.3) is notation for the intended occurrence segments.  It
does not assert that either family is a source Wave or that either
segment survives the pulled marking.

## 4. The paired-response packet

### Definition 4.1 (realized \(r\)-branch response packet)

Let \(S\) be as in Section 3.  A realized \(r\)-branch response packet
over \(S\) at horizon \(h\) consists of the following actual analytic
data.

1. **Transverse realization.**  A standard Borel transverse parameter
   space \((\Lambda,\nu)\) and a realizing family
   \(\{\alpha_\xi\}_{\xi\in\Lambda}\) for every leaf of \(S\).  It is
   enough to represent a sublamination \(S_0\subset S\) only if one
   separately proves
   \[
      \mathcal W(S\setminus S_0)=0
      \quad\text{and}\quad
      \mathcal W(S_0)=\mathcal W(S),
   \tag{4.0}
   \]
   and then replaces \(S\) by \(S_0\) throughout.  A set of
   \(\nu\)-measure zero is not automatically a family of zero extremal
   width.
2. **Occurrence segments.**  For every \(1\le i\le r\), a Borel
   selection of an actual open subpath
   \(\gamma^i_\xi\subset\alpha_\xi\) such that, for each \(\xi\), the
   \(r\) untrimmed path images are pairwise disjoint.  Their common
   ideal limit is not part of any open path image.
3. **Actual source Waves.**  Source Waves
   \(B^i\subset(X_h,P_h^*\mathcal I)\), whose leaves are exactly
   \(\{\beta^i_\xi:\xi\in\Lambda\}\), satisfying
   \[
      I_h(\beta^i_\xi)=\gamma^i_\xi
   \tag{4.1}
   \]
   as untrimmed parametrized paths.
   If a candidate source family contains omitted leaves, discard them
   before naming \(B^i\), prove that the omitted family has zero width,
   and reverify that the retained family is itself a Wave of unchanged
   width.
4. **Proper marked ends.**  Every \(B^i\) is a genuine proper
   lamination, survives the fine marking and the coarse subordinate
   filter, and ends at a marked multi-interval \(J_h^i\).
5. **Ending-multi-interval degree.**  \(P_h\) extends to the whole
   ending multi-interval \(J_h^i\), and the number of sheets is bounded
   uniformly over all of that multi-interval:
   \[
      \deg_{J_h^i}(P_h)\le d_i\le D_h.
   \tag{4.2}
   \]
   A local degree at one endpoint or access is insufficient.
6. **Accounting.**  The selections use the same target leaf,
   transverse parameter, horizon, and proof-used occurrence.  They
   neither duplicate the same target mass at another first horizon nor
   mix distinct occurrence windows.

The packet is **output-controlled** if the Wave lemma outputs

\[
 C^i\subset P_{h*}B^i
\tag{4.3}
\]

are genuine Waves and every \(C^i\) belongs to a declared uniformly
controllable output class.

For the stationary line-1603 application \(r=2\), with the signs
\(-,+\) corresponding to (3.3).

### Ideal-end and no-truncation convention

The two portions in (3.3) share the formal \(L_0^h\) occurrence in an
ideal compactification.  That ideal endpoint is not part of either
proper path on the open source surface.  KL06 `appendix.tex`, lines
12--15 explicitly allows open path-parameter intervals.  Definition
4.1 therefore requires the two **untrimmed** images in \(X_0\) to be
disjoint actual open subpaths of \(\alpha_\xi\).

This requirement cannot be replaced silently by finite collar
truncation.  A full path overflows its truncation, so its width is at
most, not at least, the width of the truncated family.  To use
truncations one would need a separately proved collar-exhaustion
continuity/no-loss theorem identifying the limiting widths.  No such
theorem is assumed here.  If the two untrimmed images meet, overlap, or
leave and reenter before reaching their ideal limits, the response
packet fails at Definition 4.1(2).

No local connectivity of a filled Julia set is used.  All landing data
are ideal-end and access data on the stated surfaces.

## 5. The series inequality before pushforward

### Definition 5.0 (exact occurrence realization)

An exact \(r\)-branch occurrence realization consists of:

1. the full-width target realization in Definition 4.1(1);
2. the pairwise disjoint untrimmed occurrence segments in Definition
   4.1(2); and
3. source path families \(B^i\) whose leaves are exactly the
   \(\beta^i_\xi\) in Definition 4.1(3) and satisfy the exact image
   identity (4.1).

The \(B^i\) need not yet be Waves for this definition.  Every realized
response packet is an exact occurrence realization with the additional
Wave, marking, degree, and accounting clauses of Definition 4.1.

### Lemma 5.1 (paired occurrence conductance)

For any exact \(r\)-branch occurrence realization,

\[
 \mathcal W(S)
 \le
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathcal W(B^i).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Here

\[
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^r x_i
 :=
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^r x_i^{-1}\right)^{-1}
\tag{5.2}
\]

for positive finite widths, with the usual limiting convention when a
width is zero or infinite.

#### Proof

Let

\[
 \Gamma^i:=\{\gamma^i_\xi:\xi\in\Lambda\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

By Definitions 4.1(2) and 5.0, every leaf \(\alpha_\xi\) contains the disjoint
union of one member of every \(\Gamma^i\).  Iterating the KL06 Series
Law (`appendix.tex`, lines 39--57) gives

\[
 \mathcal W(S)
 \le
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathcal W(\Gamma^i).
\tag{5.4}
\]

By Definition 5.0(3),

\[
 \Gamma^i=I_h(B^i)
\tag{5.5}
\]

as an untrimmed parametrized path family.  KL06's holomorphic
transformation rule gives

\[
 \mathcal L(I_h(B^i))\ge\mathcal L(B^i).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Since extremal width is reciprocal extremal length,

\[
 \mathcal W(\Gamma^i)
 =
 \mathcal W(I_h(B^i))
 \le\mathcal W(B^i).
\tag{5.7}
\]

The harmonic sum is nondecreasing in each variable.  Substituting
(5.7) into (5.4) proves (5.1).

Notice what the proof does **not** require.  It does not require
\(I_h\) to be injective on \(\bigcup_iB^i\), and it does not require the
\(P_h\)-images of different branches to be distinct.  The disjointness
needed by the Series Law is the disjointness of the actual occurrence
segments inside each target leaf. \(\square\)

### Cross-check against KKL domination

If \(I_h\) is additionally injective on \(\bigcup_iB^i\), (5.1) also
follows directly from KKL's domination lemma, master lines 2086--2090.
Lemma 5.1 is strictly safer for the present application because it
does not infer global immersion injectivity from sheet labels.  It uses
the stronger geometric input that the source leaves realize the
declared disjoint portions of the same target leaf.

## 6. Quantitative Wave response

KKL master lines 2149--2152 state the Wave lemma with an
\(O(1+\log d)\) loss, but the checked local source contains neither its
proof nor a dependency declaration for the hidden constant.  The
following response theorem therefore has the explicit imported
uniformity hypothesis

\[
 \mathsf{UW}_b:
 \quad
 \text{one finite }c_{\rm W}\text{ is valid for every branch and
 marking in the packet class under consideration.}
\tag{6.0}
\]

For one fixed finite packet, one may take the maximum of its finitely
many imported constants.  Calling the result a \(b\)-only constant
uniform over all admissible markings requires an independent proof of
\(\mathsf{UW}_b\); it does not follow merely by fixing a marking
convention.  Conditional on \(\mathsf{UW}_b\), for \(h\le b+2\) define

\[
 C_b:=c_{\rm W}\bigl(1+(b+2)\log2\bigr).
\tag{6.1}
\]

No numerical value or unconditional global uniformity is claimed for
\(c_{\rm W}\).

### Theorem 6.1 (\(r\)-branch Wave-response lemma)

Assume \(\mathsf{UW}_b\), and let \(S\) admit a realized
\(r\)-branch response packet.  For each
\(i\), let \(C^i\subset P_{h*}B^i\) be the Wave supplied by KKL's Wave
lemma.  Then

\[
 \max_{1\le i\le r}\mathcal W(C^i)
 \ge
 r\,\mathcal W(S)-C_b.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For the two stationary portions,

\[
 \boxed{
 \max\{\mathcal W(C^-),\mathcal W(C^+)\}
 \ge
 2\mathcal W(S)-C_b.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

KKL's Wave lemma, master lines 2149--2152, applied on the actual ending
multi-interval \(J_h^i\), gives

\[
 \mathcal W(B^i)
 \le
 \mathcal W(C^i)+c_{\rm W}(1+\log d_i).
\tag{6.4}
\]

By (3.2) and (4.2),

\[
 d_i\le D_h\le2^h\le2^{b+2},
\tag{6.5}
\]

so

\[
 c_{\rm W}(1+\log d_i)\le C_b.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Put

\[
 M:=\max_i\mathcal W(C^i).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Equations (6.4)--(6.6) imply

\[
 \mathcal W(B^i)\le M+C_b
\quad\text{for every }i.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Using Lemma 5.1 and the definition of harmonic sum,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal W(S)
 &\le
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathcal W(B^i)\\
 &\le
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^r(M+C_b)\\
 &=\frac{M+C_b}{r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Rearranging gives (6.2).  Setting \(r=2\) gives (6.3).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 6.2 (covering collision is still a response)

Under Theorem 6.1, suppose all pushed branches lie in one output Wave
\(T\), including the case in which the two source paths are exchanged
by the nontrivial deck involution of the central quadratic collar.
Then

\[
 \mathcal W(T)\ge r\,\mathcal W(S)-C_b.
\tag{6.10}
\]

For \(r=2\),

\[
 \mathcal W(T)\ge2\mathcal W(S)-C_b.
\tag{6.11}
\]

#### Proof

Since \(C^i\subset T\), extremal-width monotonicity gives
\(\mathcal W(C^i)\le\mathcal W(T)\).  Substitute this in (6.2).
\(\square\)

This is the reason that deck noncollision is not logically necessary
for a **width response** once the common-output containment hypothesis
of Corollary 6.2 has been proved.  Deck noncollision remains one
sufficient route to the native "two distinct pushed paths"
contradiction.  If the paths collide under \(P_h\) and both Wave-lemma
outputs are certified to lie in one Wave \(T\), the series calculation
has already counted two disjoint target-occurrence portions before that
collision, and \(T\) carries the resulting conductance.  Pointwise deck
collision alone does not construct \(T\) or prove its controllability.

### Corollary 6.3 (strict packet-local amplification)

For \(r=2\), if \(0<\mathcal W(S)<\infty\) and

\[
 \mathcal W(S)\ge2C_b,
\tag{6.12}
\]

then some pushed Wave satisfies

\[
 \mathcal W(C^i)\ge\frac32\mathcal W(S).
\tag{6.13}
\]

#### Proof

From (6.3),

\[
 \mathcal W(C^i)
\ge2\mathcal W(S)-C_b
\ge\frac32\mathcal W(S).
\]

\(\square\)

### Corollary 6.4 (when packet response implies ambient terminality)

Let \(A\) be an ambient Wave with
\(0<\mathcal W(A)<\infty\), let
\(0<\epsilon<1\), and suppose \(S\subset A\) satisfies

\[
 \mathcal W(S)\ge
 \left(\frac12+\epsilon\right)\mathcal W(A)
\tag{6.14}
\]

If

\[
 \mathcal W(A)\ge\frac{C_b}{\epsilon}
\tag{6.15}
\]

and a maximizing output \(C\) in (6.3) is
\(\eta\)-controllable, then \(A\) has an \(\eta\)-controllable Wave
satisfying

\[
 \mathcal W(C)\ge(1+\epsilon)\mathcal W(A).
\tag{6.16}
\]

Thus \(A\) satisfies the wider-Wave branch of KKL terminality with
every \(0<\delta<\epsilon\), for example
\(\delta=\epsilon/2\).

#### Proof

Equations (6.3), (6.14), and (6.15) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal W(C)
 &\ge2\mathcal W(S)-C_b\\
 &\ge(1+2\epsilon)\mathcal W(A)-C_b\\
 &\ge(1+\epsilon)\mathcal W(A).
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \((1+\epsilon)\mathcal W(A)>
(1+\delta)\mathcal W(A)\) for every \(0<\delta<\epsilon\), the last
assertion gives the strict second alternative in KKL master line 1155.
\(\square\)

The mass condition (6.14) cannot be deleted.  If a finite selector
splits the residual into many small packets, a factor-two gain relative
to one packet need not be a gain relative to the whole ambient Wave.
Either the two-sided realization must be made on the entire residual,
or the other packets must be separately charged, or a higher
multiplicity response must be proved.

## 7. The weaker degree-surplus fallback

The coefficient one in KKL's Wave lemma is essential.  To quantify
this, suppose \(S\) and the selected source families \(B^i\) form an
exact occurrence realization in the sense of Definition 5.0, but the
\(B^i\) are only proper laminations, not certified source Waves.
Assume that \(B^i\) ends on an entire marked multi-interval \(J_h^i\)
with

\[
 \deg_{J_h^i}(P_h)\le d_i,
\tag{7.0}
\]

and apply the general KKL pushforward-lamination lemma.  Assume its
extracted output laminations \(C^i\) all lie in a target family \(T\).
Then

\[
 \mathcal W(B^i)
 \le
 d_i\mathcal W(C^i)+2d_i
 \le
 d_i\bigl(\mathcal W(T)+2\bigr).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Define the reciprocal-degree surplus

\[
 s:=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac1{d_i}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Lemma 5.1 and (7.1) imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac1{\mathcal W(S)}
 &\ge
 \sum_{i=1}^r\frac1{\mathcal W(B^i)}\\
 &\ge
 \frac{s}{\mathcal W(T)+2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal W(T)\ge s\,\mathcal W(S)-2.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

For two branches:

- \((d_-,d_+)=(1,1)\) gives \(s=2\);
- \((d_-,d_+)=(1,2)\) gives \(s=3/2\);
- \((d_-,d_+)=(2,2)\) gives \(s=1\).

Thus a generic "degree at most two" slogan does not produce strict
amplification.  In the last case, (7.4) is noncoercive.  The replacement
state must retain the ending-multi-interval degree pair and, more importantly,
whether each branch is an actual Wave to which the coefficient-one
Wave lemma applies.

If \(T=S\) geometrically and \(s>1\), (7.4) gives the stationary bound

\[
 \mathcal W(S)\le\frac2{s-1}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

For \(s\le1\), no bound follows.

## 8. Application to the line-1603 survivor

### 8.1 Complete branch classification once the packet exists

Assume the two portions in (3.3) form a realized two-branch response
packet.  There are then three source-correct output branches.

1. **Native break.**  The two pushed paths satisfy all native
   separation, marking, end-properness, and controllability clauses for
   KKL breakability.  Then the leaf belongs to
   \(A^{H\text{-br}}\), contradicting its classification as an
   ambient-singleton survivor.  It must be absorbed into the existing
   break bucket, not counted as a new residual.
2. **Output collision or nonbreaking overlap.**  The pushed Wave
   outputs coincide or lie in one declared controllable output Wave.
   Corollary 6.2 gives the factor-two response.
3. **Distinct outputs without a certified native break.**  Theorem 6.1
   still gives a factor-two packet-local response through the larger
   output, provided both outputs are controllable.  Distinct geometric
   labels alone do not prove native breakability.

Thus the noncollision predicate isolated in
`kkl_stationary_tsnc_source_reduction.md` is sufficient for branch 1
but is not required for branches 2--3.  The decisive common input is
the paired realization of two actual source Waves.

### 8.2 The exact source failure

The locked source does not prove that paired realization.

There are two different possible starting mechanisms, and they must not
be conflated.

#### Route F: fixed Freudenthal ends

Freudenthal ends of finite-topology surfaces.  The stationary symbols

unconditional source obstruction is the type mismatch

\[

\tag{8.1}
\]

The locked source constructs no access-splitting localization and no
proper-degree-one Freudenthal end representing the selected \(BL_0\)
access.  Therefore the fixed-end theorem cannot presently be
instantiated from that access.

over one inner Freudenthal end and that this inner end is nonproper for

\[

## 9. \(R(F)\) and Value observability

### 9.1 \(R(F)\)-charge

Write

\[
 R(F):=\mathcal W(\mathcal RF).
\tag{9.1}
\]

A \(BL_0\)-ending label does not imply an \(R(F)\)-charge.  Suppose,
independently, that one selected source family \(B^{\rm ren}\) satisfies
all of the following:

1. it restricts to a proper lamination in
   \(\mathcal U^h\setminus\mathcal K^h\);
2. it connects one specified pulled small-Julia component \(K_h\) to
   the prescribed complement;
3. for every \(\alpha\in S\), the retained restriction
   \(I_h^*\alpha\) contains a declared leaf
   \(\beta_\alpha\in B^{\rm ren}\), with any omitted target family
   separately proved to have zero width;
4. \(B^{\rm ren}\) ends at a whole marked multi-interval
   \(J_h^{\rm ren}\) and
   \[
      \deg_{J_h^{\rm ren}}(P_h)\le d_h;
   \tag{9.1a}
   \]
5. the pushforward-lamination lemma extracts a specific lamination
   \[
      C^{\rm ren}\subset P_{h*}B^{\rm ren}
   \tag{9.1b}
   \]
   having exactly the component incidence named in KKL master lines
   1613--1617;
6. one has independently licensed the asserted comparison
   \[
      \mathcal W(C^{\rm ren})\le2R(F);
   \tag{9.1c}
   \]
   and
7. the occurrence selector does not duplicate transverse mass.

KKL domination, master lines 2086--2090, applied to item 3 gives

\[
 \mathcal W(S)\le\mathcal W(B^{\rm ren}).
\tag{9.1d}
\]

The source pushforward lemma gives

\[
 \mathcal W(B^{\rm ren})
 \le d_h\mathcal W(C^{\rm ren})+2d_h.
\tag{9.1e}
\]

Conditional on (9.1c), these inequalities give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal W(S)
 &\le\mathcal W(B^{\rm ren})\\
 &\le d_h\bigl(2R(F)\bigr)+2d_h\\
 &=2d_h\bigl(R(F)+1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.2}
\]

If the degree over the entire actual ending multi-interval is two, this is

 \mathcal W(S)\le4R(F)+4.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The degree-two value in (9.3) cannot be inferred from the total
quadratic degree alone; it must hold uniformly over the entire actual
ending multi-interval.  More importantly, the component incidence in
item 5 is the conclusion that the stationary alignment argument was
meant to supply.  It is not available before the paired realization or

therefore treats (9.1c) as a separately licensed hypothesis, not as an

conditional charge, not a completed charge for the residual.

### 9.2 Value observability

KKL `value1.tex`, lines 4--21 defines Value only on WADs in

 \operatorname{Val}(X)
 =
 \sum_{\alpha}X(\alpha)\,\ell(\alpha)^2

\]

and compare it with WAD size only when the support arcs have bounded

The raw survivor \(S\) lives in

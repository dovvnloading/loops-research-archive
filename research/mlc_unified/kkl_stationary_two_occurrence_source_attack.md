# KKL stationary two-occurrence binder: source attack and first analytic obstruction

## 1. Binary verdict

The locked Kahn--Koropecki--Lyubich source does **not** prove

```text
KKL_STATIONARY_TWO_OCCURRENCE_BINDER.
```

The failure occurs before deck noncollision and, in the source as
written, before one can audit the line-2097 retention filter for two
routes.  The first *formally typed* missing analytic datum is a
**bilateral immersion-lift incidence** at the proof-used encounter with
\(L_0^{n+1}\):

\[
\begin{split}
 \operatorname{BILI}(n,\alpha):\quad&
 \text{the two ordered one-sided portions of }\alpha\text{ at the}\\
 &L_0^{n+1}\text{-encounter lie in the image of two actual local}\\
 &\text{branches of }I_{n+1},\text{ and the two branches approach}\\
 &\text{specified pulled prime-end accesses.}
\end{split}
\tag{1.1}
\]

There is an important qualification.  KKL line 1241 gives an embedded
neighborhood of the pulled filled set.  If the informal words “passes
through \(L_0^{n+1}\)” are strengthened to an actual ordered,
two-sided crossing inside the corresponding iterated embedded
neighborhood, then (1.1) follows locally.  The locked source does not
define that crossing predicate, its two accesses, or its compatibility
with the complete ideal marking.  Thus this is a conditional local
repair, not a completed source inference.

Once (1.1) is supplied, a general local-homeomorphism argument proves
that the two germs lie in different connected components of the raw
complete inverse trace.  It does **not** prove that either maximal
component is a KKL proper path, that it admits the full proper
sequential stage extensions of Section 12, or that those extensions
survive the line-2097 filter.

Thus the exact order of the unresolved analytic clauses is

\[
\boxed{
 \operatorname{BILI}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{two raw components}
 \;\dashrightarrow\;
 \text{proper prime-end landing}
 \;\dashrightarrow\;
 \text{fine marking}
 \;\dashrightarrow\;
 \text{coarse-filter survival}.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The dashed arrows are open in the actual KKL stationary block.  The
finite fiber-product equalizer theorem applies after a sequential
chain has been restricted to its nested common parameter windows.  It
does not construct the full proper stage paths or their extension
windows; Section 12 makes this distinction exact.

No actual KKL block violating the binder is exhibited here.  The
conclusion is a source-proof obstruction, not a counterexample to the
intended stationary theorem.  The two-occurrence binder, retained
realization, two-sided noncollision, VAAPC-I, UPTCR, and MLC remain
open.

## 5. A general raw-component lemma

The following lemma isolates exactly what a bilateral lift would buy.

### Lemma 5.1 (opposite one-sided lifts lie in different raw components)

Let \(I:X\to Y\) be a local homeomorphism between surfaces and let

\[
 \alpha:(0,1)\longrightarrow Y
\tag{5.1}
\]

be an embedded path.  For every connected component

\[
 C\in\operatorname{Comp}\bigl(I^{-1}(|\alpha|)\bigr),
\tag{5.2}
\]

the map

\[
 t_C=\alpha^{-1}\circ I|_C:C\longrightarrow(0,1)
\tag{5.3}
\]

is injective and its image is an open interval.

Consequently, fix \(t_0\in(0,1)\).  Suppose two inverse germs exist over

\[
 (t_0-\varepsilon,t_0)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (t_0,t_0+\varepsilon),
\tag{5.4}
\]

and each germ approaches an ideal-boundary point of \(X\) as
\(t\to t_0\).  Then the germs lie in two different components of
(5.2).  The same conclusion follows under the stronger condition
\(\alpha(t_0)\notin I(X)\), without separately assuming ideal
landing.

#### Proof

Because \(I\) is a local homeomorphism and \(\alpha\) is embedded,
\(I^{-1}(|\alpha|)\) is locally a one-manifold and (5.3) is a local
homeomorphism.  A connected one-manifold is an interval-type
one-manifold or a circle.  The circle case is impossible: a local
homeomorphism is open, while the image of a compact circle would be a
nonempty compact open subset of \((0,1)\).

Choose an orientation of the interval-type component \(C\).
The local orientation sign of \(t_C\) is locally constant and hence
constant on connected \(C\).  Thus \(t_C\) is strictly monotone and
therefore injective.  Since a local homeomorphism is open,
\(t_C(C)\) is a connected open subset of \((0,1)\), hence an open
interval.

Suppose the two germs in (5.4) belonged to the same component \(C\).
Then the interval \(t_C(C)\) would contain points on both sides of
\(t_0\), so it would contain \(t_0\).  Since \(t_C\) is a
homeomorphism onto its image, its inverse is continuous at \(t_0\).
Both one-sided lifts would therefore converge in \(X\) to the interior
point \(t_C^{-1}(t_0)\), contrary to their specified ideal-boundary
limits.  If instead \(\alpha(t_0)\notin I(X)\), the existence of
\(t_C^{-1}(t_0)\) is already the contradiction. \(\square\)

### Qualification 5.2

Lemma 5.1 is an interior theorem.  It proves neither:

1. continuous landing of a maximal component at the ideal boundary;
2. membership in the fine marking \(f^*\mathcal I\);
3. non-subordination to one interval of
   \(\iota^*\mathcal I\);
4. the required parent/access itinerary; nor
5. terminal Wave-family membership.

In particular, “maximal lift escapes every compact subset” cannot be
silently replaced by KKL properness.  A curve can escape while having
more than one ideal accumulation point.

### Lemma 5.3 (finite iterated embedded neighborhood)

Let the same symbols \(I_h,P_h\) denote the full holomorphic
projections before the pulled tree is removed.  For every finite
\(h\), there is a neighborhood \(O_h\) of the pulled filled set
\(K^h\) in the \(h\)-th Kahn surface such that

\[
 I_h|_{O_h}:O_h\longrightarrow I_h(O_h)
\tag{5.5}
\]

is a holomorphic embedding.  Its image is an open neighborhood of the
base filled set.

#### Proof

Kahn's pullback lemma makes every stage

\[
 (i_r,f_r):(U^{r+1},K^{r+1})
 \longrightarrow(U^r,K^r)
\tag{5.6}
\]

a pseudo-polynomial-like map.  The proof of Kahn's polynomial-like
restriction theorem, `psiql2.tex` lines 155--178, proves for each
stage that \(i_r\) is injective on some neighborhood of
\(K^{r+1}\).  Also \(i_r|_{K^{r+1}}\) is a homeomorphism onto
\(K^r\).

For \(h=1\), take the neighborhood supplied by that theorem.  Suppose
compatible neighborhoods have been chosen through stage \(r-1\).
Since \(K^{r+1}\) is compact and \(i_r(K^{r+1})=K^r\), shrink the
injectivity neighborhood of \(K^{r+1}\) so that its image lies in the
previously chosen neighborhood of \(K^r\).  On the shrunken
neighborhood, \(i_r\) is injective and its image lies in the domain on
which the earlier composition is injective.  Their composition is
therefore injective.  A finite induction gives (5.5).  A holomorphic
injective map between surfaces is open, and
\(I_h(K^h)=K^0\), so the image is an open neighborhood of the base
filled set. \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.4 (conditional local route seeds)

Use the identification induced by Lemma 5.3.  Suppose the phrase
“\(\alpha\) passes through \(L_0^h\)” is certified by data

\[
 (t_0,x,a^-,a^+)
\tag{5.7}
\]

such that:

1. \(x=\alpha(t_0)\) belongs to the embedded pulled limb
   \(I_h(L_0^h)\);
2. a sufficiently short two-sided subarc of \(\alpha\) lies in
   \(I_h(O_h)\);
3. it meets the embedded pulled tree only at \(x\); and
4. its two one-sided inverse arcs land at the specified accesses
   \(a^-\ne a^+\) of \(L_0^h\).

Then the two one-sided portions have local \(I_h\)-lifts in
\(O_h\setminus T^h\), and those lifts belong to two different
components of the raw inverse trace (3.3).

#### Proof

Invert the embedding (5.5) on the short target subarc.  This gives the
two one-sided lifts, and their assumed access records give
ideal-boundary limits at \(t_0\).  Apply Lemma 5.1. \(\square\)

Corollary 5.4 is the maximum unconditional geometric leverage
available from the embedded-neighborhood theorem: the conclusion is
conditional only on making the source's informal crossing phrase
precise.  It does not extend either germ to a binder-grade proper
route.

### Lemma 5.5 (the strongest embedded-collar route theorem)

Retain the full, unpunctured maps from Lemma 5.3.  Put

\[
 \widehat T_h=I_h(T^h),\qquad
 C_h=O_h\setminus T^h,\qquad
 \widehat C_h=I_h(O_h)\setminus \widehat T_h .
\tag{5.8}
\]

After shrinking \(O_h\), if necessary, the map

\[
 I_h:C_h\longrightarrow\widehat C_h
\tag{5.9}
\]

is a conformal isomorphism.  On every hyperbolic component it induces
an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of ideal boundaries.  In
particular, it preserves the cyclic order of all access intervals that
are represented inside this collar.

Let \(Q=(a,b)\subset(0,1)\), and suppose an **actual** target subpath

\[
 \sigma=\alpha|_Q:Q\longrightarrow\widehat C_h
\tag{5.10}
\]

has the following properties:

1. \(\sigma\) is proper in \(\widehat C_h\);
2. its two ideal endpoints are specified accesses of
   \(\widehat T_h\);
3. the inclusion \(C_h\hookrightarrow U^h\setminus T^h\) extends
   continuously at their inverse accesses and sends them to the
   specified ideal points of \(U^h\setminus T^h\); and
4. \(\sigma(Q)\) meets no other pulled obstacle.

Then

\[
 \widetilde\sigma=(I_h|_{C_h})^{-1}\circ\sigma
\tag{5.11}
\]

is a proper restriction path in \(U^h\setminus T^h\), has exactly the
two prescribed accesses, and preserves their cyclic order.

#### Proof

The first assertion follows directly from Lemma 5.3:
\(I_h|_{O_h}\) is a holomorphic embedding, maps \(T^h\) onto
\(\widehat T_h\), and hence restricts to the conformal isomorphism
(5.9).

A conformal isomorphism between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces induces a
homeomorphism of their ideal boundaries.  Indeed, choose universal
covers by \(\mathbb D\).  A lift of (5.9) is a disk automorphism, so it
extends to an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism.  It
conjugates the two deck groups, carries limit set to limit set, and
therefore descends to the quotients used in KKL lines 1844--1847.
This proves ideal-boundary and cyclic-order naturality without any
local-connectivity assumption on \(T^h\).

Apply the inverse homeomorphism to the proper extension of \(\sigma\).
Clauses 2--3 identify its endpoint limits with actual ideal points of
the full surface \(U^h\setminus T^h\), rather than artificial ends
created by cutting down to \(O_h\).  Clause 4 prevents an intermediate
end at another component of \(T^h\).  Thus (5.11) extends continuously
to the two stated ideal endpoints and is a proper restriction in the
KKL sense of line 406. \(\square\)

### Qualification 5.6 (what Wave subordination does not add)

Lemma 5.5 is a theorem about an actual subpath satisfying (5.10).
Wave subordination does not prove (5.10).

KKL line 1868 defines subordination by saying that, **up to homotopy
relative to its endpoints**, a proper path has a representative in
every neighborhood of the base.  Line 1874 imports that definition
into the notion of a Wave.  Consequently subordination supplies a
collar-confined representative of the same relative homotopy class.
It does not say that the proof-used leaf \(\alpha\), or either of its
literal line-1603 portions, lies in \(I_h(O_h)\).

This distinction cannot be removed by changing representatives.
KKL line 2083 defines \(\iota^*\gamma\) from actual proper paths
contained in the actual inverse image of \(\gamma\); line 406 says that
paths are identified only up to reparameterization.  The source
contains no theorem saying that restriction components, breaking, or
retention under line 2097 are invariant under a proper homotopy that
may cross the additional pulled tree \(T^h\setminus T\).

For each sign \(\epsilon\in\{-,+\}\), let \(Q^\epsilon\) be the full
parameter interval of the claimed line-1603 portion and let
\(J^\epsilon\subset Q^\epsilon\) be the maximal interval adjacent to
the central encounter on which its local \(I_h\)-lift analytically
continues.  The exact next extension statement is

\[
 J^\epsilon=Q^\epsilon .
\tag{5.12}
\]

Equality (5.12) rules out:

- finite escape from the selected immersion sheet;
- exit through the boundary of the embedded collar;
- collision with another component of \(T^h\); and
- a second attachment access before the claimed far endpoint.

Even after (5.12), one must prove that the lift has a unique continuous
limit at the specified far ideal access.  Only then does Lemma 5.5
give a proper route.  Neither (5.12) nor far-end landing follows from
Wave subordination.

## 6. A holomorphic one-sided-lift model

The missing bilateral incidence is not a formal consequence of
holomorphic immersion, a one-sided Jordan collar, and the existence of
two topological target portions.

Let

\[
 Y=\mathbb D,
 \qquad
 \Omega=\mathbb D\cap\{\operatorname{Im}z>0\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Choose a Riemann map

\[
 \phi:\mathbb D\longrightarrow\Omega.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since \(\Omega\) is a Jordan domain, \(\phi\) extends
homeomorphically to the closed disks.  Define the holomorphic
embedding

\[
 I:\mathbb D\xrightarrow{\phi}\Omega\hookrightarrow\mathbb D.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For completeness, the same source disk also carries the proper
degree-two map

\[
 P(z)=z^2:\mathbb D\longrightarrow\mathbb D.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Now take the embedded proper target path

\[
 \alpha(t)=i(1-2t),
 \qquad 0<t<1.
\tag{6.5}
\]

It has ideal endpoints \(i\) and \(-i\).  Put \(t_0=1/2\).  The two
ordered topological portions of \(\alpha\) are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \alpha^-&=\alpha\bigl((0,1/2)\bigr)
          =\{iy:0<y<1\},\\
 \alpha^+&=\alpha\bigl((1/2,1)\bigr)
          =\{iy:-1<y<0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.6}
\]

The first portion lies in \(\Omega\), the second is disjoint from
\(\Omega\), and \(\alpha(t_0)=0\in\partial\Omega\).  Hence

\[
 I^{-1}(|\alpha|)
 =
 \phi^{-1}(\alpha^-).
\tag{6.7}
\]

The right side is one proper path in the source disk: its two ideal
endpoints are \(\overline\phi^{-1}(i)\) and
\(\overline\phi^{-1}(0)\).  There is no inverse germ at all over
\(\alpha^+\).

Thus (6.3)--(6.7) realize:

- a holomorphic embedding;
- a full Jordan boundary collar;
- one proper inverse path ending at an internal image-boundary
  access;
- two ordered target portions at that access; and
- failure of bilateral lift incidence.

The model is not a KKL Hubbard-tree block and no KKL ideal-marking
action is asserted for it.  Its exact use is narrower: it disproves
the implication

\[
\begin{split}
 &\text{holomorphic immersion + Jordan collar + one proper inverse}\\
 &\text{path + two topological target portions}\\
 &\hspace{28mm}\Longrightarrow
 \text{two raw inverse-trace components.}
\end{split}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Therefore an actual KKL proof must use the additional iterated
embedded-neighborhood geometry and must type the encounter as a
two-sided crossing.  Local univalence or an independently chosen
one-sided collar alone cannot do so.  The model does not obstruct the
conditional crossing argument described after (1.1), because its
second target portion was deliberately chosen outside the immersion
image.

### 6.1 A bilateral-germ model with wrong far endpoints

There is a stronger exact model showing why Lemma 5.5 still needs the
whole-portion hypothesis (5.12).

Let

\[
 Y=\mathbb D,\qquad
 \Omega=\mathbb D\setminus[0,1).
\tag{6.9}
\]

The slit disk \(\Omega\) is simply connected.  Let
\(\phi:\mathbb D\to\Omega\) be a Riemann map and set
\(I=\operatorname{incl}\circ\phi\).  The map \(I\) is a holomorphic
embedding.  It extends as a homeomorphism between prime-end circles,
and every interior point of the slit has two distinct, cyclically
ordered prime-end accesses.

Choose

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p&=i,          &s_1&=0.8,       &u_1&=0.65-0.15i,\\
 r&=0.5,        &u_2&=0.35+0.15i,&s_2&=0.2,\\
 q&=-i.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Let \(\alpha\) be the polygonal path, in the displayed order,

\[
 p\longrightarrow s_1\longrightarrow u_1\longrightarrow r
 \longrightarrow u_2\longrightarrow s_2\longrightarrow q .
\tag{6.11}
\]

All open segments lie in \(\mathbb D\), and their coordinate ranges
show directly that nonconsecutive segments are disjoint.  Thus
\(\alpha\) is a simple proper path from \(p\) to \(q\).  At the
distinguished encounter \(r\), its incoming and outgoing germs lie on
opposite sides of the slit.  They therefore admit local \(I\)-lifts
landing at the two distinct prime ends over \(r\).

Nevertheless the maximal continuation of the incoming germ is only
the component over

\[
 s_1\longrightarrow u_1\longrightarrow r,
\tag{6.12}
\]

and the maximal continuation of the outgoing germ is only the
component over

\[
 r\longrightarrow u_2\longrightarrow s_2.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Both are proper in the source disk, but their far endpoints lie over
\(s_1\) and \(s_2\), not over \(p\) and \(q\).  In the notation of
Qualification 5.6,

\[
 J^-\subsetneq Q^-,
 \qquad
 J^+\subsetneq Q^+ .
\tag{6.14}
\]

Finally, let \(B\) be the closed left semicircle in the ideal boundary
from \(p\) to \(q\).  Since \(\mathbb D\) is simply connected,
\(\alpha\) is properly homotopic rel \(p,q\) to crosscuts in every
neighborhood of \(B\).  Hence it is subordinate to \(B\) in the sense
of KKL line 1868.  Such collar-confined representatives lie in
\(\Omega\), but the actual path (6.11) has the extra slit collisions
\(s_1,s_2\).

The model therefore has, simultaneously:

- a holomorphic embedded collar with prime-end naturality;
- a subordinate proper target path;
- two distinct local access germs at the central encounter; and
- proper maximal lifts of both germs;

yet neither lift realizes its claimed whole target portion.  This
disproves the inference

\[
 \text{subordination + bilateral collar germs}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{two correct proper route restrictions}.
\tag{6.15}
\]

As before, this is not asserted to be a KKL Hubbard block.  It is an
exact countermodel to the proposed general inference from the source's
definitions.  A KKL repair must add (5.12), or an equivalent
no-intermediate-collision theorem, for the actual proof-used leaf.

## 7. Why the native source results stop before (1.1)

### 7.1 The pulled-tree interval formula is not a path-lift theorem

KKL Proposition 6.5, lines 1254--1261, computes
\(\iota^*I\) for a multi-interval \(I\).  It absorbs the
Carathéodory boundaries of components of \(T'\setminus T\) attached
at \(I\).  This proves an interval identity.  It does not say that
both one-sided subarcs of a fixed leaf belong to the immersion image,
nor that either subarc has a maximal proper lift.

### 7.2 End-properness does not supply the missing critical-end lift

Lines 1263--1271 prove combinatorial end-properness, but properness is
proved only on

\[
 BL_{[b,q-b-2]}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The line-1603 route begins or ends at \(BL_0\), and

\[
 F_*BL_0=\partial_{\mathrm C}T.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus the proper-range theorem cannot be applied to extend the missing
one-sided germ through that end.

### 7.3 The itinerary proposition permits non-occurrences

For consecutive intervals in its finite sequence, KKL Proposition
2099 permits:

1. an actual restriction path;
2. adjacency; or
3. containment in one pulled interval.

Only item 1 provides an analytic occurrence.  The locked source does
not exclude items 2 and 3 on every transition of both line-1603
routes.  Hence the proposition cannot establish (1.1), proper landing,
or filter survival.

### 7.4 Unbreakability has already forgotten the needed provenance

Unbreakability says that each untagged output is a single path.  It
guarantees at least one retained occurrence at every stage.  It does
not remember which inverse component produced that path, and it
cannot create a second component.  Using unbreakability to infer the
two occurrences needed to contradict unbreakability would be
circular.

            &\text{alternative of Proposition 2099.}

\tag{8.1}
\]

## 11. Two proposed repair routes

### 11.1 Repair route 1: line 1600 plus separator topology

**Verdict as a binder repair: fails.**

There are two distinct claims here.

First, if “passes through” is interpreted as an actual ordered
two-sided crossing and one also imports the iterated version of the

short one-sided subarcs have local inverse germs.  Lemma 5.1 puts

Second, one might try to apply line 1600 at levels \(n\) and \(n+1\),
then use the separator \(K_0^n\) to declare the resulting proper paths
to be the two complete line-1603 routes.  The source does not justify
that stronger declaration.

Indeed, because \(A_{n+1}\subset A_n\), line 1600 gives existential

\begin{aligned}
 &\exists\,\Theta_n:
   \Theta_n\text{ is a restriction ending at }L_0^n,\\
 &\exists\,\Theta_{n+1}:
   \Theta_{n+1}\text{ is a restriction ending at }L_0^{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.1}

There is no assertion that:

1. \(\Theta_n\) and \(\Theta_{n+1}\) project from two different

2. either path represents the route from \(L_0^{n+1}\) to the
   non-\(BL_0\) end;
3. their one-sided accesses at \(L_0^{n+1}\) are different; or
4. the two choices extend to separate segmented retained towers with

Both statements in (11.1) are compatible with one nested retained
chain.  This is exactly what the proved one-chain theorem records.
Applying line 1600 after reversing the parameterization of
\(\alpha\) does not help: the distinguished physical end remains the
\(BL_0\)-end, so the same existential restriction may be selected.

The separator statement at line 1602 concerns continua inside the
filled tree.  Together with a separately typed two-sided crossing and
the embedded neighborhood, it can distinguish the two *local* sides.
It does not prove that the maximal components land at the required
other ends, admit proper full-stage extensions, or survive the filter.

Therefore line 1600 plus line 1602 does not prove two
**binder-grade** route seeds.  At best, after formalizing the intended
crossing and invoking line 1241, it proves two local raw germs.  Repair
route 1 still stops before proper full-route realization.

### 11.2 Repair route 2: charge bad leaves directly by width

**Verdict: fails with the currently imported estimates.**

Let \(A_{\mathrm{bad}}\subset A_{n+1}\) be the family of leaves that
fail the proposed pass-through at \(K_0^n\).  A direct bypass would
need an estimate

\[
 \mathcal W(A_{\mathrm{bad}})
 \leq
 O\!\left(\mathcal W(\mathcal R F)\right)+O(1)
\tag{11.2}
\]

without first proving that every bad leaf breaks.

None of the source tools cited in the stationary proof gives (11.2):

1. The immersion-domination lemma at lines 2086--2089 starts with
   actual proper restriction leaves.  It cannot be applied to a
   route represented only by adjacency or same-pulled-interval
   containment.
2. The covering pushforward lemma at lines 2075--2078 also starts with
   an already constructed lamination in the pulled surface and a
   degree bound on its proof-used end.
3. Proposition 7.8 at lines 1514--1521 bounds the unbreakable part of
   a Wave **over** \(BL_0\).  The source does not prove that either
   bad-leaf portion forms such a retained Wave.
4. Proposition 7.9 at lines 1524--1532 applies to a localized Wave
   subordinate to
   \(\partial_{\mathrm C}T\setminus BL_0\).  Again, no such retained
   bad-leaf sublamination is constructed.
5. Proposition 7.10 at lines 1535--1623 bounds Waves **ending at**
   \(BL_0\), but this is the proposition whose proof contains line
   1603.  Invoking it would be circular.
6. The final domination step at lines 1609--1618 needs a lamination
   with one end on the selected component \(K\) and the other on its
   complement.  Constructing that lamination is precisely what the
   pass-through argument is meant to establish.

There is also a simple algebraic noncoercivity check.  Give a bad

\(M>0\).  The available one-sided domination and covering estimates

Thus a direct charge is not impossible in principle, but it would be

The locked source supplies none of these.  Repair route 2 therefore
does not bypass the occurrence problem.

## 12. Sequential-action correction: nested windows are mandatory

The common-parameter construction in
`kkl_stationary_two_occurrence_binder_design.md` is a correct
equalizer theorem, but it is not a necessary description of a KKL
sequential \(F_*\)-occurrence when it requires every untruncated
coordinate to be proper.  KKL lines 2083 and 2141 define restriction
by **containment**:

\[
 \gamma_r\in\iota^*\delta_{r-1}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \iota(|\gamma_r|)\subset |\delta_{r-1}|.
\tag{12.1}
\]

They do not assert equality in (12.1).  This changes the binder
invariant.

### 12.1 The segmented retained extension tower

In the proof-used stationary range, each selected
\(\delta_r=F_*^r\alpha\) is a leaf of the Wave \(F_*^rA_r\), and hence
may be taken simple.  A witnessed sequential occurrence through depth
\(h\) consists of

\[
 \mathfrak S=
 \left(
  \delta_0=\alpha;
  \{\gamma_r,\delta_r,a_r,J_r,\phi_r\}_{r=1}^h
 \right),
\tag{12.2}
\]

where, for every \(r\):

1. \(\gamma_r:(0,1)\to X_1\) is an actual proper path retained by the
   line-2097 filter in \(\iota^*\delta_{r-1}\);
2. \(\delta_r=f\circ\gamma_r\) is the selected proper output path;
3. \(J_r\subset(0,1)\) is an open interval and
   \(a_r:(0,1)\to J_r\) is a monotone homeomorphism satisfying
   \[
     \iota\circ\gamma_r
     =
     \delta_{r-1}\circ a_r ;
   \tag{12.3}
   \]
4. \(\phi_r\) is the complete properness, fine-marking, coarse-filter,
   endpoint-access, and route witness for that full stage path.

The orientation sign of \(a_r\) must be recorded.  It is fixed by the
oriented route convention, not discarded by untagged path equality.
If a proof-used output is not simple, (12.3) itself, including the
chosen parameter occurrence, must be stored as data rather than
inferred from set containment.

The term **extension tower** is essential: the full proper
\(\gamma_r\) records how a later common segment extends to its two
stage-\(r\) ideal ends.  Those extensions are not recoverable from a
single deepest fiber-product trace.

### Theorem 12.2 (nested-window synchronization)

Let \(\mathfrak S\) satisfy (12.2)--(12.3).  Define

\[
 A_{h,h}=\operatorname{id}_{(0,1)},\qquad
 A_{r,h}=a_{r+1}\circ a_{r+2}\circ\cdots\circ a_h
 \quad(1\leq r<h),
\tag{12.4}
\]

and

\[
 \Gamma_r=\gamma_r\circ A_{r,h}.
\tag{12.5}
\]

Then

\[
 f\circ\Gamma_r=\iota\circ\Gamma_{r+1}
 \qquad(1\leq r<h).
\tag{12.6}
\]

Consequently the array \((\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_h)\) determines one
path \(\Theta\) in the complete \(h\)-fold Kahn fiber product.  Its
base immersion trace is the nested subpath

\[
 I_h\circ\Theta
 =
 \delta_0\circ
 a_1\circ a_2\circ\cdots\circ a_h .
\tag{12.7}
\]

In general, \(\Gamma_r\) is not proper for \(r<h\), even though every
full extension \(\gamma_r\) is proper.

Conversely, the sequential membership assertions

\[
 \delta_r\in F_*\delta_{r-1}
 \qquad(1\leq r\leq h)
\tag{12.8}
\]

together with chosen actual retained occurrences yield a record of
the form (12.2).  Thus (12.2), not a common array of full proper
coordinates, is the exact finite witness for a KKL sequential chain.

#### Proof

For \(r<h\), equations (12.3)--(12.5) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \iota\circ\Gamma_{r+1}
 &=
 \iota\circ\gamma_{r+1}\circ A_{r+1,h}\\
 &=
 \delta_r\circ a_{r+1}\circ A_{r+1,h}\\
 &=
 f\circ\gamma_r\circ A_{r,h}\\
 &=
 f\circ\Gamma_r .
\end{aligned}
\tag{12.9}
\]

This proves (12.6).  Kahn lines 203--208 and 259--265 identify the
analytic pullback with the topological equalizer, while lines
418--421 identify the finite tower with relation iteration.  Therefore
(12.6) gives the unique interior fiber-product path.  Repeated use of
(12.3) gives (12.7).

If some \(J_{r+1}\) is a proper subinterval, then
\(\Gamma_r\) is a restriction of \(\gamma_r\) to a proper interior
parameter window.  Its endpoint at a finite endpoint of that window
can be an interior point of \(X_1\), so properness does not follow.

Finally, (12.8) means by the source definition
\(F_*=f_*\iota^*\) that there is a full proper retained
\(\gamma_r\in\iota^*\delta_{r-1}\) with
\(f_*\gamma_r=\delta_r\).  For simple \(\delta_{r-1}\), its literal
occurrence in that path supplies the monotone window \(a_r\); in the
nonsimple case the occurrence parameterization is retained explicitly.
This reconstructs (12.2). \(\square\)

### Corollary 12.3 (the complete prefix flag)

For every \(1\leq m\leq h\), apply Theorem 12.2 to the prefix of
\(\mathfrak S\) through stage \(m\), and denote the resulting path by
\(\Theta_m\).  In the relation-coordinate order

\[
 X_m=
 \{(z_1,\ldots,z_m):f(z_r)=\iota(z_{r+1})\},
\tag{12.10}
\]

the Kahn immersion projection
\(i_{m-1}:X_m\to X_{m-1}\) deletes the last coordinate.  The prefix
paths therefore satisfy

\[
 i_{m-1}\circ\Theta_m
 =
 \Theta_{m-1}\circ a_m
 \qquad(2\leq m\leq h).
\tag{12.11}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 I_m\circ\Theta_m
 =
 \delta_0\circ a_1\circ\cdots\circ a_m,
 \qquad
 P_m\circ\Theta_m=\delta_m .
\tag{12.12}
\]

The full segmented tower is equivalent to this complete prefix flag
together with the window maps, the initial path, and the
identifications of each terminal coordinate with the full
\(\gamma_m\).  A terminal path \(\Theta_h\) alone is not equivalent:
for \(r<h\), it remembers only
\(\gamma_r|_{A_{r,h}((0,1))}\), not the discarded proper shoulders.

#### Proof

The coordinate formula in Theorem 12.2 applied at prefix \(m\) is

\[
 q_{m,r}\Theta_m
 =
 \gamma_r\circ a_{r+1}\circ\cdots\circ a_m .
\tag{12.13}
\]

Deleting the last coordinate gives, for \(r<m\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_{m-1,r}i_{m-1}\Theta_m
 &=
 \gamma_r\circ a_{r+1}\circ\cdots\circ a_m\\
 &=
 q_{m-1,r}\Theta_{m-1}\circ a_m ,
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves (12.11) because points of \(X_{m-1}\) are determined by
their ordered coordinates.  Equation (12.12) is the prefix form of
(12.7), and its terminal identity follows because the last coordinate
of \(\Theta_m\) is the full \(\gamma_m\).

Conversely, the prefix relation and the full last-coordinate
identifications recover every occurrence equation
\(\delta_{m-1}\circ a_m=\iota\circ\gamma_m\) by applying the defining
relation to the last two coordinates.  Thus the flag recovers the
segmented tower.  The terminal trace contains only the restricted
coordinates in (12.13), so it cannot recover extensions outside those
windows. \(\square\)

### 12.2 Exact annular countermodel to full-coordinate properness

Fix \(0<r<1\) and put

\[
 X_0=\{r^2<|z|<1\},\qquad
 X_1=\{r<|z|<1\}.
\tag{12.14}
\]

Let

\[
 f(z)=z^2:X_1\to X_0,
 \qquad
 \iota(z)=z:X_1\hookrightarrow X_0.
\tag{12.15}
\]

Here \(f\) is a proper unbranched degree-two cover and \(\iota\) is a
holomorphic immersion.  Parameterize the positive radial paths by

\[
 \delta(t)=r^{2(1-t)}\in X_0,\qquad
 \gamma(t)=r^{1-t}\in X_1 .
\tag{12.16}
\]

Both paths are proper.  With

\[
 a(t)=\frac{1+t}{2},
\tag{12.17}
\]

one has exactly

\[
 \iota\circ\gamma=\delta\circ a,
 \qquad
 f\circ\gamma=\delta .
\tag{12.18}
\]

Thus \(\delta_0=\cdots=\delta_h=\delta\),
\(\gamma_1=\cdots=\gamma_h=\gamma\), and
\(a_1=\cdots=a_h=a\) form a valid sequential path chain through every
finite horizon.  However the synchronized coordinate one level before
the terminal stage is

\[
 \Gamma_{h-1}(t)
 =
 \gamma(a(t))
 =
 r^{(1-t)/2}.
\tag{12.19}
\]

As \(t\to0\), (12.15) tends to \(\sqrt r\), an interior point of
\(X_1\).  Hence \(\Gamma_{h-1}\) is not proper.  Moreover

\[
 f(|\gamma|)=|\delta|
 \quad\text{but}\quad
 \iota(|\gamma|)\subsetneq|\delta|,
\tag{12.20}
\]

so the full proper stage paths cannot satisfy an exact common-parameter
equality.

This is an analytic counterexample, with the correct proper
degree-two covering/immersion types, to the claim that a sequential
KKL chain must be representable by one array of full proper
fiber-product coordinates.  It is not a KKL Hubbard-tree
counterexample and makes no assertion about its marking filter.

### 12.3 Binder consequence

The categorical equalizer theorem remains useful as the checksum
(12.6) for the **truncated** common trace.  It cannot replace the
extension tower (12.2).  The forgetful map

\[
 \mathfrak S\longmapsto\Theta
\tag{12.21}
\]

discards the parts of each proper \(\gamma_r\) outside the cumulative
nested window.  Those discarded parts contain exactly the remote
ideal landing, fine-marking, and line-2097 retention information used
by sequential \(F_*\).

Accordingly, the necessary replacement for a complete-component
two-occurrence binder is:

> two segmented retained extension towers, one for each line-1603
> route, with all window maps, endpoint accesses, full-stage filter
> witnesses, and terminal Wave memberships, together with their
> nested fiber-product traces as coherence checksums.

Distinct complete components may still be recorded, but they are
neither sufficient nor the minimal sequential invariant.  The locked
source does not construct either segmented route tower: it stops first
at the whole-portion continuation equation (5.12).

The finite bookkeeping is regression-tested by
`audit_stationary_segmented_binder_schema.py`.  Its tests deliberately
accept strict nested windows, reject missing proper extensions and
filter failures, and verify that a valid two-route schema still does
not imply terminal noncollision.  These are diagnostic schema checks;
they do not establish any analytic occurrence.

## 14. Conclusion

The first analytic problem is geometric.  The locked proof must first
type “passes through” as an ordered two-sided crossing in the iterated
embedded neighborhood and identify the two pulled-limb accesses.  Under
that explicit reading, the two local raw germs and their component
separation follow.

What does not follow is the binder-grade extension: both maximal
continuations must traverse the entire claimed portions, land at the
required other ends, remain proper in the proof-used stage paths,
belong to the fine markings, survive every coarse filter, and enter the
terminal Wave families.  Those are the first unresolved full-route
statements.

There is also an occurrence-calculus correction, proved in Section 12.
Exact Cartesian synchronization is valid after passing to the nested
common windows of a sequential \(F_*\)-chain.  It is generally false
for the full proper stage paths themselves.  Therefore the necessary
KKL binder is a segmented retained extension tower, not an array of
full proper paths on one common parameter.  This correction does not
close the geometric gap; it makes the later binder interface accurate.

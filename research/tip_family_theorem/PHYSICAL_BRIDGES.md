## 2. A direct exclusion of a competing period-two restriction

**Lemma.** Let $f(z)=z^2+c$ be real, let $p\ge1$, let $K(f)$ be a dendrite, and assume
$$
 x_{2p}<0<x_{4p}<x_p,\qquad x_{5p}<0,\qquad -x_p<x_{2p}.
$$
Assume $G=f^p$ is decreasing on $[0,x_p]$. There are no topological disks $D\Subset E$ on which $G^2:D\to E$ is proper holomorphic of degree two with a **connected** filled set $L$ containing 0. The two strict order chains and monotonicity in v2 give precisely these inequalities for every selected $p\ge3$. [Finite source and inverse cuts](../independent_audits/real_primitive_tip_family_unconditional_20261116/verify.py) · [Tail proof, eq. (19) and its following paragraph](../independent_audits/real_primitive_tip_family_all_p_20261116/NOTE.md)

**Proof.** Evenness and monotonicity give $G([-x_p,x_p])=[x_{2p},x_p]=T\subset[-x_p,x_p]$. Thus $T$ is forward invariant under $G$, and every $f$-iterate of a point of $T$ belongs to the compact union $\bigcup_{j=0}^{p-1}f^j(T)$. In particular $[x_{2p},x_{4p}]\subset K(f)$.

Suppose the restriction exists. By definition its filled set $L$ is compact, forward invariant under $G^2$, and contained in $D$. Every $f$-iterate of a point of $L$ belongs to $\bigcup_{j=0}^{2p-1} f^j(L)$, so $L\subset K(f)$. Since $0\in L$, both $x_{2p}=G^2(0)$ and $x_{4p}=G^4(0)$ lie in $L$. In a dendrite each interior point of the unique arc between two endpoints separates them. Their connected subset $L$ must therefore contain that arc, here the real segment $[x_{2p},x_{4p}]$.

Now $G(0)=x_p>0$ and $G(x_{4p})=x_{5p}<0$. The intermediate value theorem gives $y\in(0,x_{4p})\subset L$ with $G(y)=0$. The chain rule and $G'(0)=0$ give $(G^2)'(0)=(G^2)'(y)=0$. Both distinct critical points lie in $L\subset D$. Riemann–Hurwitz for a proper holomorphic map of disks of degree two gives total critical multiplicity one. This contradiction proves the lemma. The argument answers the period-two objection M1 directly without transporting a putative restriction through a straightening. It supplies one part of v2 H1. ## 3. From intervals to KKL paths

### The filled interval and its phase neighborhoods

Here is the H1 filled-set implication *from the two proper restrictions and the actual orbit estimates of §1*. Let $\rho=3/(10|A_p|)$, let $W$ be the central component of $(G^3)^{-1}(D(0,\rho))$, and set $F=G^3:W\to D(0,\rho)$. The finite check $|A_p||x_{3p}|<3/10$ and its large-period counterpart $|h^3(0)|<3/10$ give $I=[-t,t]\subset D(0,\rho)$. The finite first-return check $x_p<R_p$ and the large-period estimate $x_p<12\cdot4^{-p}<2^{-p}$ place the outer real interval in $U_p$. [Three-return estimates and critical-value checks, §1](../independent_audits/real_primitive_tip_family_prime_end_identification_20261116/NOTE.md) · [Finite first-return check](../independent_audits/real_primitive_tip_family_unconditional_20261116/verify.py) · [Large-period estimates, Proof III–IV](../independent_audits/real_primitive_tip_family_all_p_20261116/NOTE.md)

1. By evenness, the signs and monotonicity from §1, successive images under $G$ are exactly $I$, $K_1=[x_{4p},x_p]$, $K_2=[x_{2p},x_{5p}]$, then $I$ again. In the first step $G(t)=G(x_{3p})=x_{4p}$. In the second the branch on $K_1$ is decreasing, and in the third the branch on $K_2$ is increasing. These compact intervals are pairwise disjoint by the strict orders. The interval $I$ contains 0 and lies in $(G^3)^{-1}(D(0,\rho))$, hence in its component $W$. Its forward invariance under $F$ gives $I\subset K(F)$.
2. The degree-two disk map $F$ has just one critical point, 0, which stays in $I$. Douady–Hubbard Proposition 2 gives connectedness of $K(F)$, and Theorem 1 supplies a hybrid conjugacy to $Q_d(z)=z^2+d$, sending the unique critical point to 0. It sends $-t=F(0)$ to $d$ and $t=F^2(0)=F^3(0)$ to $d^2+d=Q_d^3(0)$. Thus $d^3(d+2)=0$, with $d\ne0$ because $t>0$. At $d=-2$, the two latter images are precisely $-2$ and $2$, the endpoints of $K(Q_{-2})=[-2,2]$. Consequently $K(F)$ is an arc with endpoints $-t,t$. In an arc, a connected subset containing both endpoints is the whole arc. Since $I\subset K(F)$, this proves $K(F)=I$. [Douady–Hubbard, Proposition 2 and Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.1491)
3. All three phases lie in $[-x_p,x_p]$. This connected interval contains 0 and maps under $G$ to $T\subset U_p$, so it lies in the central component $V_p$. Choose disjoint open neighborhoods of the three compact phases with closures in $V_p$. Their inverse images under $G^j$ for the corresponding $j=0,1,2$ intersect in an open neighborhood of $I$. Shrink the polynomial-like restriction there as follows. Start with $D_0=D(0,\rho)$, $D_1=W$, and take $D_{m+1}=F^{-1}(D_m)$ inside $W$. Each $D_m$ is a disk containing $I$. Indeed, the preimage of a simply connected disk containing the critical value exhausts degree two on one component and is simply connected (equivalently cut the target disk from its single branch value to its boundary and glue the two sheets). Properness gives $D_{m+1}\Subset D_m$ inductively. The intersection of the nested compact closures is $K(F)=I$ by the definition of the filled set. Compactness places some $\overline{D_m}$ in the chosen open neighborhood. Then $F:D_{m+1}\to D_m$ has the same filled set and its first two intermediate $G$-images stay in the selected, disjoint neighborhoods in $V_p$.

This yields the indicated primitive period-three renormalization from the stated estimates and analytic imports. Section 2 excludes a competing period-two connected critical return directly. ## 5. The separate family near −7/4

For $p=3n$, $n\ge6$, the library theorem `LoopsParabolic.near_parabolic_primitive_tips` supplies a common $c,e$, the critical portrait, phase disjointness, and in **the complex plane** both $G(I)=I$ and $\operatorname{Comp}_0G^{-1}(I)=I$, with $I=[-e,e]$. The proof constructs a shooting witness for this same parameter, to which `interior_complex_fiber` applies. [Lean family theorem](../../formalization/weighted/ParabolicTipFamily.lean) · [Lean component and fiber statements](../../formalization/weighted/ParabolicComplexComponent.lean)

1. **Existence of a polynomial-like map.** A closed segment is a nonseparating continuum. Invariance identifies $G^{-1}(G(I))$ with the verified pullback. The unique critical point of $f$ is strictly preperiodic by the exact preperiod and period. The Orsay strictly-preperiodic result implies that $K(f)$ has empty interior. Since $K(G)=K(f)$, an attracting or parabolic periodic point would give a nonempty open basin in this filled set, a contradiction. This verifies the basin clause of Blokh–Oversteegen–Ptacek–Timorin Theorem B for $(G,I)$. Its conclusion supplies a polynomial-like restriction with filled set exactly $I$. [Theorem B, §4](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uab.edu/dist/0/610/files/2025/09/QL-revised-short-v-11-30-15.pdf) · [Orsay Notes, Ch. IV §4](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hubbard/OrsayEnglish.pdf)
2. **Degree and primitivity.** Choose an interior real target in $I$ outside the finitely many critical values of the polynomial $G$. Every preimage in the restriction stays in the filled set because its forward orbit after one step stays there. Conversely $I$ lies inside the restriction domain. Thus its entire regular fiber agrees with the fiber in $I$, of size two by the two real branches and the analytic isolation of the segment. Counting the regular fiber gives degree two. The $p$ phase images of $I$ are pairwise disjoint by the same Lean theorem, giving primitivity. [Complex fiber theorem](../../formalization/weighted/ParabolicComplexComponent.lean) · [Analytic corollary](../native_wave_transfer/near_parabolic_tip_family.md)
3. **Identify the straightening.** Apply Douady–Hubbard Theorem 1 to the connected quadratic-like restriction. The hybrid conjugacy preserves the critical orbit $0\mapsto-e\mapsto e\mapsto e$. In monic centered coordinates the equation for the straightened parameter is $d^3(d+2)=0$, and the critical value differs from the critical point. Hence $d=-2$. The algebra is `straightening_parameter` in Lean. The polynomial-like existence and hybrid conjugacy used here are applications of the cited analytic theorems. A marked KKL atlas or a bound for all physical Waves on these parameters requires additional input. [Douady–Hubbard, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.1491) · [Family note and scope](../native_wave_transfer/near_parabolic_tip_family.md)
